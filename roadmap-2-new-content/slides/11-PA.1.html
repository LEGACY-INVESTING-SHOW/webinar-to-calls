Slide 1 · Write the buy box before you see listings, so you can walk away without debate

This is the first lesson of the acquisition phase. By the end of it, you'll have a written, one-page buy box.

That page ranks your goals. It states your constraints. It names your hard requirements. And it describes the property you want in terms someone else could search with. It ends with a pass rule, a sentence that tells you when to walk away from a listing without arguing about it. When I say pass in this lesson, I mean decline, as in "we'll pass on that one."

Why does this come before any market research? Because Sam's PA.2 and PA.3 lessons use this page. PA.2 picks a market with it. PA.3 builds your property pipeline with it.

We'll build Maya and Chris's buy box, then test it on Cedar Ridge at two prices. Every figure is an assumption. The Cedar Ridge ones are the ones the whole course uses, and new ones are marked. First, a few terms.

Slide 2 · The math in this lesson rests on eight terms, and each one is a plain idea

Before we start, eight terms. You'll hear all of them in the worked example, so let's get them straight now.

STR is short-term rental. The base case is a normal year for a house, and the weak case is a bad year. For Cedar Ridge, the weak case is $265 a night at 50% occupancy, instead of $300 at 58%.

ADR is the average nightly rate. NOI, net operating income, is booking revenue minus operating costs, before the loan payment. Cedar Ridge's costs already include a 5% capital reserve.

Debt service, or P&I, is the yearly principal and interest on the loan. PITIA is the full monthly payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA dues.

A point is an upfront lender fee equal to 1% of the loan. And a DSCR loan is one the lender qualifies mainly on the property's income. PA.6 covers it in detail.

Two more you'll hear: cash to close is the down payment, closing costs, and points. Total cash in adds furnishing.

PA.1 · Defining Your Buy Box§ PA.1.13 / 21
Slide 3 · A buy box protects you from budget drift and from underwriting the wrong houses

A buy box is a search specification. It says what you'll buy, where, at what price, and under what conditions. Anything outside the box, you don't look at twice.

It protects you from two things.

The first is drift. You start looking for a three-bedroom house at $380,000. A month later you're touring a $450,000 house with a pool. The photos were great and the agent said it "books great". Nobody decided to raise the budget by $70,000. It just happened, one listing at a time.

The second is wasted work. Every property you seriously consider costs hours. Reading rules, pulling comparable listings (similar rentals nearby), underwriting, calling lenders. Sam's PA.3 builds a pipeline of 5 to 10 qualified properties. Without a buy box, "qualified" means nothing.

A good buy box is short and specific. You write it before you see listings. And it's hard to change on purpose. If you want to change it, you rewrite it, with numbers. You don't change it in a driveway during a showing.

Slide 4 · Your goals pull against each other, so rank them and let the top two or three decide

Before you describe a property, rank your goals. People want several things from a short-term rental purchase, and they conflict. A ranking tells you which one wins when they do.

The common seven are cash flow, long-term ownership, personal use, tax planning, portfolio growth, how involved you want to be, and safety. Safety means protecting your household's savings and income if the house underperforms.

Here's how they fight. Personal use pulls against cash flow. Your own peak-season week is a week you didn't rent.

Tax planning can pull against everything else. A deal bought "for the depreciation" can lose money every month. And the deduction may not even be usable, depending on your household. Lesson 0.2 covered why.

Low involvement pulls against cash flow. In 0.2, a full-service co-host taking 18% of booking revenue turned Cedar Ridge from $5,572 a year to minus $7,798.

Portfolio growth pulls against safety. If you stretch now to buy again sooner, this house gets a thinner reserve.

Rank your goals 1 to 7. Only the top two or three shape the search. If two owners rank them differently, settle it now.

PA.1 · Defining Your Buy Box§ PA.1.35 / 21
Slide 5 · Write down the limits you can't wish away; most of them come from your 0.3 scorecard

Constraints are limits you can't wish away. Most of them come straight from lesson 0.3.

Money comes as two caps. The first is the maximum cash to close: down payment, closing costs, and points, all due on closing day. The second is the maximum total cash in, which adds furnishing.

To find the total cap, start with your liquid savings. Take out your emergency savings and your property reserve. Then take out the other startup costs from your 0.3 cash stack. What's left is the most you can put in.

Why two caps? Furnishing comes after closing. And the cash-to-close cap stops a cheaper furnishing plan from turning into a bigger house. If your 0.3 scorecard carried a funding condition, write it here too.

Financing path. Write which loan types you'll pursue and your down payment. The lender decides eligibility later, in PA.6 and PA.7.

Distance, in driving hours. Time, in hours per week. And minimum reserves, which are floors you don't spend below.

Describe the property

Describe the property in lines concrete enough for Sam's PA.2 and PA.3 filters

  • Market type and its demand driver: drive-to leisure, urban, event-driven, business travel.
  • Property type: detached, townhouse, condo, small multi-unit. It changes legality, lending, and insurance.
  • Location inside the market. Zoning, HOA boundaries, and permit caps can change street by street.
  • Price range. A floor screens out houses too small or worn. The ceiling comes from your money and downside rules.
  • Capacity and amenities. More beds can raise the nightly rate (ADR) and the wear. Water or height brings insurance questions.
  • Operating complexity: septic, wells, long private roads, snow, older systems. Each adds cost or response time.
PA.1 · Defining Your Buy Box§ PA.1.46 / 21
Slide 6 · Describe the property in lines concrete enough for Sam's PA.2 and PA.3 filters

Now the property itself. Keep each line concrete enough that Sam's filters can apply it.

Market type: name it and its demand driver. Sam's PA.2 picks the actual market.

Property type matters for three reasons. Legality, because of condo and HOA rules. Lending, because some lenders restrict condos or certain property types. And insurance.

Location inside the market. Rules can change street by street. Zoning districts, HOA boundaries, and permit caps can all differ within one town. Write where you'll look, and where you won't.

Price range. You need a floor and a ceiling. The floor screens out houses too small or too worn to fit the rest of the box. The ceiling comes from your money constraints and your downside rule. It doesn't come from what sellers are asking. I'll show you how to calculate it shortly.

Guest capacity and amenities. More capacity can mean a higher nightly rate. It also means more wear, more cleaning, and more noise risk. Separate what guests expect from what adds complexity. Any amenity with water or height brings insurance and safety questions.

Operating complexity. None of these kills a deal alone. Decide how much you'll accept.

PA.1 · Defining Your Buy Box§ PA.1.57 / 21
Slide 7 · A property must clear every hard requirement before it gets any more of your time

Hard requirements are the lines a property must clear, every one, before you spend real time on it. They aren't preferences. Fail one, and you pass on it. That means you walk away.

Five belong in almost every short-term rental buy box.

One: legal STR use at the exact address. Unhosted means you don't live there during guest stays, and that use has to be allowed there. Any HOA or deed restriction has to allow it too. Use the SR.1 checklist and record the city or county source. Airbnb itself tells hosts to check local laws and HOA rules, but Airbnb isn't the source.

Two: acceptable insurance. That means a written quote at a premium your numbers can carry.

Three: lender fit, in writing.

Four: your two cash caps. No exceptions because the house is great.

Five: minimum downside performance. Set a floor on weak-case cash flow after debt service, and a minimum ratio of base-case NOI to debt service.

Add a sixth if 0.3 surfaced one. For Maya and Chris, the deal must work with self-management.

PA.1 · Defining Your Buy Box§ PA.1.68 / 21
Slide 8 · The pass rule has four parts, and it allows the box to change only in writing

The pass rule is one or two sentences you agree on in advance. It tells you what to do when a listing doesn't fit.

A good one has four parts. The trigger: any failed hard requirement, or a price over your ceiling that the seller won't bring down. The action: pass. A listing above the ceiling can still get an offer at or under the ceiling. If the seller won't accept it, pass. The record: log which requirement failed. And the change process: the buy box changes only on purpose, in writing, with the new numbers, signed by every owner. Never during a showing.

Why log every pass? Because after 20 passes you'll see a pattern. Maybe every house in your price range fails the insurance quote. That's information. It goes back to Sam's PA.2 market choice. It isn't a reason to lower your standards.

The log also stops you from re-reading the same bad listing three times because it keeps showing up in your feed. You already decided. The log says why.

Slide 11 · Their two cash caps set a price ceiling of $393,000 on the DSCR terms

Now the money constraints become a price.

On the DSCR terms, cash to close is 25% down plus 3% closing costs. That's 28%. Then one point. A point is 1% of the loan, and the loan is 75% of the price. So the point is 0.75% of the price. Cash to close is 28.75% of the price.

Now divide. The cash-to-close cap: $115,000 divided by 28.75% is $400,000. The total-cash-in cap: $145,000 minus $32,000 of furnishing is $113,000. Divide by 28.75% and you get $393,043.

The tighter cap wins. Their box ceiling is $393,000 contract price on these terms.

The downside rule gives another ceiling, but only for a specific house, because it depends on that house's revenue. For Cedar Ridge, the weak case reaches minus $6,000 at about $392,000. The lowest ceiling wins, so for this house the limit is about $392,000. At $392,500 it would fail hard requirement 5.

Section C turns your caps into a price.

First line: cash to close as a percentage of price. That's your down payment percentage, plus closing costs, plus points times the loan percentage. For Maya and Chris it was 28.75%.

Second line: your cash-to-close cap divided by that percentage.

Third line: your total-cash-in cap, minus furnishing, divided by the same percentage.

Fourth line: the ceiling from your downside rule. You can't fill that in yet. It comes from Sam's PA.4 model, house by house. Leave it blank for now.

Your price ceiling is the lowest of the lines you've filled in. For Maya and Chris, that was $393,000, and about $392,000 for Cedar Ridge itself.

Slide 18 · The city, your insurer, your lender, and your CPA each confirm one part of the box

Here's who confirms what for the buy box.

The city or county licensing office confirms that unhosted short-term rental use is allowed at each address that passes your other filters. Use the SR.1 checklist. If the answer is unclear, go to an STR attorney. Don't take a listing agent's word for it.

Your insurance agent confirms the premium and the exclusions for STR use on each finalist. Do that before the diligence window, the period after your offer is accepted when you inspect and verify. If insurance fails, you want to know before you've paid for inspections.

Your lender confirms that the property type, location, and planned use fit their loan. They also confirm the terms you used in section C. If the real terms are different, your price ceiling changes. Recompute it.

And your CPA. Talk to them before you put tax planning in your top three goals. That way the ranking reflects your household's actual situation, not a hope about depreciation.

Every "confirm with" line is a phone call or an email. Keep the replies with your buy box.
