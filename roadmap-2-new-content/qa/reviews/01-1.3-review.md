# Review: 1.3 Is Arbitrage Still Viable in 2026? A Market Reality Check

Reviewer stance: first-time learner with no hosting or investing background, who opened only this lesson and its 20-slide deck. Files reviewed: `parts/01-1.3.md`, `slides/src/01-1.3.json`, `slides/01-1.3.html` (screenshots of slides 1 to 20). I re-ran the Alder Street arithmetic and it holds. The problems are about clarity, ordering, and a few internal inconsistencies, not wrong totals.

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The title asks a yes/no question, and nothing in the lesson says what "arbitrage" means. I had to guess that it means renting an apartment from a landlord and re-renting it to Airbnb guests. The method (contribution, break-even, stress case, payback, three gates) is clear once it starts, and the Alder Street numbers are easy to follow on slides 11 to 15. I lost the thread three times. First, slide 6 uses $180 and $440.25 eight slides before they're explained. Second, the lesson calls Alder Street's Gate 3 "mixed", but by the exercise's own pass/fail rules it passes all three Gate 3 checks. Third, internal labels ("canonical A1", "A3", `tools/examples.py`, "retained lesson", a recording note) kept telling me I was reading a production file, not a lesson.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Arbitrage is never defined.** The whole lesson assumes I know the business model: lease an apartment, re-rent it short term, keep the spread. One sentence in 1.3.1 would fix it.
- **Slide 6 box: "12 of 30 nights at $180 (40%), so 4 stays. 4 x $440.25 = $1,761..."** $180 and $440.25 come from the stress case on slide 14. On slide 6 they appear from nowhere. The Markdown puts this at Step 8, after the stress case. The deck moved it forward and broke the dependency.
- **Slide 4 box / 1.3.3: "On Alder Street it turns the year into a $107 loss."** I haven't met Alder Street yet (slide 11 introduces it) or its base-case profit. A loss relative to what?
- **Gate 3 means different things in different places.** Slide 10 and 1.3.9 say "Payback fits inside the lease". Part C 3c says "Base payback is under half the lease term". Slide 10 says "Break-even sits well below expected occupancy", and 3b says "at least 10 points". The Alder Street verdict (Step 10) uses a third test, stress plus rent, that isn't on the sheet. I can't tell which version to apply.

The exercise lists stress and stress-with-rent as two rows. Gate 3 says "The stress case must survive." Which one?
- **"Nightly rate drops 12% to $180 and occupancy falls to 60%"** (Step 5). One change is a percent and the other is a level, and the exercise turns the second into "occupancy -10 points". A beginner won't know that "percent" and "percentage points" are different things.
- **Part B formula "(fixed / contribution x stay length) / 365"** (1.3.11). The operator order is ambiguous. The section 1.3.4 version (three separate lines) is clearer.
- **Part B "Base-case net | stays per year x contribution - fixed".** "Stays per year" is not an input in Part A and has no formula row. I have to reconstruct occupancy × 365 / stay length myself.
- **Part B "Weakest month net | slow-month stays x slow-month contribution - fixed / 12".** "Slow-month contribution" isn't defined. Is it the stress contribution? And "fixed / 12" reads like it might divide the whole expression.
- **Part A row "Cleaning fee charged / cleaner pay"** holds two different numbers in one cell.
- **Slide 13 line "255 nights = 85 stays x $503.63 ... $42,808".** It reads as if 255 nights equals a dollar figure. It is two steps squashed together.
- **Slide 14 caption.** The only derivation of the stress-case contribution, plus all four break-even percentages, sits in small grey caption text. The most important math on the slide is the least readable.
- **"Alder Street is an investigate"** (slide 16 title). "Investigate" is used as a noun without being introduced as a decision label. The labels (sign / investigate / walk) only appear on slide 1's notes and on slide 17, which comes after.
- **Slide 18 title: "Every input needs written confirmation from the person who controls it."** A platform fee page is not a person. Nightly rate and occupancy (the biggest inputs) aren't confirmed by anyone on the list. The title overclaims what the table shows.
- **Slide 12 notes: "the rent ... and the insurance ... They're the lines that decide whether the deal works."** Insurance is $1,300 of $30,590 (about 4%). It doesn't decide the deal. This contradicts the lesson's own message that rent does.
