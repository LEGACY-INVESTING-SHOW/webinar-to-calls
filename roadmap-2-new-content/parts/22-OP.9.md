### OP.9.0 Learning outcome

### OP.9.1 What Airbnb actually publishes about search

The title of this lesson says "AI ranking era," so let's be exact about what anyone knows. Airbnb publishes an explanation of [how search results work](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/39). That page is the source for everything this lesson says about ranking. I'm not promising a rank boost from any AI trick, keyword pattern, or structured-data method that Airbnb doesn't document. If someone sells you one, ask them for the Airbnb page that supports it.

Here's what the page says, as confirmed by search summaries on 2026-09-25:

- Quality, popularity, price, and location heavily influence how a listing appears in search.
- Quality looks at listing content like photos and videos, guest ratings and reviews, communication between hosts and guests, and listing characteristics.
- Popularity looks at how guests engage with a listing: how often they save it to a wishlist, book it, and message the host.
- Price considers total price and how it compares with other listings in the area. Listings priced below comparable listings with similar characteristics tend to rank higher.
- Location matters a lot. Listings in places guests like to stay tend to rank higher.
- The system encourages variety, so guests see different hosts, characteristics, and prices.
- Availability matters. The more dates a listing is open, the more likely it shows up. More flexibility on length of stay also helps a listing fit more trips.

Notice what's missing. The page doesn't give weights, and it doesn't say which features count more this year than last. It doesn't mention a secret field that AI reads. Sam's OP.2 lesson covers listing optimization and structured data in detail. This lesson covers the operating side: the parts guests experience and then write about.

Several of those factors are set before a guest arrives. Location was fixed when Maya and Chris bought the house. Price is Sam's OP.5 territory. What you control day to day is quality (accurate content, clean stays, fast and clear communication, good reviews) and availability (open dates and flexible stay lengths that fit your permit and your team).

### OP.9.2 Airbnb's ground rules set the floor

Airbnb's [ground rules for home hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) focus on four things:

1. Accuracy. The listing should describe the home and the amenities available from check-in to checkout. Change an accepted booking's details only with the guest's consent.
2. Reservation commitment. Honor accepted reservations and provide a reliable check-in. Don't cancel confirmed reservations except for valid reasons outside your control.
3. Communication. The host or co-host should be available to respond to inquiries and unexpected issues before and during stays.
4. Cleanliness. Clean between every stay to a high standard.

The same page says Airbnb enforces these rules. Actions can include education, warnings, and requiring hosts to remove content that misrepresents the listing. These four rules line up almost one-for-one with what guests review. That's the practical point: the ground rules are the minimum, and reviews measure how far above it you land.

### OP.9.4 Guest checkpoints: before, during, after

A guest journey is the sequence of moments where a guest decides whether this stay is going well. Plan a checkpoint at each one: a moment where the team checks something rather than waiting for a complaint.

| Stage | Checkpoint | What you check | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booking | Guest count and purpose | Party size matches the listing; no sign of an event | Message owner |
| 7 days out | Trip prep sent | Directions and seasonal notes current | Message owner |
| Day before | Check-in details | Correct code; house ready per photo review | Message owner |
| Arrival, same evening | "Anything missing?" | Guest got in; first impression; quick fixes | Message owner |
| Mid-stay (4+ nights) | Optional check | Supplies, questions | Message owner |
| Checkout day | Checkout steps sent | Instructions match the cleaner's needs | Message owner |
| After checkout, within 24 hours | Cleaner's walk-through notes | Damage, left items, anything guests struggled with | Cleaner, then owner |
| After checkout, day 2-3 | Thank-you and review request | Neutral wording (OP.9.5) | Message owner |
| Weekly | Friction log review | Recurring questions and complaints | Owner |

The friction log is the most useful tool on the list. Every time a guest asks a question, mark it. "Where's the water shutoff?" is harmless once. By the third time, the house manual needs a line. "How do the bunk lights work?" asked four times means the lights need a label. Most negative reviews start as a repeated question nobody fixed.

The arrival-evening message matters most. Problems caught that night get fixed during the stay. Problems found after checkout go straight into a review.

### OP.9.5 Asking for reviews within the rules

After checkout, Airbnb prompts both guest and host to leave a review. According to Airbnb's review pages, both have 14 days after checkout to submit one ([reviews for homes](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/13)).

Airbnb's policy on [authentic and trustworthy reviews](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2673) sets the limits on how you ask:

- Hosts and guests may not coordinate with, manipulate, extort, incentivize, or pressure another person to influence a review.
- Reviews may not be given or withheld in exchange for something of value. That includes a discount, a refund, a reciprocal positive review, or a promise not to take negative action.
- Neither side may threaten a negative review to get unwarranted compensation, a refund, or another incentive.

So the review request has to be neutral. It invites a review and asks for honest feedback. It doesn't offer anything, hint at a trade, or tell the guest what rating to give. I'd go a step further than the policy text: don't ask for "five stars" at all. Naming the rating reads as pressure, and the policy prohibits pressure.

A compliant request, sent two or three days after checkout:

> Hi [first name], thanks for staying at Cedar Ridge. If you have a few minutes, we'd appreciate an honest review on Airbnb. If anything wasn't right, please tell us directly too, so we can fix it for the next guests.

Things never to write or say:

- "Leave us 5 stars and we'll give you a discount next time." (Incentive.)
- "We'll return your deposit once you've left a review." (Trade.)
- "We gave you a great review; we hope you'll do the same." (Reciprocal pressure.)
- Any goodwill gesture tied to review timing. In OP.8, Maya's $100 internet credit went out during the stay, with no mention of reviews. Keep it that way.

The host's own review of the guest follows the same rules. Write it on the facts of the stay. Never hold it back to influence what the guest writes.

### OP.9.6 Responding to criticism

Airbnb lets hosts post a [public response](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/32) to a published review, as long as the response follows the reviews policy. The response appears on the listing with the review, so you're writing for future guests, not arguing with the past one.

A process that holds up:

1. Wait a day (assumption). Don't reply while you're annoyed.
2. Check the facts. Pull the turnover photos, the message thread, and the friction log for that stay.
3. Sort the complaint: fair and fixable, fair but a known trade-off, or factually wrong.
4. Fix what's fixable before you respond, if you can, so you can say it's done.
5. Respond briefly. Thank them, acknowledge the specific point, and state the fix or the fact. Keep guest details private. Never bring up something the guest did unless it's needed to correct a factual claim.

Most criticism is fair and fixable. Treat it as free inspection data.

Disputes are for policy violations, not for disagreements. Airbnb says a published review stays up unless the author asks to remove it within 30 days, it violates the reviews policy, or other circumstances apply. Hosts can [dispute a review](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3582) that violates the policy, but a request doesn't guarantee removal. A retaliatory or extortion-based review may qualify. A three-star review that says the bunk room was hot doesn't. Don't dispute every negative review. Disputing fair criticism wastes time, and it doesn't fix the house.

### OP.9.7 The metrics tracker

Track the things you can act on, monthly. Each metric needs a definition, a source, a target, and an owner. The targets below are house rules for Cedar Ridge (assumptions), not Airbnb thresholds.

| Metric | Definition | Source | Target (assumption) | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First response time | Minutes from a guest's first message to a human or approved-template reply | Platform inbox or PMS | Median under 15 minutes, 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.; all under 60 | Message owner |
| Complaint reasons | Count by category: cleanliness, accuracy, check-in, amenities, noise, communication | Friction log, reviews, messages | No category repeats 3 times in a quarter without a fix | Owner |
| Cleanliness misses | Turnovers failing photo review, plus guest reports of cleaning problems | OP.8 records | Zero; any miss gets a root-cause note | Owner, cleaner |
| Host cancellations | Confirmed reservations the host cancelled, with reason | Platform | Zero outside safety events | Owner |
| Rating distribution | Count of 5, 4, 3, 2, 1-star overall ratings, not just the average | Reviews | Track the share below 5 and read every one | Owner |
| Review rate | Reviews received / completed stays | Platform | Record only; no target, since you can't push it | Owner |
| Repeat-stay signals | Returning guests, "we'll be back" mentions, direct-booking inquiries from past guests | Messages, reviews, Sam's OP.3 channel | Record and thank; never trade for reviews | Message owner |

Rating distribution matters more than the average. An average of 4.6 can hide one 3-star review that describes a real problem. Read the low ones first.

### OP.9.8 Worked example: Cedar Ridge's first 90 days

The base case expects 61 stays a year, about 5.1 a month. In the first 90 days that's about 61 × 90 / 365 = 15 stays (computed in python; actual launch months may be slower). If about 70% of guests leave a review (assumption), that's about 10 reviews in 90 days and about 43 in a full year.

**Tracker at day 90 (all data are assumptions for teaching).**

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Stays completed | 15 |
| Reviews received | 10 |
| Rating distribution | 7 five-star, 2 four-star, 1 three-star |
| Average | (7 × 5 + 2 × 4 + 1 × 3) / 10 = 46 / 10 = 4.6 |
| First response times (15 threads) | Median 9 minutes; 14 of 15 under 60 minutes; one at 90 minutes (overnight) |
| Complaint reasons | Bunk room too warm (2), check-in directions unclear (1), internet outage (1) |
| Cleanliness misses | 0 photo-review failures; 0 guest reports |
| Host cancellations | 0 |
| Repeat-stay signals | 2 "we'll be back" mentions; 1 inquiry about next summer |

**Reading it.** The average looks fine, but the distribution and complaint log say more. Two reviewers mentioned a warm bunk room. That's a repeat, so it gets a fix before the third mention. The lower level has no dedicated cooling in the bunk room (assumption). Chris gets a quote for a ceiling fan or a portable cooling unit. Either is above the $200 approval limit, so it goes on the owner decision list. Until then, the house manual adds a line on thermostat settings for the lower level. The listing adds a note, because accuracy covers comfort too.

The unclear check-in directions come from a mapping app that routes guests down a closed lake road (assumption). Maya adds a photo of the correct turnoff to the 7-day message and the house manual. The overnight 90-minute response came after 10 p.m., outside the messaging owner's hours. The team keeps the rule that safety messages go to the after-hours phone, and adds an after-hours auto-reply giving that number.

**The three-star review.** Suppose it reads: "Great lake access and clean house, but the bunk room was really hot and the kids didn't sleep well."

A public response that follows the process:

> Thanks for staying with us and for the specific feedback on the bunk room. You're right that the lower level ran warm during that heat wave. We've added cooling to the bunk room and updated the house manual with thermostat tips for the lower level. We're glad you enjoyed the lake.

That response is only accurate once the cooling is actually installed. If the owners haven't approved it yet, wait to post, or say what has actually been done. Don't dispute this review. It's fair, specific, and useful.

**What goes to Sam's OP.10.** The 90-day tracker, the complaint log with fixes and dates, the response-time data, and the list of pending owner decisions (backup internet, bunk room cooling). Sam's OP.10 review compares actual performance with the underwriting. These records explain the "why" behind his numbers.
