# Review: 0.3 Is Ownership Right for You? A Capital & Readiness Checklist

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The main idea comes through quickly and it's useful: the down payment is only one layer, and you have to keep the house's reserve and your family's emergency fund separate. But I was dropped into a story halfway through. Nobody tells me who Maya and Chris are, what "Cedar Ridge" is, what a "DSCR quote" is, or why there are two quotes. The page is full of course codes (PA.1, PA.6, OP.11, "0.2 Case B", "0.2 Part C", "Alder Street") that mean nothing to me. The biggest problem is that the scorecard's own outcome rules send Maya and Chris to "Improve financing or liquidity first", yet the lesson (and slide 17's title) says they "proceed to PA.1". If I copy the example, I'll apply the rules wrong.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. 0.3.1, line 17: "For Cedar Ridge, 25% of $385,000 is $96,250. Maya and Chris have $210,000..." Who are Maya and Chris? What is Cedar Ridge (what kind of house, where, is it their second home or a pure investment)? They're never introduced.
2. 0.3.4, line 70: "The DSCR quote in the canonical example is not a Fannie Mae loan." What does DSCR stand for? What's a "quote" here, and why are there two (DSCR and conventional)? Where do $2,557 and $2,484 come from? This is the first time either payment appears.
3. 0.3.4, line 64: "There is no minimum reserve requirement for a one-unit principal residence. Second homes and investment properties do have requirements." Which one is Cedar Ridge? The lesson never says, so I can't tell which rule applies.
4. 0.3.5, line 76: "Three questions shape whether a lender will lend... The fourth, liquidity, is the cash stack in 0.3.8." Three or four? Why point me forward to 0.3.8 here?
5. 0.3.5, line 93: "The same calculation with the DSCR quote's $2,557 gives 36.0%." Line 80 just said a DSCR loan qualifies on the property's income, not mine. So why compute my DTI on the DSCR quote at all?
6. 0.3.5, line 91: "DTI with no STR income counted: 35.6%". Is 35.6% good or bad? The lesson never says what to compare it with, not even "ask the lender for its maximum and write it next to this number".
7. 0.3.6, line 103: "The canonical weak case, ADR $265 at 50% occupancy, loses $5,475 a year". What is ADR? Is the $5,475 after the loan payment? (The course example file says it's annual cash flow after debt service. The lesson should say so.)
8. 0.3.6, line 105: "A delay followed by a weak year would use most of the property reserve." How much is left? By the lesson's own numbers it's $5,571 - $5,475 = $96. Why not say that?
9. 0.3.7, line 111: "With 61 stays". Where does 61 come from? And 212 rented nights (line 117)? Neither is introduced.
10. 0.3.7, line 113: "whether losses are usable can depend on material participation". What losses? Why would I want my hours to beat the cleaner's? "As 0.2 explained" assumes I took 0.2.
11. 0.3.7, line 115: "a full-service co-host at 18%". 18% of what?
12. 0.3.7, line 119: "Cedar Ridge ahead $27,732 before tax in the base case and behind $27,503". Ahead of or behind what? Compared with doing what with the money?
13. 0.3.4, line 72: "Maya and Chris will set their own reserve target in the worked example." Where? I read 0.3.8 twice and never found their own target. They only use the assumed 6-month lender rule.
14. 0.3.9, outcome rules: Maya and Chris have Yellow on line 5. The rule "Yellow or Red on lines 3 to 6 → Improve financing or liquidity first" matches them. So why does 0.3.10 say "Proceed to PA.1"?
15. 0.3.9, outcome rules: what happens with Yellow on line 10 (or 9, 11, 12, 13) and no Reds? No rule covers it. And what about Red on line 1 plus Red on line 2? Both "lease path" and "Two or more Reds: Wait" match. Which one wins?
16. 0.3.9, line 181, row 10 Yellow: "Works only if you self-manage and you're unsure you will". 0.3.10 scores Maya and Chris Yellow here, but nothing says they're unsure. The base case has them self-managing.
17. 0.3.9, line 180, row 9 Green requires "plus a local backup". 0.3.10 scores them Green while admitting "they name a local backup in PA.1", so they don't have one yet. Shouldn't that be Yellow?
18. 0.3.9, row 6: "property coverage (DSCR)". How do I compute the DSCR coverage? The lesson only shows DTI. Maya and Chris's line 6 is scored on DTI alone.
19. 0.3.9, line 164: "Fill this in before you shop." Line 5 needs a "written pre-qualification or term sheet". The lesson says written quotes come in PA.6/PA.7, which are after PA.1. So won't every first-timer score Yellow on 5 and get routed to "financing first"?
20. 0.3.9 outcome row 3: "Red on line 1 or 2, and lines 9 to 11 look fine". What counts as "look fine": Green only, or Yellow too?
21. 0.3.10, line 216: "which runs while PA.2 to PA.4 happen." What are PA.2 to PA.4? The slide 17 notes say "Sam's PA.2 to PA.4". Who is Sam?
22. 0.3.9 outcome row 3: "Alder Street needed $20,370 on day one." What is Alder Street?
23. Slide 19 / 0.3.12: "Fill in the eight-layer stack for one realistic price". How do I get closing costs, PITIA, furnishing, or carrying costs for my own price before I have a lender? The lesson gives Maya and Chris's numbers but no way to estimate mine.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **The outcome rules contradict the worked example (0.3.9 vs 0.3.10, slides 16 vs 17).** Slide 16 says "All Green, or Yellow only on line 1" goes to PA.1. The very next slide says "Maya and Chris score three Yellows, so they proceed to PA.1". A learner applying slide 16 literally sends them to "Improve financing first" (Yellow on line 5). The rules also have gaps (Yellows on 7 to 13 with no Red) and overlaps (Red on 1 and 2), with no order of precedence.
- **The promised own reserve target never shows up (0.3.4, line 72; slide 8 notes: "Maya and Chris will set their own target, and you'll see it on the scorecard").** Scorecard line 3 Green needs "the lender's rule and your own target". They score Green with only "$15,342 planned against an assumed 6-month rule". By the rubric that's Yellow ("At lender rule only").
- **The delay-plus-weak-year pairing is never tested.** 0.3.6 says "Plan for that pairing", but lines 7 and 8 score the delay and the weak year separately. Both come out Green, even though together they leave $96.
- **0.3.2, Layer 4: "Items from the inspection you accept".** "Accept" is unclear. It could mean agree to fix yourself, accept the house as-is, or accept a repair credit.
- **0.3.2, line 25: "Each layer sits on top of the one before it."** The order doesn't matter for the math. The metaphor suggests a sequence or dependency that the lesson never uses.
- **0.3.3, line 56: "For this lesson, we count only liquid savings."** This comes after a long HELOC paragraph that includes Maya and Chris's $260,000 of equity. Stating the rule first, then the reasons, would save the reader from wondering whether the equity counts.
- **0.3.5: credit and loan-type paragraphs appear in the notes for slide 6, but the slide shows only the DTI table.** The viewer hears about 740 credit scores, Kiavi, and the Fannie Mae September 2026 update while looking at a table of monthly debts.
- **0.3.8, option 2: "The conventional quote needs $2,888 less at closing".** It reads like a fix for the gap. It isn't: the conventional gap is still $8,172. It narrows the gap; it doesn't close it.
- **0.3.10: "Green, pending lender" (lines 4 and 6).** The scorecard only allows Green, Yellow, or Red. "Pending" is an unofficial fourth grade that the learner isn't told how to use.
- **0.3.10 outcome: "Line 1 has a dated funding plan: about 3 to 4 months of saving".** That's a duration, not a date. The rule says "dated".
