# Recurring examples (canonical numbers)

All figures are **assumptions for teaching**, generated by `tools/examples.py`. Do not change a number in a lesson without changing it here and re-running the script.

## Example A: the Alder Street unit (arbitrage)

A hypothetical two-bedroom, two-bath apartment (Unit 4B, 218 Alder Street) in a mid-size city we do not name, 10 minutes from a hospital campus and downtown. Sleeps 5. The student leases it from a small landlord who owns the four-unit building. The city is left unnamed on purpose: rules attach to an exact address, so lessons test the same unit against the official NYC and Los Angeles rules to show how the answer changes.

| Assumption | Value |
|---|---:|

| Average nightly rate (ADR) | $205 |
| Occupancy | 70% |
| Average stay | 3.0 nights |

### B5. Three ways to compute a coverage ratio on the DSCR quote

- Gross booking revenue / annual PITIA: 2.42 (weak case 1.87).
- Gross revenue discounted 20% / annual PITIA: 1.94.
- NOI before property tax and insurance / annual PITIA: 1.18.

Same house, three different answers. The lender's written definition decides which one counts. The teaching point: a lender ratio above 1.0 does not mean positive cash flow for the owner.
