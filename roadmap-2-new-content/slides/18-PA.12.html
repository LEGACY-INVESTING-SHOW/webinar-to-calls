Read your policy as the operator, checking nine items the lender never asked about

  • Named insured matches how you took title (you, your LLC, or both)
  • Effective date is the closing date or earlier
  • Covers short-term rental to paying guests, in writing
  • Liability limit per occurrence, including dock, water, and watercraft
  • Deductibles: all-perils, plus any wind, hail, or water deductible
  • Exclusions: slow leaks, mold, pools and hot tubs, 'business' use
  • Loss of rental income: when it pays, how it's calculated, for how long
  • Flood: your FEMA flood zone and whether you have a flood policy
  • Conditions the insurer set (Cedar Ridge: railing, alarms): proof sent, confirmed in writing
Slide 3 · Read your policy as the operator, checking nine items the lender never asked about

You bought, or bound, a policy before closing, because the lender required it. Now read it as the operator. The lender cared that the house was insured. You care whether a guest injury, a burst pipe, or a month without bookings is covered.

Pull the declarations page, the summary of who's insured, the limits, and the deductibles. Pull the policy form too. Check these nine items.

The named insured has to match how you took title in PA.10. The effective date has to be the closing date or earlier. A standard homeowners policy may exclude business use, so get the insurer's answer on rental use in writing.

Check the liability limit per incident, especially for the dock and the water. Note the deductibles. Read the exclusions. Check loss-of-rental-income coverage.

And the conditions. When Maya and Chris got their quote in due diligence, the insurer required the railing and alarms within 30 days. Send proof and get written confirmation.

The packet has three rules. I've turned them into gates you check before you publish the listing.

The first rule is that the listing doesn't go up until three rows say done. Insurance, with rental use and conditions confirmed in writing. The permit, issued, or the licensing office confirmed in writing that you may list first. And the safety sign-off, signed and dated.
