# Review: 3.1 How STR Regulation Actually Works: Licensing, Zoning & Primary-Residence Rules

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The main idea comes through fast: "Is Airbnb legal here?" is the wrong question, and you need a yes from every authority for one exact address. After that, the lesson loses me with numbers and names that don't match. There are "six layers," "nine steps," "five boxes," "14 rungs," and "six parties," and each list is in a different order. I never learn what "STR," "arbitrage," "HOA," or "Alder Street" mean before they're used. The worksheet I'm supposed to fill in has a rule ("one No means prohibited") that doesn't work for half its own questions, because answering "No" to "Is the building excluded?" is good news. The 120-night loss example also confused me: it says the student moves in, but then it counts all the rent as a business loss.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"Every rung of the ladder can say no"** (Slide 2 statement). The slide opens with "the ladder" as if I already know the metaphor. The word "ladder" only gets explained in the notes and in the Markdown.
- **The word "rung" means two different things.** In 3.1.1, "Each rung is a separate authority that can say no." In 3.1.11, rungs include "Hosted / unhosted," "Night cap," and "Number display," which are questions, not authorities. So there are six authority-rungs in 3.1.1/Slide 3 and 14 question-rungs in 3.1.11. I couldn't tell whether I was missing eight authorities.
- **Four different orderings of the same checks.** Slide 3 goes State > Zoning > Permit > Building > HOA > Lease. The 3.1.10 flow goes Plan > Jurisdiction > Ordinance > Parcel/building > Private rules > Permit mechanics. The 3.1.11 ladder goes State > Zoning > Permit category > Hosted > Primary residence > ... > Building type > Safety > HOA > Lease > .... The Slide 5 notes say to check primary residence "right after the hosted or unhosted clause," and Slide 18 says to check host-present, primary residence, and night cap first. I can't tell which order to actually work in.
- **The decision rule doesn't fit the questions** (3.1.11, "A single 'No' on any rung means prohibited"; Slide 15 title). For "Is the building excluded (rent-regulated, affordable, listed)?", "Is a primary-residence test required?", and "Is there a cap?", **No is the good answer**. "What stay length is 'short-term'?", "What inspections...?", "Who holds the permit?", and "Where must the number appear?" aren't yes/no questions at all. As written, the rule would mark a clean address "prohibited."
- **"Picture it as a vertical chain of boxes, each with a 'stop' exit on the right side"** (3.1.10). The Slide 14 diagram runs horizontally and has no exits on the side. The heading "in words" also suggests a diagram is missing from the Markdown.
- **"Stays at or above the line are treated as ordinary residential tenancies"** (3.1.6; Slide 7 bullet 2 says "count as"). This is stated as a universal rule, while the next paragraph says "Depending on the state and city." It's overconfident.
- **"New York City requires the host to live there, be present, and take two guests"** (Slide 9 title). "Take two guests" reads as a requirement to host exactly two. The rule is *no more than* two.
- **"Los Angeles allows home-sharing only in your own primary residence, 120 days a year"** (Slide 10 title). The same slide says Extended Home-Sharing allows more than 120, so the title overstates the limit. The slide also says "days" while the Alder Street math says "nights."
- **Slide 13, "Hosting only 120 nights at Alder Street's rent loses $10,445 a year."** The scenario has the student living in the unit ("the student would now live in the unit"), yet 100% of the rent counts as a loss. It isn't clear whether the $10,445 is a business loss or the student's real cost of living there.
- **The "Unresolved means no lease yet" box label on Slide 14** sits over text about blog posts not being sources and about Sam's 3.2. The label and the body say different things.
- **"ALL FIGURES ON THIS SLIDE ARE ASSUMPTIONS"** (Slides 11 and 12). These slides are mostly real NYC and LA rules. The label makes me think the rules themselves are made up.
- **"Every rule in this lesson comes from the city's or platform's own page"** (Slide 19 title). The general rules in 3.1.2 to 3.1.7 (state layer, zoning, HOA, tenant protections, transferability) have no sources. Only the NYC, LA, and Airbnb facts do, so the title overclaims.
- **"Some cities make the landlord's written consent part of the permit application"** (3.1.2), then 3.1.12's "the landlord, in a signed document, that you may use the address on a permit application." These read as two different documents: lease permission versus permit-application consent. The distinction is never explained.
