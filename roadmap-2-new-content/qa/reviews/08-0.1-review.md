# Review: 0.1 Welcome to STR Concierge: What's Different About Owning

Reviewer stance: a first-time learner who has never hosted and never bought an investment property, and who opened only this lesson and its 18-slide deck. Files reviewed: `parts/08-0.1.md`, `slides/src/08-0.1.json`, `slides/08-0.1.html` (screenshots of slides 1 to 18). I re-ran the Cedar Ridge and Alder Street arithmetic against `reference/recurring-examples.md`, and every total holds: $96,250, $14,438, $110,688, $142,688, 7.0x, $7,428, -$5,475, $1,548, $46,800, $31,242, $3,850 and $7,700. The problems are framing, jargon, one misleading money-at-risk claim, gate numbering, and internal production language that shows up in learner-facing text.

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The central idea is clear and useful: a lease caps your downside, owning doesn't, so you check the deal at a series of go/no-go points. The owner-vs-operator table and the "$142,688 vs $20,370" slide land well. After that I was buried in codes (PA.4, OP.10, SR.1, 1.3), unexplained terms (DSCR, PITIA, points, underwriting, buy box, "pencil", cash stack, contingency, Closing Disclosure) and two people (Preston, Sam) nobody introduces. Some parts read like a production file rather than a lesson: "the tracker is the course", "Production type / owner", "canonical example file", "Brief-provided, not independently opened", "computed with python". The money-at-risk slide confused me. It says failing Gate 7 puts $110,688 at stake, but Gate 7's own rule is "stop before any money moves", so that money isn't lost if I fail there.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Tracker table at the top (lines 4-9).** "Production type / owner | New / Preston" and "Connects to | ... Part One arbitrage lessons (1.3) for contrast" are production metadata. A learner doesn't care whether a lesson is "New", and the row is an unreadable list of codes.
- **"First, the tracker is the course" (0.1.3).** "Tracker" is an internal word. The paragraph also repeats the table directly above it in prose ("The full tracker order for Part Two starts with 0.1, 0.2, 0.3, and PA.1. Then come...").
- **"Every table I show you is really asking one question: how big is the commitment, and who has to say yes first" (slide 2 notes) and "the number of people whose sign-off you need" (0.1.1) vs. "You make the final decision on every gate" (0.1.4).** Do other people sign off, or do I decide? The lesson says both. Only the lender and the city really "sign off"; the others advise.
- **Slide 14's bar labels mix running totals and add-ons.** "$3,850", then "+ $1,500", then "$110,688" (no plus sign), then "+ $32,000". The Gate 8 bar is drawn at $142,688, which leaves out the earnest money and inspection fees shown just above it. I can't tell whether the bars are cumulative.
- **Slide 13 mixes cases.** The repairs row ($7,428) is base case. The next row (-$5,475) is weak case. Nothing on the slide says so.
- **"Way out" row (0.1.6, slide 13).** It compares a lease's maximum remaining rent ($46,800) with a sale's transaction costs ($31,242). These aren't the same kind of number. Rent is paid over time and the unit can still earn during it. Sale costs leave out any drop in price. As shown, it makes owning's exit look cheaper, which works against the lesson's point.
- **ASCII flowchart (0.1.5).** A 50-line code block of gates with "Yes | v" arrows is hard to scan on a phone and repeats what slides 10 and 11 show better.
- **"The last row is where 0.2 picks up. An owner can earn from four places at once" (0.1.2).** The prose never names the four. I had to find them in the table cell. The deck notes (slide 4) name them; the Markdown doesn't.
- **"Read the 'Maintenance' and 'Capital repairs' rows twice" (0.1.2).** The difference between maintenance and a capital repair isn't explained. The slide 3 row label also drops the examples the Markdown gives: "(roof, HVAC, septic, water heater)".
- **Gate numbering on slides 10 and 11.** The big numerals are 01-05 on both slides, but the gates are 0-4 and 5-9. On slide 10, "02" is Gate 1; on slide 11, "01" is Gate 5. I spent a while working out which number to trust.
- **"Gate 3 ... at a real loan quote" before "Gate 4 Financing ... a written quote".** The order looks circular (see Question 7).
- **Slide 6 skips PA.5, PA.6 and PA.7** and goes PA.4 → PA.8. The title says "each stage hands the next one a document", so I wondered what financing hands over.
- **Gate log Gate 0 says "0.3 scorecard".** 0.3 is two lessons away, and "scorecard" isn't described.
