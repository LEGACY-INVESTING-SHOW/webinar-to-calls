# Review: PA.11 Closing Day: Documents, Wire Fraud Prevention & Possession

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The frame is clear and useful: money, documents, and control all move at closing, and trouble starts when one gets ahead of the others. The wire fraud section is the most usable part. I could follow it with zero background. The worked example lost me, though. "Cedar Ridge", "Maya and Chris", "the canonical example", "B1", "PITIA", and "DSCR" all appear without introduction. The table also uses Loan Estimate figures and Closing Disclosure section letters, right after PA.11.2 told me a DSCR loan may come with neither. The biggest problem is the sequence. The lesson says to walk the house and sign before you wire, but the exercise B checklist, which claims to be "in order", puts the wire before the walkthrough.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Order of operations contradicts itself.** Slide 2 notes: "Check the figures first. Walk the house. Sign what matches. Verify the wire by voice. Then collect control before you leave." Exercise B (PA.11.9, slide 15, "in order") lists: insurance binder → trusted contacts "set up at contract" → wire verified → wire sent → walkthrough → reviewed documents before signing. So in the checklist, the wire goes out before the walkthrough. Also, "Trusted contacts... set up at contract" happens weeks before "Received final figures at least 3 business days ahead", yet it's listed after it. The Markdown in PA.11.1 never states a strict order, but the notes invent one. A learner doesn't know which to follow.
- **Loan Estimate vs no Loan Estimate.** PA.11.2 teaches that a DSCR loan is business-purpose credit and may not come with the consumer forms. The worked example is a DSCR loan, but PA.11.3, PA.11.4, and slides 4-6 are framed entirely around a Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure sections. The example ends up teaching the case that PA.11.2 said might not apply.
- **"Cash to close" vs "funds to wire".** The lesson defines cash to close as down payment plus closing costs, then subtracts earnest money to get the wire. A learner holding a real statement can't tell whether their form's bottom line has already netted the deposit. The lesson never says to confirm this. Since this is the number that gets wired, a mistake here matters.
- **"The real STR insurance policy explains most of the $659 increase"** (slide 7 title). The two insurance lines add up to $700, which is more than the whole $659. "Most" undersells it, and the viewer has to work out that a later closing date (-$286) offset the other fee increases.
- **Slide 7 bar for prepaid interest.** The -$286 is drawn as a rightward green bar, the same direction as the increases, and it's longer than every fee bar except insurance. At a glance it reads as a large increase.
- **"Most of Cedar Ridge's lender and title fees matched the estimate, and four moved"** (slide 5). This is fine, but slide 5 never tells the viewer why they moved. The reasons don't come until slide 7, two slides later.
- **"Over 14 personal days in the coming year changes the treatment"** (slide 3). "The treatment" is undefined on the slide, and the consequence is never stated.
- **Wire amount authority** (PA.11.7 vs PA.11.10). The lender "confirms... the exact wire amount". The settlement agent receives the wire and confirms the instructions and the amount by voice. Two authorities for one number is exactly the ambiguity a scammer exploits.
- **"Collect six kinds of control"** (slide 13 title). Warranties, manuals, vendor records, permits, and payment proof are records, not control. PA.11.1 defines control as "keys, codes, accounts, records, and the right to occupy", so the fit is loose.
- **"ALL FIGURES ON THIS SLIDE ARE ASSUMPTIONS"** (slide 16). The slide has no figures, only three item names. The label reads as boilerplate.
- **"Its guidance comes down to a handful of steps. Here's how I'd turn them into a protocol"** (PA.11.7). The steps then mix CFPB quotes with the teacher's own additions (step 7, confirm receipt). That's fine, but the learner can't tell which steps are CFPB guidance and which are Preston's.
