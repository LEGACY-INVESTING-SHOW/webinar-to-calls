# Review: PA.7 DSCR vs. Conventional Financing: Which Path Fits Your Deal

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

I opened this lesson to find out which loan to get. The thing I most needed, what "DSCR" even stands for, is never spelled out. The lesson assumes I already know DSCR, PITIA, LTV, ADR, NOI, points, cash-on-cash, Schedule E and seasoning, and it sends me to five other lessons (PA.4, PA.6, PA.8, PA.10, OP.7, OP.11, and something called "0.3") without saying what I would find there. The core comparison is clear and the numbers add up: conventional is cheaper, and the weak case loses money on both loans. But production scaffolding shows through ("Tracker title note," "Production note," "the production brief," "this research environment"), and that made me trust the Fannie Mae section less than I should. The decision tree also contradicts itself: Maya and Chris sit at 35.6% DTI, which by the lesson's own definition is "DTI already near the limit," so they belong on Branch C, yet the worked example runs them through Branch A.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

3. **PA.7.1, line 22:** "both 25% down." PA.7.2 then says a conventional investment purchase can go to 85% LTV, which is 15% down. Why does the conventional quote use 25% down? Is it just to hold the comparison equal? The lesson should say so.

This row says the house loses money whenever they hire a co-host, even in the base case. That alarmed me, and the lesson never comes back to it. Should Maya and Chris buy at all?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Line 10, "Tracker title note | The live tracker spells this title ... 'Which Path Fits Your Heal.'"** This is internal housekeeping. A learner reads it as a sloppy typo notice at the top of the lesson.
- **Line 86, "because the full section wasn't readable from this research environment. The production brief describes..."** and **line 92, "[Production note: before recording...]"** Behind-the-scenes language. The learner doesn't know what a "research environment" or "production brief" is, and the honest message ("we haven't verified the exact conditions; your lender must") gets lost under it.
- **Line 112, "which is already inside the extra interest figure."** The arithmetic is right: $4,374 more in payments plus $1,080 more owed equals the $5,454 of extra interest. But the sentence gives the conclusion without the addition, so it reads like a contradiction.
- **Lines 114-117, the five-year cash-flow bullets.** They come right after the cost table and restate the same P&I difference from a different angle. That invites double-counting (Question 16).
- **Line 74, "Their DTI rules depend on their actual debts, credit, reserves, and the underwriting system..."** "DTI rules" is garbled. The limit depends on the system; the ratio depends on the debts.
- **Line 76 vs. line 176 (Branch C: "DTI already near the limit") vs. line 185 ("35.6% ... Conventional quote first").** The lesson calls 35.6% "almost no room" on a manual file, then defines Branch C as "near the limit," then puts the couple on Branch A anyway. A careful learner stalls here.
- **Line 42 / slide 3 title, "cheaper on every line."** "Principal paid in year 1" and "cash-on-cash" aren't costs. "Better on every line" is what's meant.
- **Line 164, "If no, treat your file as Branch C"** vs. **slide 13 notes, "If you're near the limit, you're really on Branch C."** "Over the limit" and "near the limit" are different tests.
- **Slide 4 notes, "If the house only works on the cheaper loan in the base case, the house is the problem, not the loan."** Cedar Ridge works on both loans in the base case, so this rule doesn't describe the example on screen. It confuses more than it teaches.
- **Line 46 / slide 5 title, "using its investor's guide."** In a course for investors, "investor" read as "me."
- **Slide 6 calc layout.** Rows go $2,400, "+ $600", "= 19.5%", then "+ $2,484", "= 35.6%". It looks like dollars are being added to a percentage, and the division by $15,417 never appears.
