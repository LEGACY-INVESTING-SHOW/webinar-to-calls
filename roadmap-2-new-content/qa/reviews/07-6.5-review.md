# Review: 6.5 When to Bring In a Local Property Manager

Reviewer stance: first-time learner. I have never hosted, never bought or leased a rental, and have not seen any other lesson. I read `parts/07-6.5.md` top to bottom, then watched all 19 slides of `slides/07-6.5.html` with the speaking notes from `slides/src/07-6.5.json`.

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The title promises "when to bring in a property manager," and the answer turns out to be "for this unit, almost never; hire an on-call handyman instead." That's a useful surprise, but I only learn it on slide 15. The four-setup ladder (slide 3) and the contract checklist (slides 7-8) are clear and I could act on them tomorrow. The money half lost me. I don't know what a "stress case," "ADR," "A1/A2/A3," "canonical," "break-even occupancy," "host earnings," or the "$4,420 operating reserve" are, and when I check the math myself ($205 × 255 nights = $52,275), it doesn't match the $61,625 gross the lesson uses. The threshold rule ("two or more tests true") also seems to contradict the lesson's own point that a local contact is a promise you already signed.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. **"keeps the stress case alive"** (slide 1 title; Markdown 6.5.0 "in both the base and stress cases"). What is a stress case? It isn't defined until slide 12's caption ("Stress case A2: ADR $180, 60% occupancy..."), and even there it's only a list of numbers. I later found out lesson 1.3.7 defines it, but this lesson never says so.
2. **"Alder Street books 255 nights a year at a $205 ADR, with 85 stays. Gross booking revenue is $61,625."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 10 side text). 255 × $205 = $52,275, not $61,625. Where does the other $9,350 come from? (From the reference file it's the $110 guest cleaning fee × 85 stays, but the lesson never says so.) This is the first number I tried to check and it failed.
3. **"Host earnings: $61,625 minus $9,552 = $52,073"** (Markdown 6.5.7 Setup 3; slide 11). What is the $9,552? The lesson says "platform fee" but never gives the rate (15.5% of nightly plus cleaning, per the reference file) or says it's Airbnb's fee.
4. **"If two or more are true, you need at least an on-call vendor."** (Markdown 6.5.3; slide 4 title). What if only test 4 is true, so my lease or permit requires a local contact I don't have? Slide 2's notes say "It's a commitment you've already signed." One unmet written promise should be enough on its own. Same question for test 1: if I'm 5 hours away, how do I keep the 5.7 promise with zero help?
5. **"If you're still failing them after adding one, move up a level."** (Markdown 6.5.3; slide 4 end bar). How can I "fail" the distance test after adding a vendor? My distance doesn't change. Which tests am I supposed to re-check, and after how long?
6. **"Volume: you're adding units in the same area."** (Markdown 6.5.3 test 5). Why does adding units mean I *need* help? It reads as "help gets cheaper per unit," which is a reason it's affordable, not a reason it's needed.
7. **"Net before income tax is $12,218 a year"** (Markdown 6.5.7). Which costs make this up? The list of cost categories is given but no amounts. I can't rebuild $12,218. At least tell me where the full breakdown lives.
8. **"plus a $600 yearly budget for billed call-outs"** (Markdown 6.5.7 Setup 2; slide 10). Isn't "repairs" already in the base costs? Is the $600 on top of the existing repairs budget, or does it replace part of it?
9. **"All fee rates and hours below are assumptions unless marked canonical. Every figure comes from the canonical model's inputs."** (Markdown 6.5.7, second paragraph). Which is it: assumptions, or from the model? And what does "canonical" mean?
10. **"Now run the same four setups through the stress case (A2: ADR $180, 60% occupancy, gross $47,450, net $1,548)."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 12 caption). $180 × 60% × 365 = $39,420, not $47,450. Same cleaning-fee gap as Q2.
11. **"Break-even occupancy tells the same story"** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 13). How is break-even calculated? No formula, no pointer to lesson 1.3.4 where it's defined. The exercise then asks me to fill in "Break-even occupancy" for my own unit (Part B) with no method.
12. **"With a full manager, Alder Street needs about 70% occupancy just to break even at the base rate. That's the base case itself."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 13 title "which is the whole base case"). What occupancy is the base case? The Markdown never states it. Slide 13's caption says "70%," but 255 ÷ 365 = 69.9%. Is 70.1% above or below the base case? (It's above, which is why net is -$107, but the lesson doesn't say so.)
13. **"3a. Co-host 10% of gross"** is in the break-even table, but 3b (host earnings) is missing (Markdown 6.5.7 break-even table; slide 13). Why was it dropped?
14. **"A fee of about 10.1% leaves you $500 a month."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 14 box). Why $500? Is that a recommended floor, or just an example?
15. **"Assume (assumption) the vendor saves you 4 hours a month, the co-host 14 hours, and the manager 20 hours."** (Markdown 6.5.7). Where do these hours come from? The "cheapest per hour" conclusion (slide 14 title) depends entirely on them.
16. **"which the $4,420 operating reserve can carry"** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 15). What operating reserve? This is its first mention in the lesson. How long would $4,420 last at -$71 a month?
17. **"A co-host becomes reasonable only after rent comes down or the comps support a higher rate."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 15). Can rent come down on a signed lease? What are "comps"?
18. **"For Alder Street, I'd pick setup 2 if you live far away."** (Markdown 6.5.7; slide 15). What if I live nearby? Is setup 1 then the pick?
19. **"Check your SR.1 research."** (Markdown 6.5.3 test 4; slide 4). What is SR.1, and what "research" did I do?
20. **"Sam's 9.10 and 9.13 lessons"** (Markdown 6.5.4; slide 5). Who is Sam? Slide 5's notes say "Bring back from those how to set a floor and ceiling in the tool itself," but those lessons come after this one. How do I bring something back from a lesson I haven't taken?
21. **"Go back to your 3.4 scorecard."** (Markdown 6.5.8 Part C; slide 16). What scorecard? What do I do with it once I'm there?
22. **"Alder Street scored a zero there with a 20.1-point cushion."** (slide 13 notes only). Is a zero bad? It sounds like failing. (In 3.4, 0 means no red-flag points, which is good.)
23. **"Tennessee, for example, licenses Vacation Lodging Services"** (Markdown 6.5.6; slide 9). Why Tennessee? Is this lesson assuming I'm there? What are a "firm license" and a "designated agent license" in plain words?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"a margin you share with the landlord's rent"** (Markdown 6.5.2 last paragraph; slide 3 gold box: "a margin you already share with the landlord's rent"). You don't share margin with rent. What I think is meant: rent is a big fixed cost, so what's left for fees is thin. As written, it reads like the landlord gets a cut of profit.
- **"All figures on this slide are assumptions"** tag on slides 2, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15, while slide 2's caption says "(canonical)" and slide 10's box says "canonical A1." On the same slide I'm told a number is both an assumption and canonical. Nothing tells me which figures are the running example and which are this lesson's own guesses (retainer, fee rates, hours saved).
- **"canonical"** is used 8+ times in learner-facing text ("canonical example," "canonical A1," "unless marked canonical"). That's production vocabulary. A learner reads it as "official" or "verified."
- **"4. Full manager 20% of gross (A3)"** (slide 12; Markdown "Setup 4: full manager at 20% of gross (canonical A3)"). A1 and A2 are cases (base and stress). A3 looks like a third case, but it's a fee setup. The codes add nothing for a learner.
- **Slide 11 calc layout.** Three lines ($6,162, $52,073, $5,207) have no operators, then "= Difference per year $955." It looks like the three lines sum to $955. The $52,073 is an intermediate, not a term in the difference.
- **Slide 12 title: "every setup beyond cleaner only loses money every month"**. The figures are yearly averages divided by 12, not every month. Seasonal units lose in some months and gain in others. This overclaims.
- **Slide 12 caption: "Costs are per year"** while two of the four number columns are per month. Mixed units in one table with the unit only in the caption.
- **Slide 13 bar colours.** Green, dark green (bold label), and red bars have no legend. I eventually worked out that dark = $180 and red = full manager, but the red rows are also $205 and $180. The one comparison I need, each bar versus the case's actual occupancy (69.9% / 60%), is only in the caption.
- **Slide 14 title packs two unrelated points.** "The co-host is cheapest per hour saved, but fees above about 10% leave under $500 a month." The table supports the first half, the box supports the second. The notes then say "Cost per hour ... isn't the deciding number. The stress case is." So the title leads with the number the lesson says doesn't matter.
- **Slide 12 notes start with Setup 4 base math** ("Setup 4 is a full manager at 20% of gross ... Net becomes negative $107 a year") while the slide on screen is the stress-case table. There's no slide showing the setup 4 calculation, so the viewer is looking at one thing and hearing another.
- **Markdown 6.5.7: "If a manager's fee also covered cleaning coordination, you'd still owe the cleaner."** "Coordination" versus "paying" isn't clear. Does 20% ever include cleaning? This sentence raises a question without answering it.
- **Markdown 6.5.3: "None of them says 'hire a manager because hosting feels stressful.' Stress is real..."** This uses "stress" (feeling) in the same lesson as "stress case" (a financial scenario). Easy to cross-wire.
- **Test 4 on slide 4: "True if a promise is unmet".** "Unmet" is vague. Unmet now, or at risk of being unmet?
