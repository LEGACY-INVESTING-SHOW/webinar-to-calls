# Review: SR.5 Tool comparison guide (PMS, dynamic pricing, AI guest-messaging)

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

I can tell this is a worksheet for choosing software, and the "weights before you see the tools" idea lands. But I hit a wall of undefined terms in the first two sections (PMS vs. channel manager, merchant of record, lodging tax remittance, "stack", Sam, OP.4, SR.1 Section D) and never got a plain definition of the most important one, merchant of record. The sheet asks me to rate about 27 detailed criteria in SR.5.3 to SR.5.5, then hands me a comparison table with 8 different rows, and never says how one becomes the other. The worked example is the best part. Then its Cedar Ridge rerun breaks the rule it just taught me: applied as written, Rule 3 picks Tool X, and the lesson picks Tool Y. The deck's version of that same slide skips the close-call reasoning entirely.

I rate 10 PMS criteria, 8 pricing criteria and 9 messaging criteria, then SR.5.6's table has 8 different rows ("Channel connections", "Direct booking and payments", "Cost fit vs. software budget"...). How do the 27 detailed ratings turn into the 8 summary ratings? Average? Lowest? Gut feel?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **SR.5.6 Rule 3 vs. SR.5.10 rerun.** Rule 3 says "If they split or tie, pick the one with the lower setup and migration effort." The rerun paragraph gets a tie on the only top-weight row, then quietly drops to the weight-4 rows. That contradicts the rule as written. Applied literally, the rule gives Tool X. This is the biggest logic flaw in the lesson.
- **SR.5.10: "The gap is 0.30 on rounded scores but 0.296 unrounded, so under Rule 3 this is a close call".** The lesson shows every score to two decimals, but this sentence decides the rule on an unrounded figure that was never shown. That undercuts the "just use the formula" message.
- **SR.5.3 to SR.5.5 vs. SR.5.6 table.** The detailed criteria and the summary rows are different lists with different names. Only "Calendar and rate sync" appears in both. "Direct booking and payments" and "Cost fit" have no detailed criteria at all.
- **SR.5.4: "Set a floor at your break-even rate".** Break-even here is an occupancy at an average rate, not a nightly rate. As written, the instruction could lead a learner to set a floor that's too high (floor = average) or to pick one of two numbers at random.
- **SR.5.1 Vrbo paragraph** (six sentences, two articles, three concepts). "Merchant of record", "integrated property manager", "payment processing", "tax collection and remittance" and "Owner Dashboard" all arrive in one block with none defined.
- **Slide 3 flow diagram.** The arrows run PMS → Pricing → Messaging → Payments in a line, but the text says pricing sends rates back to the PMS and messaging reads from the PMS. There is no pricing → messaging handoff. So "the failures sit at the arrows" points at arrows that don't match the real handoffs. "Payments and taxes" isn't a tool, yet it's drawn as the fourth piece.
- **Slide 10 gates.** The outcome boxes are all red (warning color), including "Pick it, if price and terms check out", which is the good outcome. Gate 3 ("Are the top two within 0.30?") is just the "no" branch of Gate 2, shown as a separate question. Gate 4 ("Is every candidate knocked out?") logically follows Gate 1 but sits last.
- **SR.5.6 order vs. use order.** The lesson teaches scoring (5.6) and pricing (5.7) before the trial (5.8) and tests (5.9). The criteria tables in 5.3 to 5.5 refer to T1 to T10 before they're defined. Nothing tells me what order to actually do things in.
- **SR.5.5 vs. T9.** SR.5.5 says "The guest is told to call 911 first". T9 says "Tells guest to leave and call 911". The two instructions don't match.
