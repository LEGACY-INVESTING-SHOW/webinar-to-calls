# Email style & voice

The emails go out from **Preston Seo**, first person ("I"). Preston is following up with someone
who attended his free class but hasn't booked a call yet.

**The point of the email is to get them to book the call** — not to answer their question. If
they asked something in the chat, address it briefly and plainly so it's no longer a reason to
hold back, then move them to the call. The answer is a means to the booking, never the main
event. Keep it short and warm, like a real person following up. Helpful, not salesy.

## What to call the event
- Call it the **"personal finance masterclass"** (that's what we advertise on the front end).
- Say "**on Zoom**" so it feels like the live class it was.
- **Never** call it the "Legacy Wealth Blueprint webinar." Legacy Wealth Blueprint is the
  product name, and "webinar" reads as a sales funnel. Both are too salesy.

## Opening (use this framing)
Start by thanking them for coming, then say you were going back through the chat and saw
their question that you didn't get to answer live. For example:

> Hey [First name], thanks for joining me on Zoom for the personal finance masterclass.
> I was going back through the chat afterward and saw a question from you I didn't get a
> chance to answer live.

Then quote or paraphrase their actual question, and answer it.

## Structure (every email)
1. Thank-you + "I was going through the chat and saw your question I didn't get to answer."
2. Their question, in their words (short).
3. A plain, direct answer (1-3 sentences). Approved facts from `offer-and-booking.md`; for a
   general finance/tax concept you may answer briefly from general knowledge. Never invent
   offer specifics, prices, or guarantees, and never give individualized advice.
4. A short bridge: the rest depends on their specific situation, which is what the free call
   is for. The team looks at their actual numbers.
5. The booking link on its own line, with a plain CTA ("You can apply here and pick a time
   that works:"). Primary link for most; backup link for booking-friction leads.
6. Sign off.

## Hard writing rules
- **No em-dashes (—) and no en-dashes (–).** Use periods, commas, or "to" instead. Short
  sentences are better than one long sentence held together by dashes.
- **100-160 words.** Subject line short and specific to their question (no hype, no "RE:").
- One clear CTA and one link.
- Plain language. Write like you talk. No corporate filler, no hype, no pressure, no emojis,
  no exclamation-point spam (at most one).
- Never quote a program price or a specific dollar/ROI guarantee in writing.
- Don't invent details about the person or the offer.

## Banned phrases (do not write these or anything like them)
- "the honest best next step" / "the best next step is"
- "No pressure" / "zero pressure"
- "a clearer picture" / "clarity on your situation" as a closing line
- "grab whatever time works" / "grab a time"
- "jump into" / "jumping into" the webinar
- "I wanted to reach out" / "I hope this email finds you well"
- "circle back," "touch base," "at the end of the day," "game-changer," "unlock"
- Anything that sounds like a template or an autoresponder.

## Segment-specific notes
- **Booking-friction** (tried to book, hit an error): open with the question framing, then
  acknowledge the snag plainly ("looks like the booking page gave you trouble"), give the
  **backup** link (bookasessionworkshop), and offer that the team will find a time if none
  work. If they reported a site block (Norton/firewall), give the Typeform mirror instead.
- **Skeptic / objection** ("is this legit / too good to be true"): take the skepticism
  seriously and answer it straight. The strategies are real, the call is free, they decide
  at the end. No hype, no defensiveness.
- **Win-back** (booked then canceled): warm and easy. "Looks like your call didn't end up
  happening." No guilt. Make re-booking feel simple.
- **Price question**: don't quote a number. The investment depends on their situation and is
  covered on the call. Mention the call itself is free, and that there are flexible / 0%
  financing options if cost is the worry.
