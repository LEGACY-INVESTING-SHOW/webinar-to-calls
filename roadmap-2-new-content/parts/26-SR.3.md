### SR.3.0 What this resource does

This is a scenario workbook for one property. It estimates how much depreciation a cost-segregation study might move into year one, and what that could mean for tax. It's built for the conversation with your CPA, not to replace it.

It does four jobs. It collects every input a CPA will ask for, with a source and a status beside each. It separates ordinary depreciation from accelerated depreciation, so you can see what the study actually changes. It shows the illustrative tax effect only after the loss limits you enter, so a big deduction you can't use doesn't look like cash. And it lists every assumption that is still unverified, so nothing gets treated as settled by accident.

Sam's PA.5 lesson explains what a cost-segregation study is and walks through a year-one example. Use this workbook after that lesson, when you have a real property and a real quote from a study provider.

One warning up front. Whether any of this applies depends on the property, the dates, and how you use it. It also depends on your participation, your income, and the tax year. The workbook makes those inputs visible. Your CPA decides them.

### SR.3.1 Workbook layout

### SR.3.5 Decision rules built into the workbook

**Rule 1. Bonus rate depends on dates and eligibility.** The IRS says the One, Big, Beautiful Bill made the 100% additional first-year depreciation deduction permanent. It covers eligible property acquired after January 19, 2025 ([IRS news release](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-guidance-on-the-additional-first-year-depreciation-deduction-amended-as-part-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill); [Notice 2026-11](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-11.pdf)). Property is not treated as acquired after the date a written binding contract for it was signed. That's why B6 asks for the contract date, not only the closing date. Publication 946 describes 100% for qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. It also describes 40% for certain property placed in service after December 31, 2024 and before January 20, 2025 ([IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946)). Taxpayers may elect 40% instead of 100% for property placed in service in the first tax year ending after January 19, 2025. The screen in B21 only checks dates. The CPA confirms eligibility and enters any election in B22.

**Rule 2. Bonus applies to short-life property, never to land or the whole building.** Qualified property must be tangible property depreciated under MACRS. Its recovery period must be 20 years or less ([IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946)). The 27.5-year and 39-year building lives are longer, so B13 applies bonus only to B11 (reclassified parts plus furnishings). Land is not depreciable at all ([IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946)).

**Rule 3. The building life is a CPA decision.** Publication 946 defines residential rental property as a building earning 80% or more of gross rental income from dwelling units. It excludes hotel, motel, or inn units where more than half the units are used on a transient basis ([IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946)). How that applies to a single house rented for short stays is a question for your CPA. Canonical B6 uses 39 years and shows 27.5 for comparison. With 27.5 years, Cedar Ridge's remaining-building line would be $8,872 per full year instead of $6,256.

**Rule 4. The personal-use day test.** Publication 527 treats a dwelling unit as used as a home in one case. That's when personal use exceeds the greater of 14 days or 10% of the days rented at a fair rental price ([IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)). B24 in the Calc tab runs that test. Publication 527 also says that with any personal use, expenses are divided between rental and personal use. Set B36 to 1 if your CPA applies that division to depreciation.

**Rule 5. Average stay and material participation.** Publication 925 says an activity with an average customer stay of 7 days or less is not a rental activity. You figure the average by dividing total days in all rental periods by the number of rentals ([IRS Publication 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925)). B22 does that math. Passing that test does not make the loss usable by itself. The CPA then applies the material participation tests. Two examples are more than 500 hours, or more than 100 hours and at least as much as anyone else ([IRS Publication 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925)). That's why B27 and B28 ask for your hours and everyone else's. A cleaner or co-host who logs more hours than you can matter here.

**Rule 6. Loss limits come before the tax effect.** If the activity is passive, the loss may be suspended and carried forward, so B32 would be 0%. Publication 925 describes a special allowance of up to $25,000 for active participation in rental real estate. It phases out between $100,000 and $150,000 of MAGI ([IRS Publication 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925)). Ask the CPA whether that allowance can apply to your activity at all, given Rule 5. At Maya and Chris's assumed MAGI of about $185,000, the allowance would be fully phased out either way. A separate excess business loss limit can also apply. For 2025 the IRS threshold was $313,000, or $626,000 for joint filers, and disallowed amounts carry forward as a net operating loss ([Form 461 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i461)). The CPA supplies the current-year figure.

**Rule 7. Late studies need a method change.** Suppose the property was placed in service in an earlier year. Reclassifying it can then require Form 3115, a change in accounting method, with a section 481(a) catch-up adjustment ([About Form 3115](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-3115); [IRS Publication 946](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p946)). Add a note in B16 if this applies. The CPA handles the filing.

**Rule 8. Depreciation reduces basis whether or not you claim it.** Publication 527 says basis goes down by the depreciation you could have deducted. That holds even if you didn't claim it ([IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527)). The Outputs tab carries the accelerated and straight-line pools forward so the recapture conversation in OP.12 starts with real numbers.

Low and high happen to land on the same remaining-building figure. That's a coincidence of these two inputs, not a rule.

Here is the base column worked by hand, so you can follow each formula:

1. Basis: $385,000 + $6,000 = $391,000.
2. Land: $391,000 × 20% = $78,200. Building: $391,000 × (1 − 20%) = $312,800.
3. Reclassified parts: $312,800 × 22% = $68,816. Remaining building: $312,800 − $68,816 = $243,984.
4. Accelerated: ($68,816 + $32,000) × 100% = $100,816.
5. Ordinary: $243,984 ÷ 39 = $6,256.
6. Year-1 deduction: $100,816 + $6,256 = $107,072.
7. Illustrative tax effect: $107,072 × 32% = $34,263, only if all of it is usable this year.

Now the adjustments your CPA is likely to raise. Each row changes one thing from the base.

| Base with one change | Year-1 deduction | Illustrative tax effect at 32% |
|---|---:|---:|
| Base as above | $107,072 | $34,263 |
| Rental-use fraction applied: 212 ÷ (212 + 10) = 95.5% | $102,249 | $32,720 |
| Mid-month convention, placed in service in June | $104,205 | $33,346 |
| Passive activity, no loss usable this year | $107,072 carried forward | $0 this year |
| No study at all (furnishings bonus + building at 39 years) | $40,021 | $12,807 |

The mid-month row uses $243,984 ÷ 39 × 6.5 ÷ 12 = $3,389 for the building instead of $6,256.

Read the table this way. The study's real contribution, compared with no study, is $67,051 more deduction in year one ($107,072 − $40,021). At 32% that's $21,456, or $16,956 after a $4,500 fee, if fully usable. The passive row is the one to fear. In that case, the year-one tax effect is zero and the deduction waits. The furnishings bonus appears in both the study and no-study rows, because furniture is short-life property whether or not you order a study.

Two more checks from the day-count inputs:

- Home-use test: the greater of 14 days or 10% × 212 = 21.2 days. Ten personal days is below that, so Cedar Ridge is not "used as a home" under the Publication 527 test, as entered.
- Average stay: 212 ÷ 61 = 3.48 days, which is 7 days or less. The CPA then decides passive status using the participation tests and Maya and Chris's hour logs.

### SR.3.7 Evidence to request from a study provider

The IRS publishes a [Cost Segregation Audit Techniques Guide, Publication 5653](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf), for its examiners. The current version is dated February 2025. It says there are no standards for preparing these studies. It also says studies vary widely in methodology, documentation, depth, format, and preparer expertise. It describes approaches ranging from a detailed engineering approach from actual cost records to survey or letter approaches and "rule of thumb" approaches. Its principal elements of a quality study include preparation by someone with expertise and experience. They also include a reconciliation of total allocated costs to total actual costs. Its information document requests ask who took part in preparing a study and what role each person played, and where the property's blueprints came from.

Use that guide to shape what you ask for before you pay. The first four rows come straight from the points above. The rest are my recommendations for making the study usable in this workbook.

| Request | Why it matters | Received? |
|---|---|---|
| Preparer's name, credentials, and relevant experience | Pub 5653 lists expertise and experience as a principal element | |
| Every participant and their role (engineer, appraiser, analyst) | Pub 5653 information document requests ask for this | |
| Name and description of the method used | Pub 5653 contrasts detailed engineering, survey, and rule-of-thumb approaches | |
| Reconciliation of allocated costs to total purchase price and basis | Pub 5653 lists reconciliation as a principal element | |
| Source of plans or blueprints, or site-visit notes and photos | Pub 5653 asks where blueprints came from; for an older house the provider may measure on site | |
| Asset-by-asset schedule: description, cost, class, recovery period | Fills B17 to B19 and the CPA's depreciation schedule | |
| Legal reasoning for each classification | Lets the CPA check why a component is personal property or a land improvement | |
| Land allocation method and support | Ties to B11; land can't be depreciated | |
| How furnishings already on your books were treated | Avoids counting furniture twice | |

### SR.3.8 How to use this with a CPA

1. Before the meeting, fill every input you can document. Leave the CPA cells blank rather than guessing. Mark each status honestly.
2. Send the CPA the workbook, the settlement statement, the furnishing receipts, the study quote, and your calendar of rental and personal days. If you already have the study, send it with the evidence from SR.3.7.
3. Ask the CPA to fill or confirm the dates and basis first: placed-in-service date (B8), capitalized costs (B10), land method (B11), and building life (B20). Then the method choices: bonus rate or election (B21, B22), rental-use division (B36), and first-year convention (B35).
4. Ask the CPA to decide participation and limits: which Publication 925 test applies (B29), professional status (B30), MAGI (B31), and the usable share (B32). Bring your hour log.
5. Ask for the marginal federal and state rates the deduction would offset (B33, B34).
6. Rerun the Scenarios tab with the CPA's inputs. Save a dated copy. Record the CPA's name and the date on the Sources tab.
7. Decide on the study only after seeing the "added deduction from the study" line, the passive case, and the fee side by side. Keep the dated workbook with your tax records for the OP.12 exit conversation.

A short script for the meeting: "Here is my estimate and every assumption behind it. Which inputs are wrong, which apply to me, and what would you enter instead?"

### SR.3.10 Open items: unverified assumptions (Cedar Ridge base)

The Outputs tab lists every input whose status isn't "Verified". For the Cedar Ridge teaching example, that list is:

- Contract, closing, and placed-in-service dates (B6 to B8), which drive the bonus rate.
- Capitalized acquisition costs of $6,000 (B10).
- Land share of 20% and its method (B11, B12).
- Study fee of $4,500 and the reclass split of 15% / 0% / 7% (B15, B17 to B19).
- Building life of 39 years vs 27.5 years (B20).

- Whether personal days are counted correctly, and whether expenses are divided (B24, B36).
- Hours logged by the owners and by others; the participation test (B27 to B29).
- MAGI, usable share, and tax rates (B31 to B34).
- First-year convention (B35).

### SR.3.11 Who confirms what

**Confirm with:** your CPA the placed-in-service date, basis, land allocation method, and building recovery period. Also confirm bonus eligibility, any election, the first-year convention, and whether expenses are divided for personal use.

**Confirm with:** your CPA whether the activity is passive and which material participation test applies. Also confirm how much of the loss is usable this year, and which loss limits apply.

**Confirm with:** your CPA the marginal federal and state rates. Ask whether Form 3115 is needed for a study on property placed in service in an earlier year.

**Confirm with:** the study provider, in writing, the method, preparer qualifications, participant roles, reconciliation, asset schedule, and fee before you engage them.

**Confirm with:** your CPA how the depreciation pools on the Outputs tab would be treated at a sale or exchange (OP.12).
