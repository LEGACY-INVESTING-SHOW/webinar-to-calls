PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.12 / 19
Slide 2 · A DSCR loan is sized on the property; a conventional loan is sized on your household

A DSCR loan sizes the loan mostly on the property's income. DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio. It's the property's income divided by its loan payment. A ratio of 1.0 means the income just covers the payment. PA.6 covers how lenders calculate it.

A conventional loan sizes it mostly on you: your documented income, your existing debts, your credit, and your assets. The property still matters, but your household is the main borrower.

That one difference changes almost everything else. It changes which documents you gather and how long underwriting takes. It changes whether you can close in an LLC. And it changes what happens to your borrowing capacity for the next property.

Every line on this slide says "usually" or "often" for a reason. These are typical patterns, and they vary by lender. Confirm each one with the actual lender you're talking to.

PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.25 / 19
Slide 5 · A conventional lender weighs four things about you, under the loan buyer's rules

On a conventional loan, the lender underwrites your household. I'm using Fannie Mae's Selling Guide as the example because it's public. Lenders sell many loans to buyers like Fannie Mae, and the loan buyer sets the rules. So your loan may follow Fannie Mae, another loan buyer's guide, or the lender's own rules. Ask which one.

Four things carry most of the weight. First, debt-to-income: your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. Fannie Mae sets 36% for manual underwriting, where a person applies the rules by hand. It allows up to 45% with the right credit score and reserves, and 50% through its automated system, Desktop Underwriter.

Second, documented income. Third, occupancy type. Investment loans carry an extra price adjustment. A one-unit investment purchase can go up to 85% loan-to-value, so at least 15% down.

Fourth, reserves and your other properties. Maya and Chris would have two financed properties: their home and Cedar Ridge. Personal use is one of the things that separates a second home from an investment property. So don't guess the occupancy type from their 10 personal-use days. Tell the lender the real plan and let it classify.

Slide 7 · Fannie Mae added a short-term rental income section in September 2026

You'll hear people say conventional loans can't use Airbnb income. Don't repeat that. It's out of date.

On September 2, 2026, Fannie Mae published Announcement SEL-2026-08. It restructured the rental income chapter and added a section called B3-3.8-03, Rental Income from the Subject Property: Short-Term Rental. The announcement says it introduces new requirements for using STR income, including alternative documentation options.

On timing, as of September 2026: lenders are encouraged to adopt it immediately. They must apply it to loans with application dates on or after November 1, 2026. So for an application before November 1, ask your loan officer directly whether they've adopted it. From November 1 on, the question is whether your transaction fits.

I need to be straight with you. The exact conditions in the new section were not confirmed for this lesson. That includes eligibility, occupancy types, purchase or refinance, operating history, and documents. Those conditions decide whether Cedar Ridge qualifies. Get them from your lender in writing.

If the lender confirms that Cedar Ridge's transaction fits B3-3.8-03, it may count documented STR income under that section's calculation rules.

Comparing written quotes

Compare the same eight lines on each written quote, dated the same day

  • Interest rate, whether it's locked, and for how long
  • APR where shown (rate plus points and certain charges)
  • Points and lender credits, in dollars
  • Estimated cash to close
  • Monthly P&I and full monthly PITIA
  • Reserves required after closing, in dollars
  • Prepayment penalty, year by year
  • Refinance terms: wait period, max cash-out, STR income method
Term sheet or Loan Estimate

Rental credit you won't occupy is business-purpose credit (Reg Z, § 1026.3), so a lender may send a term sheet. Ask for every rate, fee, and penalty in the same layout.

PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.611 / 19
Slide 11 · Compare the same eight lines on each written quote, dated the same day

The CFPB's guide to comparing loan offers tells you to request several Loan Estimates and focus on the fees that vary by lender. It also warns that rates change daily. So get both quotes on the same day if you can.

Compare these eight lines. The APR matters because, as the CFPB explains, it includes the rate plus points and certain other charges. Estimated cash to close includes your down payment and closing costs, minus your deposit and any seller credits.

The last line is the one people forget: what the lender will require at refinance. How long you must own the property first, which lenders call seasoning. How much cash you can pull out on a cash-out refinance, where the new loan is larger and you take the difference. And how it will count STR income then.

One wrinkle. Federal rules treat credit to buy a rental you won't occupy as business-purpose credit. So a lender may not have to give you a Loan Estimate. Some will anyway. Others send a term sheet, a short summary of the rate, fees, and terms. Either way, get every rate, fee, and penalty in writing, in the same layout.

Slide 13 · First rental on W-2 income: get the conventional quote first and keep DSCR live

Here's the decision tree. Use it to decide which lender to call first. It orders your calls. The lender makes the actual eligibility decision.

Branch A is buying your first rental with documented W-2 income. That's Maya and Chris.

Step one: is your DTI, with the full new payment and no rental income, under the lender's limit with some room to spare? If yes, get a conventional quote first. If you're over the limit, or within a point or two of it, also follow Branch C.

Step two: will the lender classify it as an investment property? If yes, keep going. If not, ask what that means for rental income and occupancy rules.

Step three: can you close the way you need to on the conventional loan, in an LLC now if you need one? If yes, keep going. If no, get a DSCR quote.

Step four: is the conventional approval in writing? Until it is, keep a DSCR quote live as your backup. Deals stall in underwriting. A live backup means you don't lose the house when one does.

Decision tree · Branches B and C

A refinance and a thin-income file each follow their own branch of the tree

Branch B: refinance an STR you own

  • 12+ months of statements and a filed Schedule E? Ask if B3-3.8-03 or the lender's rules count it.
  • Price your current prepayment penalty in dollars first.
  • Does weak-case cash flow still meet the minimum you set for yourself after the refinance?
  • Compare conventional and DSCR on the same day, same amount.

Branch C: complex or thin income

Self-employed with short history, job changes, big write-offs, or DTI over or near the limit
  • Ask a conventional lender to pre-underwrite your income first.
  • If your income won't carry it, DSCR may fit. Plan the higher cost in PA.8.
  • Check weak-case cash flow on DSCR pricing. DSCR removes the income test, not the risk.
PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.814 / 19
Slide 14 · A refinance and a thin-income file each follow their own branch of the tree

Two more branches.

Branch B is a refinance of an STR you already own. Do you have 12 months or more of platform statements and a filed Schedule E? Ask the conventional lender whether B3-3.8-03 or its own rules would count that income. Does your current loan have a prepayment penalty? Price it in dollars before you compare anything. Then check the weak case. If the refinance pushes weak-case cash flow below the minimum you set for yourself, the refinance is making the property riskier. PA.8 walks through setting that rule. And compare both loan types on the same day for the same amount.

Branch C is for complex or thin income documentation. Self-employed with a short history, recent job changes, large write-offs, or DTI over or within a point or two of the limit. Don't assume you won't qualify. Ask a conventional lender to pre-underwrite your income first. If your income truly won't carry the payment, a DSCR loan may fit. Just remember: DSCR removes the income test. It doesn't remove the risk.

The conventional rules come from Fannie Mae's Selling Guide, because it's public. Your loan may follow a different guide. The DTI limits, occupancy types, the 85% investment LTV, and the reserve rules are all Fannie Mae sections, checked on September 25, 2026.
