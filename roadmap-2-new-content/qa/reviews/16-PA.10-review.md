# Review: PA.10 Entity & Tax Structure Setup: LLC, S-Corp or Holding Company

Reviewer stance: a first-time learner who has never hosted or bought a rental, opening only this lesson and its 20-slide deck. Files reviewed: `parts/16-PA.10.md`, `slides/src/16-PA.10.json`, `slides/16-PA.10.html` (screenshots of slides 1 to 20). I re-ran the arithmetic. $300 + $125 + $1,200 = $1,625. $5,572 - $1,625 = $3,947. $3,947 / 12 = $329 and $5,572 / 12 = $464. $5,172 - $1,625 = $3,547. 10% of 212 = 21.2. 212 / 222 = 95.5%. All of these hold. One number doesn't: the **$127 in the slide 15 title** can't be derived from anything in the lesson, and the idea behind it is also wrong (see Questions 1).

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The core idea is useful and lands fast: "put it in an LLC" and "run it through an S-corp" answer two different questions, and your lender may decide the third. After that the lesson turns into a wall of acronyms and form numbers (DSCR, Form 2553, Form 8832, 1065, Schedule C/E, BOI, FinCEN, Reg Z, 1026.3, Pub 925). Almost none of them are explained, and I couldn't tell which ones I need to act on and which are there for the record. Sections PA.10.5 and PA.10.6 (name map, then personal-use days and participation tests) felt like two other lessons dropped in. I lost the thread about why day counts belong in an entity lesson. The Cedar Ridge cost example is the most useful part, but slide 15's headline says Option B "leaves $127". The math on the same slide says it leaves them $1,453 *short* of their target. That made me distrust the numbers.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. "Option B's LLC costs about $1,625 a year, which leaves $127 on the PA.9 numbers" (slide 15 title). What's $127? The side text on the same slide says $5,172 - $1,625 = $3,547, "under their $5,000 minimum". That's $1,453 short, not $127 left over. The nearest number I can find is PA.9's $172 cushion above $5,000, and that's *before* the LLC. The title contradicts the slide body, the notes, and the Markdown.
2. "Neither answers the question your lender asks first." (PA.10.1). What *is* that question? The Markdown never says. Only slide 2's notes do ("which is who's borrowing").
3. "your lender often makes the first" (slide 1 title). The first what: the first choice, which is title? I had to read slide 2 to find out.
4. "Sam's PA.5 Cost Segregation & Year-One Depreciation" (tracker table; PA.10.6 twice; slide 10 notes). Who's Sam? What is cost segregation? Do I need PA.5 before this lesson?
5. "Your financing choice from PA.7" (PA.10.3). I haven't seen PA.7. What are the choices? The lesson uses "conventional" and "DSCR" but never says what a DSCR loan is.
6. "Kiavi says some DSCR lenders allow borrowing through a business entity" (PA.10.3, slide 5). Who's Kiavi? Why is one lender's web page the source for the whole market? The lesson admits it's "one lender's published page", so why lead with it on the slide?
7. "I couldn't confirm a lender's published guarantee terms" (PA.10.3, slide 5 notes). This reads like a researcher's note. As a learner, should I worry that guarantees are rare, or common?
8. "If a guarantee is required, the LLC does not separate you from the loan debt. It may still separate you from some other claims." (PA.10.3, slide 5). What "other claims"? A guest slipping on the stairs? One plain example drawn from what the lesson already says (liability protection is state law) would help, or say the attorney will give examples.
9. "Decide on the entity before closing" (PA.10.3, slide 6). How far before? The lender picks the borrower when you apply for the loan, not at closing. Maya and Chris already have a DSCR quote (PA.10.8). Is it too late for them? The flow on slide 6 starts at "Before closing" with no date.
10. "A transfer to your own LLC is not on that list." (PA.10.3). So what happens if I've already bought in my own name? Is the only answer "ask the lender"? Say so plainly.
11. "If anyone suggests putting the real estate itself inside an S corporation, ask the CPA to explain what happens later." (PA.10.2, slide 4 warning box). Why? The lesson raises the alarm but never says what the risk is, even in one clause. I'm left anxious and uninformed.
12. "In community property states, the IRS will respect spouses' treatment..." (PA.10.2, slide 4). Is Cedar Ridge in one? Option B says "Partnership (Form 1065) unless a community property exception applies". I can't tell which applies to Maya and Chris, because the lesson never names their state.
13. "Its activity then shows on the owner's own return, such as Schedule C or Schedule E" (PA.10.2). What's the difference? CPA question 3 asks "Schedule E or Schedule C, given the services we'll provide to guests?" and I don't know what services change the answer.
14. "an activity isn't a rental activity for the passive rules if the average customer stay is 7 days or less" (PA.10.6, slide 11). My Airbnb isn't a *rental activity*? What are "the passive rules"? Why is being "not a rental" a good or bad thing?
15. "It's the reason people talk about 'the STR loophole.'" (PA.10.6, slide 11). What is the STR loophole? The lesson names it and then says it's "really a set of tests" without saying what it lets you do. The next sentence ("Whether your losses can offset W-2 income") is the only hint.
16. "A beach house the owner will occupy over 14 days next year isn't treated as non-owner-occupied under that rule" (PA.10.6, slide 11). Double negative. What does this mean for Maya and Chris at 10 days? Is their loan a business loan or not? The lesson never applies it to the example.
17. "Here that's roughly 212 of 222 total days, or 95.5% rental." (PA.10.6, slide 10). Where does 222 come from? I worked out it's 212 rented + 10 personal. What about the empty nights? They aren't mentioned.
18. "10% of fair-rental days" (PA.10.6, slide 10). Are "fair-rental days" the same as "rented nights"? The slide treats them as equal without saying so.
19. "Against the canonical DSCR base-case cash flow of $5,572" and "On PA.9's final budget, the base case is $5,172" (PA.10.8, slide 15). Why are there two base cases? What changed between them? What does "canonical" mean?
20. "under the $5,000 minimum they set in PA.8" (PA.10.8, slide 15). What's this minimum? Is it a lender rule or their own rule? Does falling under it mean they can't buy?
21. "Extra annual cost (assumption) | $0 | See below | Similar to Option B, plus drafting the agreement" (PA.10.8 table). Does the $1,625 include forming the LLC, or is formation a separate one-time cost? Option A's $0: is an umbrella policy free? The "Liability approach" row says Option A uses one.
22. "Operating LLC under a written management or lease agreement" (Option C). A lease between me and my own company? Why would I do that? What does "whether the arrangement holds up" mean? Holds up against what, and to whom?
23. "Bring it to all four meetings" (PA.10.9). Which four? Slide 18 lists five professionals. Part 2 of the worksheet lists five, including the licensing office. The closing rule adds a sixth party, the title company.
24. "CPA | The eight questions in PA.10.6" (worksheet Part 2, slide 16). One "Written answer" cell for eight questions? Where do I write the answers?
25. "Email your insurer the four named-insured questions" (slide 19). The worksheet has only two insurer rows, and they aren't the same as the four questions on slide 7. Which list do I send?
26. "FinCEN also reports that a court vacated its residential real estate reporting rule in March 2026." (PA.10.5, slide 9 notes). What was that rule? Why does a first-time buyer need to know it was vacated?
27. "You'll leave with a filled decision worksheet" (PA.10.0, slide 1 notes). How can I fill Part 3 (decision, "Confirmed by") before any professional has answered? At the end of the lesson I can only fill Part 1.
28. "If they don't, don't close until they do." (PA.10.9, slide 17). What if my purchase contract has a closing date? Is delaying closing risky too? The rule is absolute and gives no next step. For example, "call your attorney and agent the same day".

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- "An LLC's default federal tax treatment depends on how many members it has" (slide 4 title). Two of the four rows aren't about member count (spouses in community property states; filing Form 2553). The title promises one rule and the table shows four.
- "To become an S corporation, the LLC files Form 2553. Publication 3402 says that filing Form 2553 also counts as electing corporate classification, so a separate Form 8832 isn't needed first" (PA.10.2, slide 3 bullet 3, slide 4 row 4). A beginner doesn't know what "corporate classification" is or what Form 8832 does, so this sentence answers a question they never asked. It also appears three times (Markdown, slide 3, slide 4).
- "Map every closing document to one name" (slide 8 title). The table shows *different* names on different documents (borrower, title owner, individual guarantors, lender). "One name" contradicts the table. The point is "the *right* name, consistently".
- "If two rows disagree, fix it before closing" (slide 8 notes). The rows are supposed to differ (the guaranty is in personal names, the deed may be in the LLC's). It's unclear what counts as "disagreeing".
- "Walk down this column with your PA.10 worksheet open" (slide 8 notes). The worksheet has no name-map section, so there's nothing to compare against.
- "The operator's name goes on the platform, permit, and vendor accounts" (slide 9 title). The table right below says the permit goes to "Whoever the local office requires". The title overclaims.
- "Ten personal days sits under both personal-use thresholds" (slide 10 title; PA.10.6 "under both thresholds"). The rule is one threshold: the greater of 14 days or 10%. "Both" makes it sound like two tests to pass. The calc lines "10% of fair-rental days: 21.2" and "Threshold: 21.2" repeat the same number.
- "ALL FIGURES ON THIS SLIDE ARE ASSUMPTIONS" (slides 10 and 14). On slide 10, "14 days" and "10%" are IRS rules, not assumptions. Slide 14 has no figures.
- "A 3.5-night average stay changes which tests the CPA applies, and no entity does the hours" (slide 11 title). "No entity does the hours" is cryptic until you've read the notes. It means an LLC can't log participation hours for you.
- "Same days, different offices" (slide 11 right column). This is a lending-disclosure rule inside a tax section inside an entity lesson. It breaks the thread three ways and is never tied back to Maya and Chris.
- "Cedar Ridge has three workable structures" (slide 14 title). The Markdown says "None of these is my recommendation", Option B works "only if the lender lends to LLCs", and Option C's open question is "whether the arrangement holds up". "Workable" overstates it.
- "Personal use, participation, and exit: questions for the CPA" (PA.10.6 heading). "Exit" gets one CPA question and a pointer to OP.12. The heading promises more than the section gives.
- "That lesson assumes the CPA has already settled several facts about your situation. Here's what drives those facts, and why the entity choice doesn't change most of them." (PA.10.6 opening). If the entity choice doesn't change them, why is this section here? The lesson should say so directly: "these facts go to your CPA whichever structure you pick".
- "Bring back from Sam's PA.5 the depreciation figures." (slide 10 notes). The sentence is inverted and hard to say aloud.
- Sources list: "Checked 2026-09-25 via web search summary (direct fetch blocked)" (PA.10 sources, every line). A learner reading this wonders whether anyone opened the pages. Slide 20's title says "checked 2026-09-25" with no caveat.
