# Review: 3.4 Red Flags: Markets, Cities & Leases to Avoid in 2026

Reviewer stance: a first-time learner with no hosting or investing background who has seen no other lesson. Files reviewed: `parts/04-3.4.md`, `slides/src/04-3.4.json` (19 slides + notes), `slides/04-3.4.html` (screenshots of all 19 slides). All Alder Street figures were re-run with `tools/examples.py`. The rent-up figures quoted below (net $818/month, payback 14.7 months) come from that script.

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The title promised me a list of cities to avoid. The first real paragraph says there won't be one, and I felt a little baited until the reason sank in. The two-stage idea (kill switches first, then a points score) is clear and I could repeat it back after slide 2. I got lost at the scoring. "Points" means two different things. The exercise tells me to fill in a scorecard "before you contact the landlord", but half the scorecard needs the landlord's lease. And the worked example ends at "a thin advance" without noticing that its own city answers are still "pending", which the rules say caps it at investigate. I also hit a wall of undefined shorthand (arbitrage, STR, ADR, comps, stress case, "Sam's 4.2") that the lesson assumes I already know.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. 3.4.0 and slide 1 notes: "It takes about an hour of desk work." How is that possible if it includes emailing the licensing office, pulling a year of comps (Sam's 4.3) and running a supply screen (Sam's 4.2)? Is the hour just the first pass?
2. 3.4.1 / slide 2: "Rules attach to an exact address, building, zoning district, permit category, and signed lease." What are a zoning district and a permit category? This is the key line on slide 2 and I can't unpack it.
3. H1 (3.4.2): "An arbitrage unit is almost always unhosted." What is an arbitrage unit? The word is never defined in this lesson, and the whole lesson is built around it.
4. 3.4.3 / slide 5: "New York State counts the check-in date as day zero, so a 29-night rental is still short-term." A 29-night stay is under 30 days anyway, so why do I need the day-zero rule? What mistake is this warning me against?
5. 3.4.3 / slide 5: "In Los Angeles, the same operator runs into H2 and H3." Why H3? I'd apply for my own registration. Why does it matter that someone else's can't transfer to me? And in 3.4.7 Version 3, only H2 (plus the RSO point) is cited. Which is it?
6. 3.4.3: RSO units "home-sharing isn't allowed". Which hard stop is that: H1 (use prohibited at the address) or H2?
7. C2 (3.4.4): "Run the same deal with lower nightly rates and lower occupancy." How much lower? For my own address, what stress case do I use? I only find out in 3.4.7 that the course uses "ADR 12% lower, occupancy 60%".
8. C1 table (3.4.6) / slide 8: "15 points or more". Is that 15 scorecard points? The slide 8 title says "starting with a 15-point cushion" and I read it as 15 scorecard points. I only understood it was percentage points of occupancy from the slide 8 speaker notes.
9. C4 table: "Three or more demand sources" scores 0, and "One source drives most stays" scores 1. What if I have two sources, neither dominant? Nothing covers it.
10. C4 table: what separates "One source drives most stays" (1) from "One event or season drives most revenue" (2)? Is a hospital a "source" and a football season an "event or season"?
11. 3.4.6: "Use them as written so two people scoring the same address get the same answer." Then: "Tighten them if the unit depends on one employer or one event." Alder Street depends on one employer. Did you tighten? How? Doesn't tightening break the "same answer" promise?
12. Labels (3.4.6 / slide 9): what if my total is 3 but C2 scores 2 (the stress case loses money every month)? The rules say advance. Is that intended?
13. Labels: if I score 11 and also have an L row at 2, is it reject or investigate? The order of the rules isn't stated.
14. 3.4.8: "Fill in the scorecard below before you contact the landlord." How can I answer H5 ("Does the lease lack explicit written permission, with no addendum offered?") or score L1 to L5 before I have a lease draft from the landlord?

16. 3.4.7 Version 1: "H1 to H6 all answer 'no,' provided the licensing office confirms H1 to H3 in writing." Until they confirm, aren't those "unknown"? The rule says unknown caps the label at investigate. So how does the fixed version reach "advance" at 4 points?

18. 3.4.7 C7: "loses about $788 after all costs". How was $788 computed? The revenue side is shown ($2,600) and the cost side isn't.
19. 3.4.7 rent what-if: "Under the stress case, annual net becomes -$852". How did $1,548 become -$852? And what is the new total score? "The deal starts to look like a reject" doesn't tell me whether it is one.
20. 3.4.7 rent what-if: does anything besides C2 change at $2,150? (Checking `tools/examples.py`: base monthly net falls to $818, so C6 payback becomes $12,050 ÷ $818 = 14.7 months. That scores 1, not 0. The lesson never mentions it.)
21. H6 / exercise: "Has every insurer I asked declined STR liability coverage?" How many insurers must I ask? If I asked none, is that "unknown"?
22. L4: "Minor item verbal only" (1) vs "Key item verbal only" (2). Is "I'll renew you" key and "parking comes with it" minor? Both examples are used together on slide 7 with no ranking.
23. L1: what does "Cause defined loosely" (1 point) look like in a real clause?
24. Who is Sam? "Sam's 3.2", "Sam's 4.2 and 4.3". Sam is never introduced.
25. "Lesson 1.3 built the math." What math? Which parts of it do I need for this lesson?
26. 3.4.8 exercise column "Official source URL". What official URL do I put for H4 (HOA documents), H5 (a lease) or H6 (an insurer's email)?
27. Slide 17: "Five people confirm..." The fifth row is "Sam's 4.2 and 4.3 methods (or your own data)", which isn't a person. Also, 3.4.2 says "The landlord and your attorney confirm H5", but the landlord isn't on the slide 17 list. Why not?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"points" means two things.** C1 is scored in "15 points or more / 8 to 14.9 points / Under 8 points" (percentage points of occupancy). The scorecard itself totals "points" (0, 1, 2 per row). Slide 8's title, "starting with a 15-point cushion", sits right next to a table whose label bands are "0 to 4 points" and "10 points or more". I read "15-point" as 15 scorecard points. "20.1 points" (slide 10 title) and "2.9 points" add to the confusion.
- **The exercise contradicts itself on ordering.** "Fill in the scorecard below before you contact the landlord" (3.4.8, slide 16 keyline) vs "Read the lease draft for L1 to L5" (slide 18) vs "Bring your scorecard and the landlord's draft lease" (3.4.10). H5 and L1 to L5 can't be answered without landlord contact.
- **The worked example breaks its own rule.** Stage one is "pending written confirmation of H1 to H3" (slide 15). By 3.4.6, "Any hard stop marked 'unknown' means the label can't be better than investigate." Yet slide 13's title promises "a thin advance at 4", and written confirmation isn't on the to-do list.
- **"The score climbs and the deal starts to look like a reject."** (3.4.7, slide 14.) This is vague for a lesson that sells fixed rules. The learner can't tell whether the starting point is the 9-point draft or the fixed 4-point version. Computed from the canonical model: from 9, C2 +1 and C6 +1 gives 11, which is **reject**. From the fixed 4, the same changes give 6, which is **investigate**. Slide 14 notes ("moves the deal from a thin advance toward a reject") overstate the fixed-version case.
- **Slide 14 has no bridge.** It lists "Rent per month $2,150" and then "Stress-case net per year -$852" with no operator between them. The missing step is simple: $1,548 minus $2,400 of extra rent (12 × $200) = -$852.
- **"Tighten them if the unit depends on one employer or one event."** (3.4.6) There's no instruction on how. It contradicts "use them as written", and the worked example (one employer) doesn't do it.
- **"Without a short-term-rental liability policy for this unit and use, you can't meet any sensible lease or addendum."** (H6) This asserts that every sensible lease demands such a policy. Nothing in this lesson supports that.
- **"New York State counts the check-in date as day zero, so a 29-night rental is still short-term."** The "so" doesn't follow for a reader. 29 nights is already under 30.
- **LA fails "H2 and H3" (3.4.3, slide 5) vs "H2 fails" (3.4.7 V3, slide 15).** The same example is described two ways. H3 (non-transferable registration) only bites if you planned to use someone else's registration, and the example never says that.
- **C6 vs L5.** Both score setup payback: C6 in absolute months, L5 against the lease term. The lesson never says why payback counts twice, so it looks like double counting.
- **H5 and H6 exercise questions are double negatives:** "Does the lease lack explicit written permission, with no addendum offered?" I had to read it three times to work out that "yes" is the bad answer.
- **The title promises what the lesson refuses.** "Markets, Cities & Leases to Avoid" vs "I'm not going to hand you a list of cities to avoid." The reframe comes at the end of 3.4.1. It should come first.
