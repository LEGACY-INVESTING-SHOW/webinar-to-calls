PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.12 / 19
Slide 2 · A DSCR loan is sized on the property; a conventional loan is sized on your household

A DSCR loan sizes the loan mostly on the property's income. We covered how in PA.6. A conventional loan sizes it mostly on you: your documented income, your existing debts, your credit, and your assets. The property still matters, but your household is the main borrower.

That one difference changes almost everything else. It changes which documents you gather and how long underwriting takes. It changes whether you can close in an LLC. And it changes what happens to your borrowing capacity for the next property.

Every line on this slide says "usually" or "often" for a reason. These are typical patterns, and they vary by lender. Confirm each one with the actual lender you're talking to. We'll come back to this comparison in more detail later in the lesson.

PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.25 / 19
Slide 5 · A conventional lender weighs four things about your household, using its investor's guide

On a conventional loan, the lender underwrites your household. I'm using Fannie Mae's Selling Guide as the example because it's public. Your loan may follow Fannie Mae, another investor's guide, or the lender's own rules. Ask which one.

Four things carry most of the weight. First, debt-to-income: your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. Fannie Mae sets 36% for manual underwriting, up to 45% with the right credit score and reserves, and 50% through its automated system, Desktop Underwriter.

Second, documented income. Third, occupancy type. Investment loans carry an extra price adjustment, which is one reason investment rates run higher. And a one-unit investment purchase can go up to 85% LTV, so at least 15% down.

Fourth, reserves and your other properties. Maya and Chris would have two financed properties: their home and Cedar Ridge. Don't guess the occupancy type from their 10 personal-use days. Tell the lender the real plan and let it classify.

Slide 7 · Fannie Mae added a short-term rental income section in September 2026

You'll hear people say conventional loans can't use Airbnb income. Don't repeat that. It's out of date.

On September 2, 2026, Fannie Mae published Announcement SEL-2026-08. It restructured the rental income chapter and added a section called B3-3.8-03, Rental Income from the Subject Property: Short-Term Rental. The announcement says it introduces new requirements for using STR income, including alternative documentation options.

On timing: lenders are encouraged to adopt it immediately, and must apply it to loans with application dates on or after November 1, 2026. So for an application before November 1, ask your loan officer directly whether they've adopted it.

I need to be straight with you about what I couldn't confirm. The full section wasn't readable from our research environment. So I can't tell you the exact eligibility conditions. Those conditions decide whether Cedar Ridge qualifies. Treat anything more specific you hear as unconfirmed until your lender shows you the text.

Since I can't give you the exact conditions, here's the conditional version.

If the lender confirms that Cedar Ridge's transaction fits B3-3.8-03, it may count documented STR income under that section's calculation rules.

Comparing written quotes

Compare the same eight lines on each written quote, dated the same day

  • Interest rate, whether it's locked, and for how long
  • APR where shown (rate plus points and certain charges)
  • Points and lender credits, in dollars
  • Estimated cash to close
  • Monthly P&I and full monthly PITIA
  • Reserves required after closing, in dollars
  • Prepayment penalty, year by year
  • Refinance terms: seasoning, max cash-out LTV, STR income method
Term sheet or Loan Estimate

Rental credit you won't occupy is business-purpose credit (Reg Z, § 1026.3), so a lender may send a term sheet. Ask for every rate, fee, and penalty in the same layout.

PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.611 / 19
Slide 11 · Compare the same eight lines on each written quote, dated the same day

The CFPB's guide to comparing loan offers tells you to request several Loan Estimates and focus on the fees that vary by lender. It also warns that rates change daily. So get both quotes on the same day if you can.

Compare these eight lines. The APR matters because, as the CFPB explains, it includes the rate plus points and certain other charges. That helps you compare loans with different rates and fees. Estimated cash to close includes your down payment and closing costs, minus your deposit and any seller credits.

The last line is the one people forget: what the lender will require at refinance. Seasoning period, maximum cash-out LTV, and how it will count STR income then.

One wrinkle. Federal rules treat credit to buy a rental you won't occupy as business-purpose credit. So a lender may not have to give you a Loan Estimate. Some will anyway. Others send a term sheet. Either way, get every rate, fee, and penalty in writing, in the same layout.

Slide 13 · On a first purchase with W-2 income, get the conventional quote first and keep DSCR live

Here's the decision tree. Use it to decide which lender to call first. It orders your calls. The lender makes the actual eligibility decision.

Branch A is a first purchase with documented W-2 income. That's Maya and Chris.

Step one: is your DTI, with the full new payment and no rental income, under the lender's limit? If yes, get a conventional quote first. If you're near the limit, you're really on Branch C.

Step two: will the lender classify it as an investment property? If not, ask what that means for rental income and occupancy rules.

Step three: do you need to close in an LLC now? If yes, and the conventional lender won't allow it, get a DSCR quote.

Step four: get a DSCR quote anyway as a backup. Keep it live until the conventional approval is in writing. Deals stall in underwriting. A live backup means you don't lose the house when one does.

Decision tree · Branches B and C

A refinance and a thin-income file each follow their own branch of the tree

Branch B: refinance an STR you own

  • 12+ months of statements and a filed Schedule E? Ask if B3-3.8-03 or the lender's rules count it.
  • Price your current prepayment penalty in dollars first.
  • Does weak-case cash flow still meet your rule after the refinance?
  • Compare conventional and DSCR on the same day, same amount.

Branch C: complex or thin income

Self-employed with short history, job changes, big write-offs, or DTI near the limit
  • Ask a conventional lender to pre-underwrite your income first.
  • If your income won't carry it, DSCR may fit. Plan the higher cost in PA.8.
  • Check weak-case cash flow on DSCR pricing. DSCR removes the income test, not the risk.
PA.7 · DSCR vs. Conventional Financing§ PA.7.814 / 19
Slide 14 · A refinance and a thin-income file each follow their own branch of the tree

Two more branches.

Branch B is a refinance of an STR you already own. Do you have 12 months or more of platform statements and a filed Schedule E? Ask the conventional lender whether B3-3.8-03 or its own rules would count that income. Does your current loan have a prepayment penalty? Price it in dollars before you compare anything. Then check the weak case. If the refinance pushes weak-case cash flow below your rule, the refinance is making the property riskier. And compare both loan types on the same day for the same amount.

Branch C is for complex or thin income documentation. Self-employed with a short history, recent job changes, large write-offs, or DTI already near the limit. Don't assume you won't qualify. Ask a conventional lender to pre-underwrite your income first. If your income truly won't carry the payment, a DSCR loan may fit. Just remember: DSCR removes the income test. It doesn't remove the risk.
