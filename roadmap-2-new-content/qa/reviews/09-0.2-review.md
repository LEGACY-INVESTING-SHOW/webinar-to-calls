# Review: 0.2 Why Ownership Wins: Cash Flow + Tax Savings + Equity

Reviewer stance: a first-time learner who has never hosted a short-term rental or bought an investment property, and who opened only this lesson and its 19-slide deck. Files reviewed: `parts/09-0.2.md`, `slides/src/09-0.2.json`, `slides/09-0.2.html` (screenshots of slides 1 to 19). I re-ran the arithmetic and it all holds, including the $6,256, $44,500, $85,531 and five-year totals. I checked the less obvious figures against `reference/recurring-examples.md` (B1 to B8). The problems are about introductions, ordering, jargon, and a few statements that clash with each other. None of the totals is wrong.

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The central idea is strong and easy to remember: four streams, and only one of them is money you can spend. The Markdown starts calculating with "For Cedar Ridge, the canonical base case" and never tells me what Cedar Ridge is, who Maya and Chris are, what a "DSCR quote" is, or where the $142,688 of "cash in" comes from. The deck only explains these in slide 1's speaker notes. The tax section (0.2.5, slides 6 to 9) is where I got lost. It is the longest part of the lesson, it stacks eight IRS concepts in a row, and the $34,263 "could" figure is so much bigger than everything else that it pulls attention away from the lesson's real message. The five-year test (0.2.8, slide 14) is the payoff and it lands well. But the lesson never applies its own three-part decision rule to Cedar Ridge, so I finished without knowing whether Cedar Ridge "wins".

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Characters and the example appear without introduction.** "For Cedar Ridge..." (0.2.2) and "Maya and Chris have $185,000..." (0.2.5) arrive with no setup. The Markdown is the self-study version, so it needs the two-sentence intro that slide 1's notes already have.
- **"Tax timing" vs. "tax savings".** The title promises "Tax Savings". 0.2.1 renames it "Tax timing", and the reason ("partly taken back when you sell") isn't explained until the last bolded paragraph of 0.2.5, about 1,000 words later. Say why it's called "timing" when you name it.
- **"Principal paydown also depends on the house holding its value"** (0.2.3). Paydown happens whatever the value. Equity is what depends on value. The deck's version (slide 4, last bullet) is correct: "If the value falls, you can pay down the loan and still have less equity than you started with."
- **"That part is not an expense in the economic sense"** (0.2.3). "In the economic sense" is a phrase that makes a beginner feel there's an accounting sense in which it *is* an expense, and nothing explains it.
- **Full-year depreciation right after "a late-year launch gives a small first-year deduction"** (0.2.5; slide 6 vs. slide 7). These two statements sit next to each other in the deck and seem to contradict each other.
- **The passive rules paragraph uses stacked negatives.** "An activity is not a 'rental activity' ... That exception does not make the losses non-passive by itself." I read it three times. A plainer version: "If the average stay is 7 days or less, the IRS doesn't treat it as a rental. That sounds good, but it only opens the door. The loss can offset your W-2 income only if you also materially participate."
- **"Read the word 'could' carefully. That result depends on the next three facts."** (0.2.5). What follows is four or five bolded items (passive rules, the $25,000 allowance, personal use, "timing, not a gift"), and the deck says "the next two slides". The count doesn't match either way.
- **Slide 8 is titled "Three tax tests decide whether the loss can offset your other income"**, but gate 3 (personal use) isn't described anywhere as a test for offsetting other income. Its "no" outcome ("Treated as a home; split expenses by days") doesn't say what that means for usability.
- **Slide 9's right column mixes two topics.** The heading is "The $25,000 allowance phases out", but the third bullet is about recapture ("Depreciation lowers your basis...").
- **Lease table row labels combine two things with a slash.** "Property tax and building insurance / renter's liability policy" and "Principal and interest / rent" (0.2.6, slide 10). The lease "Repairs and capital reserve" cell (−$1,200) is actually "minor repairs", not a reserve.
- **The 18% co-host row appears in the own-vs-lease table (0.2.6, slide 11) before the co-host case is introduced (0.2.8).** I saw "−$7,798" and had no idea what it was.
- **"$34,263" is a different kind of number from the others in the 0.2.7 table.** The other rows are dollars of return. This one is estimated tax saved on a deduction that might be suspended. It is also three times bigger than the next-largest row, so it's the first number my eye goes to on slide 12.
- **"The owners manage with local help"** (0.2.8) clashes with Case A's label "self-managed". I couldn't tell whether "local help" means the cleaner and handyman (already in the costs) or something else.
- **0.2.8 closing: "Change any one of those and it loses over five years before tax."** But the decision rule (0.2.9) allows the weak case to be behind "by no more than you could accept losing". So is losing in the weak case a fail or not? The two sections point different ways.
- **Slide 2 uses arrows between the four streams.** Arrows say "this leads to that". The streams run in parallel, not in sequence.
