# Review: OP.6 Furnishing & Interior Optimization for an Owned Property

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The big idea lands fast and it's practical: decide who the guest is, set the bed layout, buy safety first, and let style take what's left. The Cedar Ridge example is concrete, and the linen math is easy to follow. Then the example breaks its own rule. The lesson says "cut layer 4 before layers 1-3", but the $2,760 in cuts includes bed frames (layer 2) and delivery (layer 5). And the final plan is shown room by room, so I can never see Cedar Ridge's five layer totals, which is exactly what the exercise asks me to fill in. I also kept meeting people and codes I don't know (Sam, Maya and Chris, PA.4, PA.9, B1, B6, DSCR, OP.10), and a replacement table whose costs I can't trace back to the plan I just read.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. OP.6.0: "a room inventory Sam can use in OP.1". Who is Sam? Who is Preston (slide 1)? Neither is ever introduced.
2. OP.6.0: "a safety list your insurer can read". What does "can read" mean? Do I send the list to the insurer? Is it something they approve?
3. OP.6.1: "Read that last part in reviews of the comparable set from Sam's PA.4 package." What is the comparable set? What's in the PA.4 package? If I skipped PA.4, how do I choose comparables?
4. OP.6.2 / slide 3: "a bunk bed counts as 2 beds in the bed count shown on the listing. So six beds can mean six people or twelve, depending on bed types." How does the bunk rule lead to "six or twelve"? If a bunk counts as 2 beds, the bed count for bunks roughly equals the number of sleepers. The six-or-twelve spread comes from twins versus queens, not from bunks.
5. OP.6.2: "local occupancy limits and any permit condition on guest count, which PA.9 should have captured." What is PA.9? What do I do if I never did it?
6. OP.6.2: "egress: every sleeping room needs a fire exit". What is "egress"? Does a window count? (The lesson rightly defers to the official, but it should say in plain words what they will look at.)
7. OP.6.3: "write the result in the handoff packet". What handoff packet? (The tracker says PA.12, but the body never does.)
8. OP.6.6: "Maya and Chris set $32,000 for Cedar Ridge, and the financing table counts it in total cash in ($142,688 on the DSCR quote)." This is the first time Maya, Chris, and Cedar Ridge appear in the lesson. What is a DSCR quote? What is "total cash in"? Do I need that number for anything in this lesson?
9. OP.6.6, layer list: which layer do bedroom dressers, nightstands, bed frames, bedroom lamps, and bedroom rugs belong to? Layer 2 is "Beds and mattresses". Layer 4 is "Kitchen, dining, living, work space, outdoor", with no bedrooms.
10. OP.6.6, item 5: contingency sits inside layer 5 here. The exercise in OP.6.9 and slide 12 show it as its own row. Which is it?
11. OP.6.6: "Towels follow the guest count, not the bed count: three sets per guest." What is in one "towel set"? What is a "lake towel", and why 16 (OP.6.7)?
12. OP.6.6: "B6 treats the full $32,000 as 5-year property for its depreciation illustration." What is B6? What does "5-year property" mean for me? Is this a tax deduction?
13. OP.6.7 cuts table: the king bed frame ($200) and queen bed frame ($150) were cut. Those are "beds", so layer 2. Delivery and assembly ($400) is layer 5. Doesn't that break the rule "If the draft is over budget, cut layer 4 before layers 1-3"?
14. OP.6.7: "The first draft of the room-by-room plan came to $33,565". Is the first draft shown anywhere? I only see the cuts and the final plan.
15. OP.6.7 safety line: "3 extinguishers". CPSC says "At least one". Why three, and where do they go? The room inventory hints at one by the deck.
16. OP.6.7 replacement table: where do "$4,375", "$3,500", "$1,200", and "$14,110" come from? The linens line was $4,005, not $4,375. The mattresses are never priced except the bunk-room twins ($1,200). I can't check this table against the plan.
17. OP.6.7: "That $5,982 a year is about $98 per stay, or 8.1% of the $74,275 base-case gross." What is "base-case gross"? Where does $74,275 come from?
18. OP.6.7: "B1 carries repairs at 5% ($3,714) and a capital reserve at 5%". What is B1? What is a capital reserve? And is 81% a problem or not? The lesson hands the question to OP.10 and never tells me whether Cedar Ridge is short.
19. OP.6.7: "Maya and Chris earmark part of the monthly repairs line for linens and smallwares." How much? "Part" is not something I can copy.
20. OP.6.7 schedule: "Before closing: Order long-lead items". What happens if closing slips or falls through? Am I stuck with a sectional and four mattresses?
21. OP.6.7 schedule: safety is "the first line in the budget", yet alarms and extinguishers go in on Day 10-12, after the living room furniture. Why so late?
22. OP.6.7: "Maya and Chris plan about 10 personal-use days a year." Why does this matter here? (It turns out to explain the owner closet many paragraphs later.)
23. OP.6.8: "Safety equipment in place and visible where required". Required by whom? What does "visible" mean for an alarm?
24. OP.6.9 Part B: "total must equal your underwriting budget". If I didn't do underwriting in an earlier lesson, where does my number come from?
25. OP.6.9 Part C: "If the replacement pace exceeds your repairs line, flag it for OP.10." How do I work out my replacement pace? The exercise has no row for it.
26. Slide 19 notes: "Four actions this week. Each one takes under an hour." Calling a licensing office and getting a fire marshal's answer in under an hour seems optimistic.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **The cut rule and the example disagree (OP.6.6, OP.6.7, OP.6.9 Part C; slides 8, 10, 17).** The rule is "cut layer 4 before layers 1-3". Cedar Ridge cut $350 of bed frames (layer 2 by the list's own wording) and $400 of delivery and assembly (layer 5). The lesson dodges this by saying "Safety, mattresses, and linens stayed untouched", which isn't the same as "layers 1-3". Bedroom furniture has no layer at all. A learner applying the rule literally won't cut bed frames or dressers, and won't know where dressers go.
- **Slide 8 title: "cut from the top layer only".** On the slide, the "top" (first) layer is Layer 1, the one marked "Nothing gets cut here". The title says the opposite of what's meant.
- **The final plan is room by room, but the exercise is layer by layer.** I'm asked to fill in five layer totals, but the only worked example never shows five layer totals. I can't copy the example into the exercise.
- **Slide 12's column is headed "Layer" but the rows aren't the five layers.** "Linens and towels" is layer 3, "Safety and access" is layer 1, and "Owner storage and spares" plus "Launch logistics" are both layer 5.
- **The replacement table uses different groups from the plan.** "Case goods, upholstery, rugs, lighting, decor $14,110" and "Mattresses $3,500" are regroupings I can't reconcile. "$4,375" doesn't match the $4,005 linen total until you guess it includes encasements.
- **"Six beds can mean six people or twelve" (OP.6.2, slide 3).** Placed right after the bunk rule, it suggests bunks cause the ambiguity. They don't; bed size does. And slide 9 then shows "6 beds, sleeps 8", which is neither six nor twelve.
- **Slide 9 total row: "6 sleeping surfaces" next to "Beds shown on listing: 6".** Two columns with the same total make me wonder what the difference is. "Sleeping surfaces" is a new term that appears only here.
- **Replacement pace versus reserves (OP.6.7).** "Furnishing replacement alone would use about 81% of both, before the roof or furnace. Bring that question to Sam's OP.10 review." I'm left not knowing whether Cedar Ridge is fine or underfunded.
- **Slide 20 title: "Every rule in this lesson traces to Airbnb's help pages or CPSC guidance."** Not true. The five layers, 3 sets per bed, 3 towel sets per guest, the 3% contingency, the replacement lives, and the $200 approval limit are the course's own planning assumptions.
- **Slide 5 shows only CPSC consumer guidance.** The Airbnb requirement (state whether alarms are present, keep them working, test them) is the platform rule. It's only in the notes, so a viewer sees the "optional" guidance and not the platform requirement.
- **The $200 approval limit.** It's set in one sentence in OP.6.7 and never shown on a slide. Slide 19 then says "the $200 replacement limit" as if the viewer already saw it.
- **OP.6.6: "In year one, that's mostly glasses, towels, and the odd lamp. The worked example shows how to fund it."** This forward reference points to a paragraph that sits after the linen cycle, the replacement table, and the reserve comparison, so it's hard to find.
