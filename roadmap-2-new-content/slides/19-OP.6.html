Buy for the guest and safety first, style last

Slide 1 · Buy for the guest and safety first, style last

This lesson turns a furnishing budget into a purchase plan for one house. You'll leave with a guest profile, a sleeping layout the listing can state truthfully, and a safety list you can show your insurer and fire marshal. You'll also get a five-layer budget, a setup schedule, and a room inventory for Sam's OP.1 listing lesson.

We'll use Cedar Ridge the whole way through. Maya and Chris are buying it. It's the lake house in the course example. They set a $32,000 furnishing budget in the underwriting. My job is to show you how they spend it well, in the right order, without finding more money. The Cedar Ridge figures in this lesson are assumptions.

Write down who books before you shop

  • Who comes
  • How many sleep over
  • What they do all day
  • What they complain about nearby
Slide 2 · Write down who books before you shop

Most furnishing mistakes start in a store. Someone falls for a sofa, then makes the house fit the sofa. Reverse that. Write down who books this house, then buy for that person.

The profile has four parts. The fourth comes from reviews of similar nearby listings, the ones in the underwriting package Sam walks through in PA.4. Those reviews tell you what guests in this market already dislike.

Then write what the house is not for. No parties means no sound system. If the lower level is stairs only, don't imply step-free access. Airbnb's ground rules say the listing should describe the home as it is from check-in to checkout. So every claim has to be true on arrival day.

Set the sleeping layout first, and count it by bed

  • Airbnb counts a bunk as 2 beds
  • List bed types next to the guest count
  • Say so plainly if you need a sofa bed
  • Confirm occupancy, egress, and insurance before ordering
Slide 3 · Set the sleeping layout first, and count it by bed

Guests check the sleeping layout twice, because it decides whether their group fits. It also sets your linen count and how long every turnover takes. So I set it before any purchase, and I count by bed.

Airbnb counts a bunk bed as 2 beds on the listing. Six twins sleep six people. Six queens could sleep twelve. So list the bed types and the guest count together.

Before you lock the layout, run three checks. The licensing office confirms maximum occupancy and any permit condition on guest count, which should already be in your PA.9 notes. Your building or fire official confirms each bedroom has an accepted way out in a fire. And your insurer confirms the guest count, because a mismatch with the listing can become a claim problem.

Work space, storage, and outdoor gear show up in reviews

  • Test the internet speed at the desk
  • Give guests room for a suitcase
  • Lock one closet for the owner
  • Only list outdoor gear you can maintain
Slide 4 · Work space, storage, and outdoor gear show up in reviews

Three areas get skipped in most furnishing budgets, then show up in reviews.

The first is the work space. If you claim a dedicated workspace, a guest expects a desk, a chair, a lamp, and outlets in reach. Test the internet at the desk itself and write the result in your PA.12 handoff packet. If it can't hold a video call, don't advertise remote work.

The second is storage. Guests need somewhere for a suitcase and clothes. The owner needs a locked closet for personal items, spares, and cleaning stock. Mix the two and you get the review about closets full of the owner's stuff.

The third is outdoor use. A lake house earns its rate on the deck and the water, and many injuries happen there. If you can't maintain an item every turnover, don't list it.

Safety equipment is the first line in the budget

  • Smoke alarms in every bedroom and level
  • CO alarms on every level
  • At least one fire extinguisher
  • TVs and tall furniture anchored
  • Cribs that meet CPSC standards
  • Cleaning supplies locked away
Slide 5 · Safety equipment is the first line in the budget

Safety goes first in the budget, and nothing gets cut from it later.

Airbnb requires hosts to say whether smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are present. If they are, they have to work and you have to test them. You're also responsible for fire and electrical hazards in any area guests can use.

This list comes from the Consumer Product Safety Commission's vacation rental tips. If you have a pool or spa, their list adds compliant drain covers and a fence at least four feet high with self-closing, self-latching gates. Check child products for recalls before you buy a crib.

It's consumer guidance, so I treat it as a planning floor. Your city or state may require more, like interconnected alarms. The fire marshal confirms what this address needs.

Slide 6 · No cameras indoors, and disclose any camera outside

Security devices touch privacy and listing accuracy at the same time.

Airbnb bans cameras and recording devices that monitor indoor spaces, even when they're turned off. Hidden cameras are prohibited everywhere.

Exterior cameras are allowed if the listing says where they are. They can't point into the house or cover private areas like an enclosed outdoor shower or a sauna.

Noise monitors that only measure sound level are allowed in common spaces. They have to be disclosed, and they stay out of bedrooms, bathrooms, and sleeping areas.

So at Cedar Ridge, Maya and Chris buy a doorbell camera, mount it outside, and write its location into the listing. The noise monitor goes in a common area. Check Airbnb's current rules before you publish, because they change.

Every item costs its price, cleaning minutes, and replacement

  • Washable or performance fabrics in the living room
  • Encasements on every mattress from day one
  • One-color bedding from one product line
  • Dishware you can rebuy by the piece
Slide 7 · Every item costs its price, cleaning minutes, and replacement

Every item has three prices: what you pay, the minutes it adds to each turnover, and what it costs to replace. The cheapest sofa is often the most expensive one by year three.

A few buying rules hold up. Washable fabrics let the cleaner handle a spill without a service call. A stained mattress is a replacement, but a stained encasement is a wash. One-color bedding from one line means any sheet fits any bed that size. Dishware sold by the piece means one broken plate doesn't cost a new set. And skip glass tabletops and lamps that need an app.

Then think in cleaning minutes. Twelve decorative pillows get placed after every stay. The cleaner is paid per turnover, so extra minutes get rushed or turn into a higher rate later.

Slide 8 · Spend in five layers, and cut the last two first

The furnishing budget is a fixed input from the underwriting. Your job is to spend it well, not to find more.

I build the plan in five layers, in this order. Safety and access come first. Mattresses and encasements are second, because the beds carry the listing and the reviews. Linens are third, sized to a cycle: three sheet sets per bed and three towel sets per guest. Room furniture and decor is fourth. Delivery, assembly, spares, and owner storage are fifth.

On top of that, hold a contingency of 3 to 5% of the budget. If the draft runs over, cut layers four and five before you touch the first three. Your CPA decides how each piece is treated for tax.

Cedar Ridge is a three-bedroom, two-bath house in a lake market guests drive to. In a normal year, the base case, it expects 61 stays of about 3.5 nights. Maya and Chris use it themselves about 10 days a year.

The layout reaches 8 without a sofa bed. The king sleeps 2, the queen sleeps 2, and the two bunks sleep 4. The listing shows 6 beds, because each bunk counts as 2. Every bed type is listed exactly as installed.

The guest profile matches the house: two families, or two couples with kids, doing lake days and cooking dinner. Midweek in shoulder season, the months between peak and off-peak, brings remote workers and small groups. That's why the plan has a work nook. No parties, and no pets at launch.

Slide 11 · Furniture wear, $5,982 a year, outruns the repairs line

Furniture doesn't fail on schedule, but it does wear out. So I give each group an assumed replacement life and divide. Linens last about two years, mattresses seven, hard furniture eight. These are planning lives, not tax lives.

At Cedar Ridge that comes to $5,982 a year, about $98 per stay. The operating budget sets aside 5% of gross for repairs, which is $3,714. Furniture wear alone is bigger than that. Add the 5% capital reserve and you have $7,428, and furniture would use about 81% of it before the roof or the furnace.

Take that question to Sam's OP.10 review: do the reserves cover furnishings as well as the building? And any single replacement over $200 needs owner approval. OP.8 builds that into the cleaning SOP.

Slide 12 · Setup runs 21 days, with a test stay before photos

Day 0 is possession, the day you get the keys after closing. Photo day is Day 21.

Order the long-lead items before closing: the sectional, the mattresses, and the bunks. Set delivery for Day 3 or later, and pick orders you can reschedule if closing slips.

Safety goes in first. Locks, alarms, extinguishers, and the first aid kit go in on days 0 to 2, before any crew arrives. Beds go in before living room furniture, and tall pieces get anchored the day they're assembled. The owner closet gets locked once the kitchen and linens are stocked.

The step people skip is the test stay. Maya and Chris sleep there and use every item. That's how you find the missing shower hook and the blind that won't close.

Photograph the house as guests will find it

  • Beds made the way the cleaner will
  • Every lamp working, surfaces cleared
  • Safety gear in place and easy to reach
  • Doorbell camera shot, no camera indoors
  • Owner closet locked
  • One shot of each bed type
Slide 13 · Photograph the house as guests will find it

Airbnb says quality is a major factor in how listings appear in search, and photos count toward quality. Guests can filter by amenities, so an amenity you don't list can't be found. One you list but don't have is an accuracy problem. Airbnb can also ask you to retake photos in person through the app.

Run this checklist the evening before photo day. Make the beds the way the cleaner will make them every time, and photograph the doorbell camera location for the disclosure.

Then fill in the room inventory: one row per room with beds, amenities, safety items, photo shots, and notes like "lower level, stairs only." Sam's OP.1 lesson turns that sheet into the listing on each booking site. Your job is to hand him facts that are true and complete.

Slide 14 · Turn your budget into a plan for one real address

Three actions this week.

Start with the guest. Pull three comparable listings from your PA.4 package and read their reviews. Write who books, how many sleep over, what they do, and what they complain about.

Next, call the licensing office. Ask for the maximum occupancy at your address and any permit condition on guest count. Then write your bed list by room and type. Ask the fire marshal about alarm placement while you're at it.

Third, fill in the five-layer budget with your underwriting number. Check that contingency is at least 3%. If you're over, cut layers four and five.

Now someone has to keep the house this way after every stay. OP.7 decides who cleans, restocks, and inspects, and who approves spending like the $200 replacement limit.
