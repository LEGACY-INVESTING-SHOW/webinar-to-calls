Platforms collect some lodging taxes. You owe the rest.

Slide 1 · Platforms collect some lodging taxes. You owe the rest.

A lot of new hosts believe Airbnb handles the taxes. Sometimes it handles some of them. The rest are yours, and the agency expects them whether or not you knew.

By the end of this lesson you'll be able to build a channel-by-channel tax worksheet for one address. It shows which lodging taxes apply, who collects and remits each one on Airbnb, Vrbo, and direct bookings, and what you still owe. You'll know how to keep a registration number current on a listing. And you'll be able to reconcile a quarter of bookings against your tax filings before the agency finds the gap.

We'll use Alder Street again, in a made-up tax jurisdiction. Every rate in the example is there for teaching only.

Slide 2 · A permit doesn't open a tax account

Two words you'll hear all lesson. To collect a tax is to charge it to the guest. To remit it is to file the return and send the money to the agency. A platform can collect a tax and still leave the remitting to you.

Now, three things people blur together. The operating permit is the city's permission to run a short-term rental at the address, and it comes with a number for your listings. Lesson 3.1 covered it.

The tax registration is an account with each agency that collects lodging tax. It has its own number and filing schedule. A permit doesn't open a tax account, and a tax account doesn't give you a permit.

Income tax is separate again. It's tax on your profit, usually on Schedule E, sometimes Schedule C if you provide substantial services. Your CPA decides, and lesson 9.9 covers it.

Slide 3 · Every tax layer has four rules to check

Lodging taxes stack. You'll also hear them called occupancy, hotel, or transient occupancy taxes. One stay can carry a state tax, a county tax, a city tax, and a special district charge like a tourism improvement district. Sometimes one agency collects several layers on one return. Sometimes you file with each one separately.

For every layer, check four rules. First, which stays are taxable. Texas, for example, treats stays of 29 days or less as short-term for hotel tax. Second, what's in the base: nightly rate, cleaning fee, other fees. Third, exemptions. Only use one if the agency's own document lists it, and keep the paperwork.

Fourth, how often you file. Many agencies expect a return every period once you're registered, even when you owe nothing. A missed zero return can bring a penalty in some places.

Slide 4 · Airbnb may collect some taxes, but not all

Airbnb collects and remits some occupancy taxes in some places, and its help center lists them. It works out which taxes apply from your listing address. So if the address is wrong, the taxes can be wrong too.

Even where Airbnb collects some taxes, you may still owe others. Its own example is collecting regional taxes but not local ones. Check the list for your address.

For the taxes Airbnb doesn't collect, you can collect them yourself, or use Airbnb's custom tax tool if your listing is eligible. Airbnb charges the guest and pays you the tax separately from your payout. You still file and pay it.

Your payout doesn't change either way. It's your price minus Airbnb's service fee, and the tax sits on top of what the guest pays.

Slide 5 · Where Vrbo isn't liable, you have three options

Vrbo works on a similar split. Where Vrbo is liable for a lodging tax, it collects and remits that tax itself. Where it isn't, the owner or manager is responsible, and Vrbo lists by state where it collects.

For the taxes you owe, you have three options. One, you collect, file, and pay them yourself. Two, Vrbo collects from the guest and passes the tax to you with your payout, and you file and pay. Three, Vrbo collects and Avalara MyLodgeTax, a tax filing service, prepares, files, and pays. Vrbo describes that as available at no extra cost.

Watch option two. The tax money lands in your bank account mixed with your payout. It isn't yours. It belongs to the agency, and it has to be there when you file. Don't spend it.

Slide 6 · On a direct booking, every tax layer is yours

On a direct booking, nobody collects for you. You quote the tax, charge it, hold it, report it, and pay it, for every layer.

Your booking engine is the checkout on your own website. Your PMS, the property management system, holds reservations and pricing. Whichever you use needs the right rate and the right base for each layer. Check those settings against your worksheet before your first direct booking.

Texas spells out the split plainly. Owners collect and remit state hotel tax on bookings through their own website. And even when a platform handles state tax, owners should still check local hotel tax with the county and city.

When you reach Sam's lessons 9.11 and 9.12, find the screen where tax rates are set and check it line by line against your worksheet.

Keep every listing's number exact and current

  • Enter it exactly as the city issued it
  • Match name and address, down to the unit
  • NYC platforms verify it before collecting fees
  • Calendar each expiration 60 days out
Slide 7 · Keep every listing's number exact and current

Where a city requires a permit or registration number, the platform usually has a field for it. On Airbnb, it's in the listing's laws and regulations section. Some cities allow "City registration pending" or "Exempt." Only use those where the city allows them for your listing.

Some cities make the platform check the number. In New York City, a booking service can't collect its fee until it has checked the city's verification system. That check matches the number, the host's legal name, and the address. So keep the name and address identical everywhere, down to the unit number.

Numbers also expire. In Los Angeles, the registration lasts one year and the renewal window opens 30 days before expiration. Put every expiration on the calendar with a reminder 60 days out. That's my suggested buffer, not a rule. File when the window opens, then save a dated screenshot of the listing.

Reconcile owed and collected tax before filing

  1. Take out refunded cancellations
  2. Split taxable receipts by channel
  3. Compute the tax you owe, by layer
  4. Compare with tax collected. Explain every gap.
Slide 8 · Reconcile owed and collected tax before filing

Reconciliation means proving that the tax you filed matches the stays you hosted. Do it every filing period, before you file. Taxable receipts are what guests paid for nights plus any fees the tax applies to, like cleaning.

You need three records: your PMS reservation report, each platform's earnings report, and your tax worksheet.

Start from the reservations and take out full refunds. Split receipts by channel. Separate the receipts where a platform remitted the tax from the ones where you owe it, and compute your tax by layer. Then add up the tax you actually collected, and compare the two.

A shortfall usually means you forgot to charge something, and you owe it anyway, out of your own pocket. An overage usually means you charged tax that didn't apply. Ask the agency how to handle it.

Now the worked example. Every rate and collection arrangement here is made up. It's a hypothetical place I'm calling Alder City, not any real city. Before you use a worksheet like this, replace every rate with the official agency's rate, its link, and the date you checked it.

Alder City has four layers: state tax at 6%, county at 2%, and city at 5%, all on nightly plus cleaning, plus a tourism district charge of $2 per occupied night.

Alder Street's year is 85 stays and $61,625 of bookings. I've split them 64 on Airbnb, 13 on Vrbo, and 8 direct. Guests pay about $8,521 in occupancy tax on top. That's roughly 13.8%. On one $725 stay, it's $100.25.

None of this is your revenue. But somebody has to send every dollar of it to the right agency.

Slide 11 · A quarterly check finds you $12 short

Last, the quarterly check. Say the quarter has 24 stays: 17 on Airbnb, 4 on Vrbo, and 3 direct. One direct stay cancels with a full refund, so its $725 comes out. That leaves 69 nights and $16,675 of taxable receipts.

Owed means tax you must file and pay yourself. Here that's state on direct, county on Vrbo and direct, and city and district on everything. It comes to $1,145.75.

Collected means tax money that reached you from guests: Airbnb's custom tax payout, Vrbo's pass-through, and what you charged on direct bookings. That's $1,133.75.

The gap is $12. The booking tool missed the district fee on the two direct stays. Pay it from operating cash and fix the setting before the next direct booking. Twelve dollars is small. The same mistake across several units and years isn't.

Build a five-part worksheet for one address

  • A. Your registrations, kept separate
  • B. Every layer's rate, base, and rules
  • C. Who collects each layer, by channel
  • D. A quarterly reconciliation
  • E. A check of every listing's number
Slide 12 · Build a five-part worksheet for one address

The exercise is a channel-by-channel tax worksheet for one real address, in five parts.

Part A lists your registrations: the permit, then a tax account for each layer, with the agency, the number, and the renewal date. Part B is the layers: rate, base, taxable stay length, exemptions, and whether a zero return is required. Part C is who collects each layer on Airbnb, Vrbo, and direct. Part D is the quarterly reconciliation by channel, with an explanation for every variance. Part E checks every listing's number, name, address, and expiration.

Every rate needs an official link and a date. If you can't find one, write unresolved. No bookings yet? Practice Part D with the Alder Street quarter.

Your CPA confirms how the taxes land in your books, and the city licensing office confirms how the number must appear.
