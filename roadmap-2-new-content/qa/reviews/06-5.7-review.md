# Review: 5.7 The 2026 Landlord Pitch: Handling Regulation & Liability Objections

Reviewer stance: a first-time learner who has never hosted, never leased a unit to re-rent, and hasn't seen any other lesson. Files reviewed: `parts/06-5.7.md`, `slides/src/06-5.7.json` (17 slides + notes), `slides/06-5.7.html` (screenshots of all 17 slides). Math re-checked by hand: $1,950 × 24 = $46,800; 85 ÷ 52 = 1.63. Both are correct. Clause numbers were checked against `parts/05-3.5.md` (ten-topic table and illustrative addendum).

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The promise is clear and I liked it: get a clear yes or a clear no in writing, and back every answer with a document. The objection table and the Dana role play made that idea concrete. Then the lesson kept assuming vocabulary I don't have: STR, declarations page, additional insured, "binds", hard stops, scorecard label, L1 and L2, cure period, change-in-law clause, SR.1, "Sam's 5.6". I also tripped on two places where the lesson seems to contradict itself. The sample opening promises to "get the city permit" right after I'm told not to promise things I can't control. And one sentence in the insurance section says the Airbnb program "covers" fungi and bacteria when it means those are excluded. The insurance section is the part the lesson says matters most, so that one worried me.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. Title / 5.7.1: "the part of the conversation that decides most deals in 2026." What makes this a 2026 pitch? Nothing in the lesson is specific to 2026. And who measured "most deals"?
2. 5.7.1 / slide 2 box: "Sam's 5.6 lesson showed you how to find the owner." Who is Sam? What do I need to bring from 5.6? The slide 2 notes say "the owner's name and a working phone number", but the Markdown doesn't.
3. 5.7.1 / slide 2: "the address should already have cleared the hard stops in lesson 3.4." What is a hard stop? Can you give one example so I know what I'm checking?
4. 5.7.2 sample opening: "I run a small furnished-rental business." What if this is my first unit and I don't have a business yet? How do I say this honestly, when the lesson keeps telling me not to say anything I can't back up?
5. 5.7.2 sample opening: "I'll ... get the city permit." Two paragraphs earlier I'm told "Don't promise the owner things you can't control." The worked example says the permit needs the owner's signed consent and the city has to issue it. Isn't "get the city permit" exactly that kind of promise?
6. 5.7.2: the four points are operator, guests, maintenance, and benefit. The sample also covers insurance, the permit, and a written addendum. Are those a hidden fifth point, or should they wait for the owner's questions?
7. 5.7.3 table, "Insurance declarations page (or a written quote)". What is a declarations page? Can I get a quote before I have a signed lease?
8. 5.7.3 table, "Operating summary ... Where it comes from: Your underwriting." What underwriting? I haven't done any in this lesson.
9. 5.7.3 table, "Local contact sheet ... Your cleaner or on-call vendor (Module 6)." Module 6 comes after this lesson. How do I name a local contact for the owner before I've hired one?
10. 5.7.3: "I'll send the declarations page when the policy binds." What does "binds" mean?
11. 5.7.4 row 1: "change-in-law clause (3.5, clause 8)." In the ten-topic table in 3.5, number 8 is "Permit and tax authority" and change in law is number 10. Which clause 8 does this mean? (It turns out to be clause 8 of 3.5's sample addendum, but the lesson calls the addendum "the ten topics from lesson 3.5", which pointed me at the topic table.)
12. 5.7.4 row "Who pays for damage?" says to show your "STR policy declarations page". Does a liability policy pay for damage to the owner's unit? I thought liability meant injuries and claims against me. If it doesn't cover the unit, showing it here would mislead the owner.
13. 5.7.4 rows "Who pays for damage?" and "What if you can't fill it": "security deposit". Is that the deposit I pay the owner under the lease, or a deposit I collect from guests?
14. 5.7.4 row "What if you can't fill it and stop paying?" says to show "Your stress case and cash reserve (lesson 1.3, 3.4)". What is a stress case? Do I really hand the owner my own finances?
15. 5.7.4 row "My other tenants will complain": "Airbnb's neighbor line exists as a backstop." Is it a phone line? The source (article 3290) is described as a page where neighbors can report parties or disturbances.
16. 5.7.5 / slide 9: "I treat the platform program as a backup for Airbnb bookings only." What does "backup" mean here? Does Airbnb's liability insurance pay after my policy does? The lesson never says how the two interact, and this is the section where it warns me against saying something inaccurate.
17. 5.7.6: "assume (assumption) the unnamed city." Why is the city unnamed? Is Alder Street real?
18. 5.7.6: "Before the call, run two numbers the landlord may care about. Rent over the full term: $46,800." Why would the landlord care? And where do I use it? The operator never says $46,800 in the role play. Slide 10's title makes it the headline number, and it then disappears.
19. 5.7.6 role play: "That's clause 4 in the draft addendum." Dana hasn't been handed the addendum yet. That happens at the end ("Here's the addendum"). Is the operator pointing at a document Dana doesn't have?
20. 5.7.6 role play: "No guest checks in before the permit is issued. That condition is written into the addendum too." Which clause? The 3.5 sample addendum I checked doesn't include that condition.
21. 5.7.6 Ending A: "the 30-day cure period should start only after written notice by email and by mail." What is a cure period? And on slide 12 the wording is only "start only after written notice". What did the attorney change, if notice was already required to be written?
22. 5.7.6 Ending A: did Dana ever check with her own insurer and lender? The operator asked her to. Ending B turns on exactly that check, but Ending A skips it and goes straight to signing.
23. 5.7.6 Ending A: "The 3.4 scorecard rows L1 and L2 drop to 0." What are L1 and L2? Why does signing this addendum drop them?
24. 5.7.6 Ending B: "ask whether a standard lease is of interest." What is a standard lease, and why would I want one if my plan is short stays?
25. 5.7.7 vs Ending A: 5.7.7 says the approval email lists what you still owe: "declarations page, permit number, local contact". Ending A lists only "the declarations page and the permit number". Which is right?
26. 5.7.7 pass log: "Follow-up date". How far out should it be for a pass?
27. 5.7.8 exercise: "Fill this in for one real address before you call." What if I don't have an address yet? Can I practice on Alder Street?
28. 5.7.8 row "Address and 3.4 scorecard label". What is a label?
29. Slide 16 notes: "Four things this week, each under an hour." An email to a broker takes minutes to send, but the answer can take days. Is the hour estimate for my time only?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- 5.7.5, line 85: "The program summary lists exclusions for injury or damage the insured intended or should have expected. Others cover fungi or bacteria and liability assumed under a contract." "Others cover" reads as though the program covers fungi and bacteria. It means that other exclusions apply to them. This inverts the meaning in the section where accuracy matters most.
- Slide 7 title: "Some objections belong to the owner: if their insurer or lender says no, log a pass." Only the last of the four rows on this slide is the owner's to answer. The other three (vacancy, night calls, guest injury) are yours. The title describes one row and mislabels the other three.
- Clause numbering. "The ten topics from lesson 3.5" (5.7.3, 5.7.6, slide 5) sends me to the 3.5 topic table. But "(3.5, clause 8)" and "clause 4" use the numbering of 3.5's sample addendum, which is different: topic 8 is permit authority, and sample clause 8 is change in law. As written, I'd look up the wrong clause.
- Ending A (slide 12 and its notes): "the 30-day cure period start only after written notice." The attorney's change, "by email and by mail", was dropped from the slide and the notes. Without it, the change sounds like nothing, because notice was already written.
- 5.7.2: "Don't promise the owner things you can't control," followed by a sample that says "I'll ... get the city permit." The example contradicts the rule it follows.
- 5.7.5 / slide 9: "I treat the platform program as a backup." This implies an order of payment that the lesson never establishes.
- 5.7.4, row "Who pays for damage?": the evidence is a liability declarations page. It isn't clear that a liability policy responds to damage to the owner's unit. The lesson doesn't say, and a learner will present it as if it does.
- 5.7.6 setup: "assume (assumption) the unnamed city allows unhosted stays". "Assume (assumption)" is redundant, and "unhosted" is never defined. It means you don't live in the unit.
- 5.7.6, "Before the first stay, I'll give you and the other three units a name and a 24-hour number." Units don't take calls; tenants do. Say "the tenants in the other three units".
- Slide 10: the whole role-play setting (city rules, hard stops cleared, sleeps 5, insurance, permit) is squeezed into small grey text beside a rent calculation. The title is about rent, so I skipped the setting and then didn't know why Dana's permit needed her consent.
