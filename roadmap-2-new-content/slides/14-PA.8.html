Slide 1 · Set your walk-away price from net operating income before you see the seller's number

This lesson is about one number you set before you ever talk to the seller: your walk-away price.

By the end you'll build that number from three things. Your base-case and weak-case net operating income. Your loan terms. And the cash you actually have. Then you'll learn the eight contract terms that protect it. They include earnest money, three contingencies, seller concessions, closing date, furniture, and existing bookings.

And you'll learn a habit. Every negotiation decision gets recorded as proceed, renegotiate, or walk, with the reason in one line.

We stay with Cedar Ridge, the course's hypothetical lake house, and Maya and Chris, our example buyers. The asking price is $399,000. They end up under contract at $385,000. You'll see exactly why $399,000 fails their tests and $385,000 passes. All Cedar Ridge figures are assumptions from the course example.

Slide 2 · Decide the most you'll pay first, then negotiate below that number

The asking price is the seller's opening position. It tells you what the seller hopes to get. It says nothing about what the house can carry as a short-term rental.

A common way STR offers go wrong is working backward from the asking price. The buyer sees $399,000, then nudges the nightly rate up until the spreadsheet works. Now the offer depends on the most optimistic line in the model.

Reverse the order. Decide the most you'll pay first, from numbers you'd defend to a lender. Then negotiate below that number. If the seller won't come under it, you walk, and you already know why.

Sam's PA.4 lesson is the live underwriting demo. I'm not redoing it here. You bring back two lines. NOI is net operating income: revenue minus operating costs, before the loan payment. Base-case NOI is your expected year. Weak-case NOI is a bad year. This lesson turns those two lines into a price.

Slide 3 · Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings, run on the costlier loan

Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings.

Test one is base-case cash flow. At your expected revenue, the house must clear a minimum annual cash flow after the loan payment. You set the minimum. Set it above zero, because base-case years still bring surprises.

Test two is weak-case survival. It has two versions, and you pick one before you run any numbers. Strict means the house breaks even in a weak year. Funded means you carry a set shortfall, in writing, with the cash set aside. Maya and Chris pick funded.

Test three is cash. Down payment, closing costs, points, furnishing, lender reserves, a weak-year reserve, and your emergency fund all have to fit. Their $231,000 is the $210,000 from lesson 0.3 plus six months of saving $3,500.

Use the costlier quote for all three. For them that's the DSCR loan, sized mostly on the property's income. PITIA is the full monthly payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and dues.

Slide 4 · Keeping $5,000 of base-case cash flow caps Cedar Ridge's price at $394,310

Here are the inputs: base-case NOI $29,209, weak-case NOI $18,162. Property tax and insurance are already inside NOI.

Test one. Because NOI already covers tax and insurance, cash flow is simply NOI minus principal and interest. I'll call that P&I.

They want at least $5,000 a year in the base case. So the most P&I they can carry is $29,209 minus $5,000. That's $24,209 a year, or $2,017 a month.

At 7.25% over 30 years, $2,017 a month supports a loan of about $295,732.

How do you get that? A loan calculator or a spreadsheet does it. In a spreadsheet, PV of 0.0725 over 12, 360 months, and minus 2,017 gives about $295,700. The small difference is rounding in the monthly payment.

With 25% down, the loan is 75% of the price. Divide by 0.75 and the price ceiling is $394,310.

PA.8 · Offer Strategy & Negotiation§ PA.8.49 / 20
Slide 9 · Four of the eight contract terms decide how much cash you could need or lose

Price is one term. Eight others decide how much risk you carry between signing and closing. Purchase contracts are state-specific, often a standard form your agent uses. So treat all of this as questions for your agent and attorney.

This slide is the four money terms.

Earnest money counts toward your cash to close. It doesn't lower the total cash you need. You pay part of it earlier, and it's credited at closing. A bigger deposit can make an offer stronger. It also raises what you could lose if you miss a deadline.

The financing contingency lets you cancel if the loan isn't approved by a date. Match it to your lender's real timeline.

Lenders generally size the loan on the lower of price or appraised value. Ask yours to confirm. If you offer gap coverage, only offer cash you already counted in Test three.

Seller credits reduce your cash to close, but lenders limit them. The limit can depend on how the lender classifies the house. Get the limit in dollars first.

Slide 10 · The other four terms decide whether you can test the house and launch on time

Now the other four terms: time and scope.

The inspection period is where you test the business, not just the house. That means permits, homeowners association or deed rules, insurance quotes, and septic capacity. PA.9 builds that list. Make sure the window is long enough to get STR insurance quotes and a written answer from the licensing office.

The closing date has two drivers. Your rate lock expires, and extensions cost money. And your launch season matters. Closing in time to furnish, photograph, and list before Cedar Ridge's lake season is worth more than a small price cut.

For furniture, ask if it fits your OP.6 design plan, whether it's usable, and how your lender treats it. If you want it, list it in the contract or a bill of sale. If you don't, say it's excluded.

For existing bookings, ask whether they transfer, who keeps the payments, and whether the platform even allows a transfer.

Before you waive or shorten

Get written answers from your agent and lender before you shorten any contingency

Ask your agent

  • Can we still fit the inspection, septic or sewer check, and STR insurance quote?
  • What happens to my earnest money if I cancel after the contingency expires?
  • What's normal here for this type of house?
  • Can the permit answer arrive before inspection ends?

Ask your lender

  • How many days do you really need to approve this loan?
  • What happens if the appraisal comes in $10,000 low?
  • Does approval depend on proof of STR permit eligibility?
  • What's your seller credit limit for this occupancy?
  • When does my rate lock expire, and what does extending cost?

PA.8 · Offer Strategy & Negotiation§ PA.8.511 / 20
Slide 11 · Get written answers from your agent and lender before you shorten any contingency

Sellers sometimes ask for a shorter inspection period, no financing contingency, or appraisal gap coverage. Before you agree, get written answers to these questions.

From your agent: can you still fit the inspection, the septic or sewer check, and the STR insurance quote in a shorter window? What happens to your earnest money if you cancel after the contingency expires? What's normal in this market for this type of house? And can the permit or licensing answer arrive before inspection ends?

From your lender: how many days does it really need? What happens if the appraisal comes in $10,000 low? Does approval depend on proof of STR permit eligibility, and when? What's the seller credit limit? And when does the rate lock expire?

The rule is simple. If any answer puts your earnest money at risk for a problem you can't test in time, keep the contingency.

Slide 17 · Your walk-away price is the lowest of your three ceilings, rounded down

Here are the three ceilings, in formula form.

Test one: base NOI minus your minimum cash flow gives your maximum annual P&I. Turn that into a loan amount, then divide by your loan-to-price. At 25% down, that's 0.75.

Test two works the same way, starting from weak NOI plus your allowed shortfall. If you hold the strict rule, the allowed shortfall is zero.

Test three is the highest price where total committed cash stays at or under your liquid cash. Try prices in a spreadsheet until you hit the limit.

To turn a payment into a loan amount, use the PV function. For Cedar Ridge, PV of 0.0725 over 12, 360 months, and minus 2,017 gives about $295,700.
