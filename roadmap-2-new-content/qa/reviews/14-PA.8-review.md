# Review: PA.8 Offer Strategy & Negotiation for Investment Property

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The core idea is clear and useful: pick the most I'll pay before I see the seller's number, and write down why I proceed, renegotiate, or walk. The worked example gives me concrete numbers from start to finish. But I get lost early. NOI, P&I, PITIA, DSCR, STR, "points", "0.3", "Example B", "Sam", "Maya and Chris" and several lesson codes all show up before anyone explains them. There's also a hidden inconsistency. The $10,000 "keep unallocated" rule shows up only in PA.8.6, but the exercise asks me to set it before I compute my ceiling. If Maya and Chris had followed it, their walk-away price would be about $387,000, not $393,000, and the $392,000 counter that "passes on paper" would actually fail. Slide 6 also contradicts its own title: it says the funded test is "the lowest ceiling" while showing a much shorter strict bar.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. "base-case NOI and weak-case NOI" (PA.8.1, slide 2). What's the difference between them, and who decides what counts as a "weak" year? The lesson says to "bring his outputs", but I don't have PA.4. One sentence defining each would let me follow along.
2. "Sam's PA.4 lesson" (PA.8.1). Who is Sam? Who are Maya and Chris (first named in PA.8.2)? Nothing introduces them.
3. "The tracker asks for an offer ceiling from conservative NOI and cash required" (PA.8.2). What tracker? This reads like an internal production note.
4. "Use the more expensive financing quote" / "they underwrite on the DSCR quote: 7.25%, 1 point, 25% down" (PA.8.2, slide 3). What is DSCR? Why is it the more expensive one? What does "1 point" cost me in dollars?
5. "In 0.3 they had $210,000 and a funding condition" (PA.8.3). What is "0.3"? At first I read it as a number, not a lesson code.
6. "Lender reserves: 6 months of PITIA" (PA.8.3, slides 3 and 8). What's PITIA? Is the lender reserve money I spend, or money that just has to sit in my account? Can it overlap with my emergency fund?
7. "At 7.25% over 30 years, $2,017 a month supports a loan of about $295,732" (PA.8.3, slide 4). How do I get that number? The PV function only appears three sections later (PA.8.8).
8. "The ceiling works out to about $409,984" (PA.8.3 Test 3, slide 6). How? PITIA and the weak shortfall both change with price, so I can't reproduce this by hand. Is it trial and error in a spreadsheet?
9. "Round down: $393,000" (PA.8.3). Why round down to the nearest $1,000? Is that a rule or a habit?
10. "I set one more rule for Maya and Chris: keep at least $10,000 of the $231,000 unallocated after every commitment" (PA.8.6). If they had this rule, why wasn't it inside Test 3? At $392,000 (the seller's counter) and $393,000 (the walk-away) they'd have only $7,931 and $7,490 unallocated, below their own rule.
11. "Why $372,000? ... That leaves room for two counters" (PA.8.7). Why two counters? Where does the $21,000 cushion come from? Is it a percentage of price, a habit, or arbitrary?
12. "They gave the seller what cost them little: a larger deposit" (PA.8.7). PA.8.4 says a bigger deposit "raises what you could lose if you miss a deadline." So why does doubling it cost little here? I'm guessing it's because all three contingencies stay open, but the lesson doesn't say so.
13. "No seller credit, so the canonical cash to close of $110,688 stands" (PA.8.7). PA.8.4 says the deposit reduces cash to close. Doesn't the $7,700 deposit reduce the $110,688? Or is the deposit already inside it?
14. "Value $375,000 | Gap $10,000" (decision log) vs "$281,250. That's $7,500 less than planned" (PA.8.6, slide 11). Is the gap $10,000 or $7,500? The difference is never explained.
15. "If the seller holds at $385,000 and the appraisal contingency is still open, walk" (PA.8.6). What if the contingency has already expired? Then what?
16. "Seller concessions ... the limit may depend on the occupancy type" (PA.8.4). What occupancy types are there, and which one is an STR?
17. "closing in time to furnish, photograph, and list before the lake season" (PA.8.4). What lake? I don't know where Cedar Ridge is.
18. "Canonical inputs (all assumptions from Example B)" (PA.8.3). What is Example B? What does "canonical" mean to me as a learner?
19. "Not in counter" (slide 13, Round 2). Did the seller accept the 30-day financing contingency and the appraisal contingency, or drop them? The Markdown table just leaves them out.
20. "Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings" (PA.8.2) vs four bars on slide 6. Which one don't I count, and why is it on the chart?

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"The lowest ceiling, the funded weak-case test, sets the walk-away price at $393,000"** (slide 6 title). On the chart, the strict bar ($295,818) is obviously the lowest, so the title contradicts what I see in five seconds. The strict bar needs to look excluded (greyed, and labeled "not their rule").
- **"Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings. Test 2 comes in two versions, strict and funded, and you pick one before you run the numbers."** (PA.8.2). But PA.8.3 computes the strict version first and only then says Maya and Chris "decide in writing" to use the funded one. The order suggests they picked after seeing the numbers, which is the exact behavior PA.8.1 warns against. State their choice before the arithmetic.
- **The $10,000 unallocated rule is introduced late and never feeds into the ceiling.** It appears in PA.8.6 as "one more rule". Then the exercise (PA.8.8 Part 1 inputs, slide 15) and the "four rules" on slide 18 ask me to set it up front. But the Test 3 formula ("Highest price where total committed cash ≤ liquid cash") never uses it. I computed Test 3 with a $10,000 floor, using the lesson's own inputs: about $387,308. The walk-away price would then be about $387,000. That changes the $392,000 counter from "passes on paper with almost no margin" to a clear fail.
- **"It should cover more than zero, because base-case years still bring surprises."** (PA.8.2 Test 1). "Cover" is the wrong verb here. The sentence means "set it above zero."
- **"Cap rate uses NOI but ignores your loan. Cash flow shows what the loan does to that NOI. Both belong in Sam's PA.4 package. This lesson uses the one that decides whether you can hold the house."** (PA.8.2). If I don't know what a cap rate is, this paragraph adds a new term only to set it aside. See Simplify or cut.
- **"Total cash needed at a price P has seven parts."** (PA.8.3 Test 3). "Price P" suddenly turns into algebra. The seven parts are listed in one long sentence that's hard to count.
- **"Pay the full gap | $385,000 | Cash to close $118,112"** (PA.8.6 table, slide 11). The baseline cash to close ($110,688) isn't in the table, so I can't see that the extra cash is $7,424. I also can't match this column to the $142,688 "Total cash in" on slide 8, because that figure includes furnishing and this one doesn't.
- **"Earnest money ... It goes toward your cash to close. The CFPB's Loan Estimate explainer notes that your estimated cash to close is reduced by any deposit you've already paid."** (PA.8.4). This is correct, but it made me think the deposit lowers the total cash I need. It doesn't; it only changes when I pay it. Say that plainly.
- **"Set your walk-away price from conservative NOI"** (cover, PA.8.0 "from conservative net operating income"). Test 1 uses base-case NOI, which the lesson never calls conservative. Which NOI is "conservative": base, weak, or both?
- **"$392,000 is under $393,000, but only by $1,000 ... It passes on paper"** (PA.8.7). The mock treats a $1,000 margin as a pass, yet Scenario 2 shows a $4,500 repair can push them past a cash rule. Readers will ask why the unallocated rule wasn't checked here (see above).
