# Review: OP.9 Guest Experience & Review Strategy in the AI Ranking Era

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The title promised me something about "AI ranking," and the lesson's honest answer is that nobody outside Airbnb knows. That's useful, but the lesson never says it in one plain sentence, so I spent the first three slides waiting for the AI part. After that, the practical material (checkpoints, friction log, the review-request script, the "never write this" list) is the clearest and most usable part. I kept tripping on cast and role words I was never given: who Maya, Chris, and Sam are, what "Owner" versus "Message owner" means, what the "$200 approval limit" and "owner decision list" are, what "base case" and "B1" are. The "when does a complaint need a fix" rule changes between two and three mentions depending on where I look. Slide 17's decision boxes contradict themselves.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. "Airbnb names four search factors: quality, popularity, price, and location" (Slide 2 title). The slide then lists six bullets, including variety and availability. So are there four factors or six?
2. "Guest Experience & Review Strategy in the AI Ranking Era" (title, cover). What is the AI ranking era, and does anything I do in this lesson change because of AI? The lesson never answers this directly.
3. "Price is Sam's OP.5 territory" (MD OP.9.1, Slide 3). Who is Sam? And "Listings priced below comparable listings ... tend to rank higher": does that mean I should cut my price? Nothing tells me not to.
4. "stay lengths that fit your permit" (MD OP.9.1, Slide 3). What permit? Why would a permit limit my stay lengths? This is assumed knowledge from other lessons.
5. "Owner" vs "Message owner" (MD OP.9.4 table, Slide 6, Slide 11). Is "Owner" the person who owns a task, or the property owners? Is the message owner a different person from the owner?
6. "A question guests ask three times means the house needs a fix" (Slide 7) vs "A complaint reason that appears twice in a quarter goes on the fix list" (MD OP.9.9, Slide 17) vs "No category repeats 3 times in a quarter without a fix" (MD OP.9.7, Slide 11). Which number do I use: two or three?
7. "Two reviewers mentioned a warm bunk room" (MD OP.9.8). There's only one three-star review. Was the second mention in a four-star review, a message, or the friction log? Slide 15's notes say "the complaint log already shows the same issue from another guest." So the "2" on Slide 13 isn't two reviews?
8. "You're right that the lower level ran warm during that heat wave" (MD OP.9.8, Slide 15). What heat wave? The guest's review doesn't mention one, and nothing earlier does either.
9. "Either is above the $200 approval limit, so it goes on the owner decision list" (MD OP.9.8, Slide 14). What approval limit? What's the owner decision list, and who decides?
10. "Fix what's fixable before you respond, if you can" (MD OP.9.6 step 4) vs "Respond publicly only after the facts are checked and any promised fix is done" (MD OP.9.9, Slide 17 gate 3) vs "Post the public response only once the promised fix is really installed" (Slide 15). If the owners take a month to approve the fan, do I stay silent for a month? Or do I respond now and say only what's done? The three places disagree.
11. "Sort the complaint: fair and fixable, fair but a known trade-off, or factually wrong" (MD OP.9.6 step 3, Slide 10). What's an example of a "known trade-off"? And what do I do differently for each of the three kinds? Only "fair and fixable" gets an example.
12. "Wait a day (assumption)" (MD OP.9.6, Slide 10). Why does it say "(assumption)"? Is that Airbnb's rule, or yours?
13. "Median under 15 minutes, 7 a.m. to 10 p.m.; all under 60" (MD OP.9.7, Slide 11). Does "all under 60" include overnight messages? The worked example counts a 90-minute overnight reply as a problem, so I guess yes, but then the target can't be met with a 10 p.m. cutoff.
14. "Platform inbox or PMS" (MD OP.9.7). What's a PMS?
15. "a human or approved-template reply" (MD OP.9.7, Slide 11). Who approves a template? Does an automated reply count?
16. "Track the share below 5 and read every one" (MD OP.9.7) and "A review below four stars gets a read of the turnover photos and message thread within 48 hours" (MD OP.9.9). So I read four-star reviews but only investigate three-stars and below? Two thresholds, and they're not explained together.
17. "Review rate ... Record only; no target, since you can't push it" (MD OP.9.7, Slide 11). "Can't" or "shouldn't"? I can obviously send more reminders. Does the policy forbid it?
18. "If anything wasn't right, please tell us directly too" (MD OP.9.5, Slide 9). Isn't steering unhappy guests to private feedback a way of keeping bad reviews off the listing? Is that allowed under the "pressure" rule?
19. "sent two or three days after checkout" (MD OP.9.5, Slide 9). Why day 2-3 and not checkout day, given the 14-day window?
20. "7 days out: Trip prep sent" (MD OP.9.4, Slide 6). What if the booking comes in 3 days before arrival?
21. "Party size matches the listing; no sign of an event" (MD OP.9.4, Slide 6). Why am I checking for events? What do I do if I see one?
22. "Watch for how he compares actual revenue, occupancy, and expenses with the PA.4 underwriting" (MD OP.9.11, Slide 19 notes). What's PA.4? What's underwriting?
23. "From the canonical file, B1: 61 stays" (Slide 20). What canonical file? What's B1?
24. "Start a friction log in your task app and add every guest question from the last month" (Slide 19). What task app? I don't have guests yet.
25. "Internet outage on the advertised workspace" (Slide 14). What advertised workspace? The Markdown never says Cedar Ridge advertises a workspace.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **Slide 17 gates are logically inconsistent.** The red box reads as the answer to the question, but it's the "yes" action for gates 1-2 and the "no" action for gates 3-4. Gate 1: "Has a complaint reason appeared twice this quarter?" gives "Put it on the fix list" (that's the YES action). Gate 3: "Are the facts checked and the promised fix done?" gives "Wait to post" (the NO action). A viewer can't tell which answer the box belongs to.
- **Repeat threshold of two vs three** (see Q6). The lesson's central rule ("repeats get fixed") has three different numbers.
- **Slide 13 mixes units.** Star-rating counts (7, 2, 1 reviews) and complaint-mention counts (2, 1, 1) sit on one bar chart with one scale. The "2" for bunk room looks like two three-star reviews.
- **"Owner" means two things.** In tables it's a task-owner role ("Owner", "Cleaner, then owner"). In "owner decision list" and "owners haven't approved it," it means Maya and Chris as property owners. Maya is also the message owner (Slide 14 notes: "outside Maya's messaging hours"), but the Markdown says "the messaging owner's hours."
- **Slide 14 title vs content.** "Every repeat complaint gets a fix..." but three of the four rows are one-off findings (directions 1, late reply 1, internet 1). So do one-offs get fixed or not?
- **"No secret field that AI reads"** (Slide 3). Under the heading "What the page doesn't say" this is fine. But as a standalone bullet it reads as a flat claim that no such field exists. The Markdown wording ("It doesn't mention a secret field that AI reads") is the accurate one.
- **"These four rules line up almost one-for-one with what guests review"** (MD OP.9.2, Slide 4 title). No source is given for what guests review. It's stated as fact.
- **"Most negative reviews start as a repeated question nobody fixed"** (MD OP.9.4, Slide 7, Slide 7 notes). This is an unsupported "most" claim presented as fact.
- **Two "most" claims back to back** (MD OP.9.4). "The friction log is the most useful tool on the list," then "The arrival-evening message matters most." Which one is it?
- **"as confirmed by search summaries on 2026-09-25"** (MD OP.9.1, Slide 2 notes). For a learner this sounds like the author didn't read the page, which undercuts the lesson's own "ask for the Airbnb page" rule. It's production detail.
- **Slide 8, "reciprocal review, or promise."** "Promise" of what? The Markdown has "a promise not to take negative action." The slide truncates it to a word that means nothing.
- **Slide 8, "Coordinating with..."** Coordinating what? It isn't clear from the slide.
- **Slide 9, "Any goodwill gesture tied to review timing."** Abstract with no example on the slide. The Maya $100 credit example is only in the notes and Markdown.
- **Slide 18 / MD OP.9.10 "Who confirms what"** is a production checklist ("Sam, before recording," "checked before recording"). A learner is told Sam must approve a recording, which isn't their job.
- **MD line 123, "[Recording note: Preston can add a real example here if he has one.]"** is a production note visible in the learner Markdown.
- **Slide 12 label "ALL FIGURES ON THIS SLIDE ARE ASSUMPTIONS"** conflicts with Slide 20, which says 61 stays is the canonical base case, not an assumption.
- **Slide 12 rounding.** 15 × 70% = 10.5, shown as "about 10." A learner checking the math gets 10.5 and wonders whether it should be 11.
- **Slide 5 "Where it lives" column** mixes lessons ("OP.8", "Sam's OP.5") with people ("Message owner", "Owner"). The title says "a job someone on the team already owns," but most cells are lesson codes, not people.
