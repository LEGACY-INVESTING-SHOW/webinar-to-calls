# Review: OP.12 Exit Strategy: Hold, Refinance, Sell, or 1031 Exchange

Files reviewed: `parts/24-OP.12.md` (lesson), `slides/src/24-OP.12.json` (deck spec and notes), `slides/24-OP.12.html` (20 slides, screenshotted; the tool reported no overflow).

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The frame is clear: one question ("is this equity doing more here than elsewhere?") and four options. The first four slides land. Then the lesson turns into a tax seminar full of section numbers (1245, 1250, 1231), internal case codes (B1, B4, B6, B7, B8, "canonical"), and course codes (PA.1, 0.3, SR.3, OP.8). None of those means anything to someone opening only this lesson. The worked example has one serious problem. It tells me to "subtract $400 from every cash-flow figure below," then never does, so the corrected and uncorrected numbers sit side by side. When I got to the worksheet, I couldn't compute the "return on after-tax equity" row for Hold, because the formula divides by a cash-received figure of $0.

2. OP.12.0: "hand to that CPA and to a qualified intermediary". What is a qualified intermediary? It isn't defined until OP.12.5.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- OP.12.6, second paragraph: "These comparisons use the original underwriting (B1) ... Subtract $400 from every cash-flow figure below. The hold becomes $5,172 / -$5,875 ... The hold earns 3.6% on $142,560 instead of 3.9%." Every figure after that point (Step 2, Step 3, the 4.4%/4.9% line, the Step 6 table) uses the uncorrected numbers. The learner has to adjust in their head, and the deck (slides 9, 10, 14) shows only the uncorrected figures. This is the biggest thread-loss in the lesson.
- OP.12.3: the $5,000 / $140,000 / 3.6% example. With the $400 correction, Cedar Ridge's hold return is also 3.6%. Two different "3.6%" figures built from different inputs invite confusion.
- OP.12.4, item 1 is one 90-word list item. It mixes personal property, cost-segregation lives, 15-year land improvements, section 1245, "allowed or allowable", the lesser-of rule, and allocation. Cost segregation is used here but only explained two paragraphs later ("Cost segregation matters at this step").
- OP.12.5 vacation-home caveat: it gives two specifics (24 months, 14 days) and leaves the third vague ("Personal use stays under a limit"). The sources log says all three came from non-IRS summaries. Giving some numbers and not others makes the unverified ones look verified.
- Step 5: "That can be later than the due date of their 2031 return. If it is, the return due date comes first unless they extend." This is a hedge about a date the example itself chose. The learner can't tell which deadline applies.
- Part B: "Return on after-tax equity (cash flow ÷ cash after tax)". The formula is undefined for Hold (÷ $0). For Refinance it would divide cash flow by the cash-out, which isn't a return on equity. For 1031 it's unknown.
- Slide 16 title: "clear this checklist before the sale closes". Two checklist items are "Day 45 written identification" and "Day 180 or return due date", which by definition happen after closing.
- Slide 20 title: "Every tax and exchange rule here traces to an IRS publication or notice". The Rev. Proc. specifics came from non-IRS summaries. The lesson's own sources list says the sale treatment of suspended losses was "not confirmed". Also, a Rev. Proc. is not a "notice".
- Slide 19 title: "Four requests this week give you real numbers for every column". The four requests don't produce the tax estimate, the refinance terms, or anything for the 1031 column.
- Slide 3 title says "four documented inputs", but the slide has five bullets. The furniture/bookings bullet is a sub-point of value shown as its own item.
