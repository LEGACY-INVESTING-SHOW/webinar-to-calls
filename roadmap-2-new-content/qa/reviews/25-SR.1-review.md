# Review: SR.1 Regulatory compliance checklist, by state and city

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The core idea lands fast: "is it legal?" gets a written answer for one address and one plan, and the NYC and LA tests make it concrete. But I was lost in two places. The shorthand for other lessons and for how the course was made ("Sam's 3.2", "PA.2", "STR Concierge", "5.3", "2.4", "canonical example", "brief-provided", "this environment") assumes I've seen things I haven't. And the slides say several things that disagree with each other or with the lesson: the NYC and LA rule slides are stamped "All figures on this slide are assumptions", the email slide says "one topic at a time" and then lists five topics, and the layer count is eight in one place and ten in another. I also can't tell whether "unresolved means don't sign" stops me from making an offer with a contingency, which is exactly what the Cedar Ridge example tells me to do.

The two lines point different ways.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- Slides 12, 13, 15: "All figures on this slide are assumptions". The rows on these slides are official rules with links. The banner tells me the rules are made up, which undercuts the whole lesson. Only the Alder plan (5 guests, 255 nights) is an assumption.
- SR.1.7 "Ask one topic per email where you can" next to a five-question sample email. That's a direct contradiction, and slide 18's title makes it the headline.
- SR.1.2 "'Unresolved' means do not sign ... You do not sign a lease, release a deposit, or waive a contingency" vs SR.1.6 "before anyone signs a purchase contract without a regulatory contingency". The Cedar Ridge sentence is a double negative I had to read three times, and it implies signing a contract *with* a contingency is fine. The rule doesn't say so.
- SR.1.2 Rule 2: "or the landlord has not given written permission" → Prohibited. On a first pass every leased address would come out "Prohibited" before I've even asked. Also unclear for owned property.
- Slide 5 gates: the pill to the right of each question has no "If yes" label. Rule 1 is a question ("Does any official source clearly bar...?") and the pill says "Prohibited". I had to guess that "yes" leads to the pill and "no" moves down to the next rule.
- Slide 8: the third column, "Where the answer lives", says "Official source URL" six times in a row. It tells me nothing the intro sentence didn't.
- Slide 12 "Deciding?" column mixes "Yes", "Plan fails", "Plan fails: 5 guests" and "Not reached". There's no legend.
- SR.1.4 "(canonical example, assumptions)" and "The canonical file leaves the city unnamed on purpose"; SR.1.6 "The canonical file names no state or county"; sources "brief-provided". This is production language, not learner language.
- SR.1.4 NYC "Fee and term" row: "OSE's pages describe a registration term that ends at lease end for tenants. Exact term to verify on the page." It's hedged twice and doesn't give me a usable fact.
- SR.1.2 recheck: "(assumption: a teaching default; use a shorter interval if the city has a pending ordinance)". The parenthetical buries the instruction. Same on slide 6.
- Slide 10 intro text ends with a dangling "Section G:" and puts E and F only in prose. I never see what the E and F tables look like on a slide.
- Slide 13 title "fails on primary residence and the 120-day cap": the lesson says Extended Home-Sharing allows more than 120, so the cap is not the clean failure the title claims.
- Slide 7 row "Nights and channels ... Your underwriting": channels aren't underwriting, and "underwriting" is never explained.
