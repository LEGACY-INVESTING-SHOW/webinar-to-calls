## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The opening idea lands fast. "Can I sublet?" and "Can I run an Airbnb?" are different questions, and slide 2 makes that clear in five seconds. After that the lesson starts to lean on things I never saw. I got L1, L2, H2, H5, C3, C8, "investigate", "canonical", a "stress case" and "sunk setup" with no explanation. By slide 13 the title ("clears L2, and breach and cure clears L1") is pure code to me. The worst snag is "clause 9". It means the lease's sublet clause on slide 10 and the addendum's breach-and-cure clause on slide 11, so I thought the lesson was telling me to ask for the clause that restricts me. ## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"Clause 9" means two different things.** Slide 10 / 3.5.6 Item A: "Lease clause 9" is the sublet restriction. Slide 11 side box / line 143: "Clause 9 of the illustrative addendum (breach and cure)". They sit on back-to-back slides. A viewer who glances at "Clause 9" will think they're being told to ask for the restriction. This is the single biggest confusion in the deck.
- **Scorecard codes as slide titles.** Slide 13: "A signed addendum plus a one-page summary clears L2, and breach and cure clears L1". Without lesson 3.4 the title teaches nothing. It fails the five-second test.
- **"Canonical" on screen.** Slide 11: "Sunk setup cost (canonical)". Slide 12: "(canonical)" twice. Lines 21, 143, 155, 158. It's an internal production label. To a learner it sounds like a technical term they should know.
- **Ten topics versus ten clauses.** The 3.5.3 topics and the 3.5.5 sample clauses are both numbered 1–10, but the numbers don't line up (topic 8 = clause 4, topic 9 sits inside clause 7, clause 9 is breach and cure, clause 10 is authority). Part C then asks for "Where (clause number)".
- **The worksheet contradicts itself.** "Fill in every row before you sign anything" (line 170) and "Covered in signed document? (Y/N)" (line 192, slide 15). As written, every honest answer is N.
- **The sample addendum appears before its subject.** Line 107 uses Alder Street's keypad, laundry and parking before line 129 says "Back to Unit 4B, 218 Alder Street".
- **"Most arbitrage clauses sound permissive but give you nothing until someone signs"** (slide 8 title). "Arbitrage clauses" isn't a thing; these are ordinary lease clauses. "Most" is unsupported. And the last two rows of the same table are consent language, so "give you nothing" isn't true of every row.
- **"mixing them up is how people end up in court"** (line 17, slide 2 notes). This is dramatic and unsourced. Nothing in the lesson shows a case.
- **"Here's where most arbitrage leases go wrong"** (line 86, slide 8 notes). The claim is unsupported.
- **Slide 4 as a flow.** The arrows imply steps in order, but the four parts are simultaneous conditions ("If any one is missing"). The "This fails if" box only illustrates Part 1, while the label reads as if it covers the whole slide.
- **Slide 9 title.** "An explicit addendum puts compliance on you" reads like a downside. The lesson means it as reassurance to the owner, and never says so on the slide.
- **Slide 3 last cell.** "Nothing for a whole-unit arbitrage plan" can be read two ways (see Q4).
- **Section order loses the thread.** 3.5.2 teaches how to judge a lease. 3.5.3 and 3.5.4 move to what to put in writing and what to disclose. Then 3.5.5 goes back to judging lease clauses. Nothing at the top of 3.5.5 tells me why we went back.
- **"Sunk setup cost"** (slide 11). "Sunk" is finance jargon. I need "money already spent on furniture, supplies, photos and the permit application, which you don't get back".
