# Review: PA.9 Due Diligence: STR-Specific Inspections, Appraisal & Title Review

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The first half (PA.9.1 to PA.9.7) is clear and useful: order the work by what can kill the deal, ask "is it sound" and "can it run the business," hand the inspector an STR list, read the title commitment. Then PA.9.8 turns into a wall of numbers that assumes I already know Cedar Ridge, Sam, B1, "Test 1," "Test 3," "the $10,000 floor," "the $15,342 property reserve," "the $4,000 limit," and "0.3." I could not rebuild the rerun from what this lesson tells me. Some parts of the worked example contradict each other. The "first draft" says the buyers pay "all the repairs" but leaves out the $1,850 septic job. A slide says the four new costs are ones "PA.4's generic lines don't cover," and two slides later three of them are paid from PA.4's lines. The final move from failing to passing reads like moving numbers between budget lines until the rules pass, and the lesson never tells me directly why that's OK.

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. PA.9.0 / cover slide: "the underwriting you brought from Sam's PA.4." Who is Sam, and what's in that package? I don't have one.
2. PA.9.2: "at his guest count and price." What is his guest count and price? I only find out it's 8 guests in PA.9.4, and I never learn the nightly price.
3. PA.9.3 and PA.9.8: What is Cedar Ridge? I have to work out that it's a lake house on a well and septic from the list items. I only work out that it has 3 bedrooms from row 1's cap formula and row 4's "3-bedroom design."
4. PA.9.4, fifth test: "Check that number against three limits: the septic permit's bedroom count, any occupancy cap..., and what the insurer will write." The septic limit is in bedrooms and the others are in guests. How do I compare them? The lesson never converts bedrooms to guests, except inside the row 1 cell ("2 per bedroom plus 2").
5. PA.9.5: "At 75%, the loan drops to $281,250." Why 75%? Where does the 75% come from?
6. PA.9.5: "Points fall to $2,812, so closing costs become $14,362." What are points? How were closing costs built? I can't get from $2,812 to $14,362. (Slide 8's notes say "Points are 1% of a smaller loan," but the lesson doesn't.)
7. PA.9.5: "the canonical $110,688." Canonical according to whom? What is that figure made of?
8. PA.9.6: "On an investment property with about $96,000 of your own cash in it." PA.9.8 says their money in the deal is $142,688, and cash to close is $110,688. Which one is "my own cash"?
9. PA.9.8 tracker row 2: insurance quote $3,200 a year. Why isn't it in the "Added annual cost" table? Is it already in the PA.4 budget? The lesson never says. (B1 in `reference/recurring-examples.md` does carry STR insurance at $3,200, but the learner can't see that.)
10. PA.9.8: "The weak case is -$5,475." Then "First-rerun weak-case cash flow: $18,162 - $3,820 - $23,637." Where did $18,162 come from? It's the first time I see it.
11. PA.9.8: "DSCR debt service of $23,637" and the table's "Base NOI / annual P&I." Are debt service and P&I the same number? What is P&I? What is DSCR?
12. PA.9.8: "The first draft of this tracker had the buyers paying for all the repairs: $350 + $1,650 + $2,300 + $380 + $275 = $4,955." The $1,850 septic repair isn't there. So was it "all the repairs" or not?
13. PA.9.8: "PA.8 left Maya and Chris $11,020 of unallocated cash after their Test 3 set-asides." What is Test 3? What were the set-asides?
14. PA.9.8: "They still save $3,500 a month (assumption, from 0.3)." What is "0.3"?
15. PA.9.8, buy-box check: "The $2,955 punch list is under their $4,000 limit." What's a punch list? Where did the $4,000 limit come from? The same applies to "cash to close plus furnishing stays $142,688": what furnishing?
16. PA.9.8: "Any other capital bill... comes from the $15,342 property reserve." What property reserve? This is its first and only mention.
17. PA.9.8: "It's $2,735 under the $10,000 floor they set in PA.8.6." Is being under the floor a failure or not? The lesson says they'll refill it by saving before closing. Is that allowed under their own rule?
18. PA.9.8: Tracker deadlines are title Day 15, survey Day 18, appraisal Day 21, but the inspection contingency is 10 days. If the title had turned up a problem on Day 15, which contingency would have let them walk? When is the "proceed" decision dated: Day 10, or after the appraisal?
19. PA.9.8: "The first rerun is the cautious version." Later: "So the final budget counts each item once." Did the first rerun count things twice (an error) or was it just cautious (a choice)? These are different stories.
20. PA.9.9 exercise: I don't have a house under contract or a PA.4 package. How do I start?
21. PA.9.9 rerun box: The weak-case cash flow row needs a weak-case NOI, but the box has no weak-case NOI or weak-case operating cost row. Where do I compute it?
22. PA.9.11: "Bring your lender's written answer on whether it lends to an LLC." Why would that matter? LLC is never defined, and the link to "whose name goes on the deed" is only implied.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- PA.9.8: "Four findings add recurring annual costs that PA.4's generic lines don't name." Slide 12's title makes it stronger: "that PA.4's generic lines don't cover." Three pages later the lesson pays three of the four from PA.4's capital reserve and repairs lines. The slide title and the resolution contradict each other.
- PA.9.8: "The first rerun is the cautious version. It stacks all four on top of PA.4's budget." ... "So the final budget counts each item once." The second sentence implies the first rerun double-counted. If it did, it wasn't "cautious," it was wrong. If it didn't, the final budget is a choice to spend reserve money, not a correction. As a learner, I can't tell which. It reads like moving numbers until the rules pass.
- PA.9.8 ordering in the Markdown: the story goes cash test (first draft fails) → renegotiation → buy-box check → reread budget → final numbers → cash test again. The deck goes new costs → rerun → buy-box → final budget → cash test with three columns. Neither version keeps the renegotiation beat clean.
- PA.9.8: "The seller did agree to make repairs before closing: the $1,850 septic work plus the $2,000 well treatment." The buyers' repair bill only drops by $2,000 ($4,955 → $2,955). The septic was never in the buyers' first draft. So the renegotiation "win" on the septic changes nothing in the math, and the reader is left wondering where the $1,850 went.
- Tracker table (PA.9.8) and slide 11 show final decisions ("Asked for a roof credit. Seller declined."; "Seller treats and installs UV before closing"). The prose then tells a "first draft" story where buyers paid for the well. The table spoils the story and contradicts the draft.
- PA.9.7: "The decision column only has four answers." Slide 10 draws them as 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 with arrows, as if they were steps. They're alternatives.
- Slide 2: "In parallel" sits in a box connected by an arrow, so it reads as a step after inspections, not alongside them.
- PA.9.9 rule 6 / slide 18 gate 4: "Changing the budget or the rule counts only in writing, with the reason." This is the only hint that the Cedar Ridge budget move is legitimate, and it comes after the example.
- Slide 18 title: "before the inspection window closes." Gate 5 (cash test) and the appraisal-dependent numbers can't all be known by Day 10 in the Cedar Ridge timeline.
- Slide 13: the negative weak-case values (-$5,475, -$9,295) are drawn as bars growing right, the same as positive values. -$9,295 looks like a bigger, better bar than -$5,475. NOI and cash flow also share one scale.
- Slide 16 title: "turn a $1,575 cash shortfall into $7,265 spare." The box underneath says $7,265 is "$2,735 under their $10,000 floor." "Spare" oversells it.
