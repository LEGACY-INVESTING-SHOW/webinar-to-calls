# Review: PA.1 Discovery: Defining Your Buy Box (Market, Property Type, Price Range, Goals)

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The idea lands fast: write your rules down before you fall in love with a listing, and slide 2's "$380,000 house turns into a $450,000 house with a pool" drift example is memorable. After that, the lesson leans on vocabulary and earlier lessons I have not seen (weak case, base case, NOI, P&I, PITIA, DSCR, ADR, points, "0.3 scorecard", "cash stack", "canonical file"). By the worked example I can follow the arithmetic but not where the inputs come from ($115,000, $145,000, $23,637, -$5,475, -$6,000, 1.20). The word "pass" confused me most. In the pass rule it means "walk away", but in the test tables "Pass? Yes" means "the house cleared the check", and on slide 6 a pink "Pass" box sits next to every question. I also caught the lesson contradicting itself on the price ceiling: $393,000 in one place, and a tighter $392,000 in another that it calls "agreeing".

## Questions I had (numbered; quote the line; say where)

1. "Pass" (PA.1.5 "If a property fails one, you pass"; slide 6 pink "Pass" labels; PA.1.7 tables "Pass? Yes"). Does "pass" mean I decline the house, or that the house passed? The lesson uses it both ways.
2. "Their ceiling is **$393,000 contract price**" (PA.1.7) and later "the weak case reaches -$6,000 at about $392,000 ... a good sign that the two rules agree" (PA.1.7, slide 9 side box). Section C of the template says the price ceiling is the "Lowest of the three". Isn't their ceiling $392,000, then? At $392,500, is the house in the box or out?
3. "At $385,000, Cedar Ridge fits the box, subject to the two pending checks" (PA.1.7 Test 1, slide 11 title). Hard requirements 3 (lender fit in writing) and 6 (self-management with a named cleaner plus backup) are not in the Test 1 table. Are they passed, pending, or forgotten?
4. "Rank | Goal ... 1 | Protect the household" (PA.1.7 goal table, slide 8). "Protect the household" is not one of the six goals in PA.1.2. Where did it come from? And "Desired involvement" is one of the six goals, but it is not ranked in their table; it is a caption underneath.
5. "Maximum cash to close: $115,000 (assumption). Maximum total cash in, including $32,000 of furnishing: $145,000" (PA.1.7). If $145,000 includes $32,000 of furnishing, only $113,000 is left for closing. Why is the cash-to-close cap $115,000? Why do I need two caps if one follows from the other?
6. Same place: how did they get $145,000? PA.1.3 says "Both must leave your property reserve and personal emergency savings untouched", but the lesson never shows the subtraction. (The course example file gives $210,000 liquid savings. $210,000 - $51,000 emergency - 6 x $2,557 PITIA = $143,658, which is *below* the $145,000 cap. Please confirm.)
7. "Max cash to close | Down payment + closing costs + points | 0.3 cash stack" (PA.1.8 section B, slide 14). That is the definition of cash to close for a given price, not how to find my *maximum*. How do I compute my cap before I have a price?
8. "Weak-case annual cash flow after principal and interest: no worse than -$6,000" (PA.1.7). What is the "weak case"? Why -$6,000 and not -$3,000 or -$10,000? How do I choose my own number in section E line 5?
9. "Base-case NOI divided by annual principal and interest: at least 1.20" (PA.1.7). What is NOI? Why 1.20?
10. "Base NOI / annual P&I | $29,209 / $23,637 = 1.24" (Test 1, slide 11). Where does $23,637 come from? The loan amount and term are never stated in the lesson.
11. "Test 1: Cedar Ridge at the contract price, $385,000" (PA.1.7). What is Cedar Ridge, and who are Maya and Chris? Also: the lesson says the buy box is "written before you see listings", yet the test starts from a contract price that has already been negotiated. How would I know a contract price before making an offer?
12. "Pass on any listing ... whose price is above $393,000 unless the seller has shown they'll accept a price at or under it" (PA.1.7 buy box table, slide 10). How does a seller "show" that before I make an offer? Am I allowed to offer on a $399,000 listing or not?
13. "Or a preference score below a stated level" (PA.1.6, slide 7 Trigger box). What preference score? Nothing in the lesson or the template scores preferences.
14. "the canonical file shows three different ratios for the same house" (PA.1.7). What are they, and why should I care here? It's a teaser that goes nowhere.
15. "In 0.2, a full-service co-host at 18% turned Cedar Ridge from $5,572 a year of cash flow to -$7,798" (PA.1.2, slide 3). 18% of what? (The example file says 18% of booking revenue.)
16. "On the DSCR terms, cash to close is 25% down plus 3% closing costs" (PA.1.7). Is 3% an assumption or from the example file? It isn't marked either way.
17. "$325,000 (assumption) to $393,000" (PA.1.7 price range). Why have a price floor at all, and why $325,000? PA.1.4 says to set "a floor and a ceiling" but only explains the ceiling.
18. "Confirm with: your CPA before tax planning is ranked above 4" (PA.1.9, slide 17). Does "above 4" mean ranks 1 to 3? Why 4, when PA.1.2 says only the top two or three shape the search?
19. "Apply hard requirement 1 to each market Sam screens" (PA.1.10, slide 16). Requirement 1 is "Legal STR use at the exact address". How do I apply an address-level test to a whole market?
20. "Take the buy box through Sam's four demos, and bring each result back to PA.6" (slide 16 title). What results do I bring back to PA.6? The lesson only mentions the financing line.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- **"Pass" means two opposite things.** "If a property fails one, you pass" (PA.1.5) means walk away. The "Pass?" column in Tests 1 and 2 (slides 11 and 12) means "cleared the check", and "Yes" is good. Slide 6 then shows a pink "Pass" box beside each gate with no "if no" label, which a new viewer reads as "passed". "Test 3: a pass example" means a rejected house. This is the lesson's central verb, so the confusion spreads to the pass rule, the pass log, and slide 13.
- **Two price ceilings.** "The tighter cap wins. Their ceiling is $393,000" contradicts "the weak case reaches -$6,000 at about $392,000". The section C rule ("Lowest of the three") gives $392,000. Calling the two numbers "agree[ing]" (PA.1.7, slide 9 side box "The downside rule agrees") hides a $1,000 disagreement that the lesson's own rule resolves the other way.
- **"fits the box, subject to the two pending checks"** (PA.1.7 Test 1). The table omits two of the six hard requirements, so "fits the box" overstates the result.
- **Timeline of the worked example.** The lesson says to write the box before seeing listings, then tests it on a house already "at the contract price". The learner can't tell whether this is a retrospective check or a live one.
- **"after principal and interest" vs "after debt service" vs "after debt service and reserves".** PA.1.5 says "weak-case annual cash flow after debt service". PA.1.7 says "after principal and interest". Goal 3 says "positive after debt service and reserves". Are these the same number? (In the example file, base cash flow $5,572 = NOI $29,209 - P&I $23,637, with the capital reserve already inside NOI.)
- **"Hypothetical canonical quote (7.25%, 1 point, 25% down)"** (PA.1.7 constraints). "Hypothetical" and "canonical" in one phrase reads as a contradiction to a newcomer. The loan term (30-year fixed in the example file) is missing, so the P&I can't be reproduced.
- **"For example, set a floor on weak-case annual cash flow after debt service. Then add a minimum base-case ratio..."** (PA.1.5 item 5). "For example" makes it optional, and "Then add" makes it sound required. Which is it?
- **Two cash caps** (PA.1.3, PA.1.7). The lesson never says why cash to close and total cash in need separate caps. In the example, the second cap is always the binding one.
- **Slide 6 sub-line: "Use the SR.1 checklist and record the official source (Airbnb, article 1397)".** The parenthetical reads as if the Airbnb article is the official source. It isn't: Airbnb only tells hosts to check local rules.
- **Slide 15 order.** The "Then finish the page: D ... E ... F ... G" sentence sits *above* the section C table, so the viewer reads "then" before the thing it follows.
- **Slide 16 placement.** The "next lessons" flow (PA.1.10) appears before "Who confirms what" (PA.1.9) and the do-this list (PA.1.8), and slide 18 repeats the next-lesson line. The lesson seems to end, restart, and end again.
