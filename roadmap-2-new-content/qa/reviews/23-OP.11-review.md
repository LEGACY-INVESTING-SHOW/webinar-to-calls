# Review: OP.11 Scaling Your Portfolio: Refinancing, HELOCs & Property #2

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The opening idea ("start with a bank statement, not a listing") is clear, and I understood the verdict at the end: not yet. The middle is where I lost the thread. From OP.11.6 on I kept hitting labels I have never seen (B1, B2, B4, B7, DSCR, PITIA, ADR, PA.9, PA.12, "45-day launch carry", "septic program and the UV lamp"), and several numbers I could not check because the inputs they depend on (underwritten ADR and occupancy, the purchase price, the original loan rate, the cleaning fee) are not in this lesson. The cash-reserve test that decides the case is only really built in the Markdown and in the slide 15 speaker notes; on screen I just see "$37,783.50 left vs $81,684" with no build-up. I would finish the deck knowing the answer but not able to reproduce the reserve test or fill Part B of the worksheet for my own house.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

Then line 131 recomputes NOI and cash flow with the $400 anyway. Having two treatments of one $400 item, in two places, makes me doubt every table. Handle it once, after the table.
- **Slide 10 bar chart.** The title says "8.4% revenue miss became a 22.6% NOI miss", but the chart shows cash flow, not NOI. All four bars grow rightward from the same baseline, so "-$1,022" looks like a small positive bar and the "-$6,211" bar is longer than the "$5,572" bar. It doesn't read as a walk from +$5,572 down to -$1,022.
- **"Most costs stay put when revenue drops, so the miss grows on the way down" (line 125).** This is the key idea, but in the same table costs *rose* $383. The notes on slide 10 explain it better ("Insurance, property tax, software, the handyman retainer... don't shrink"). The Markdown needs that sentence.
- **"$15,725 ... is not enough for a second down payment. Cedar Ridge's total cash in was $142,688" (line 151; slide 12 box).** "Down payment" and "total cash in" are different things. A learner will compare $15,725 to the wrong target.
- **Step 3 subtracts HELOC interest from property two's cash flow, while Step 2 charges the refinance to property one.** That's reasonable, but the lesson never says why the two costs land on different properties. Part B of the worksheet then puts both on property one.
- **Terminology drift for the downside case:** "a bad season" (OP.11.0), "the low season" (OP.11.3), "weak-case NOI", "a weak year", "slow season" (slide 2 notes), "Underwritten weak (B2)", "weak case (B4)". A no-context learner can't tell whether these are one thing or several. "Low season" is a few months; "weak case" is a whole year.
- **Slide 9 omits four cost rows** (handyman, supplies, software, property tax). The caption says so, but a viewer who adds up the visible rows won't reach -$45,066 and will think the table is wrong.
- **Slide 15 "$37,783.50 left vs $81,684".** The $81,684 is never shown as $30,684 + $51,000 on any slide. The Markdown row says "$43,900.50 short" instead. The two framings of one test don't match.
- **The "Not yet" rule appears in three wordings:** "one 'No' in the first three rows means not yet" (line 248), "Any No in the first three rows" (slide 15), and "Every test passes" for Proceed. That leaves rows 4-7 undefined: if only the team test fails, is it Proceed or Not yet?
- **Slide 8** runs the eight OP.7 roles and five "systems" together in one checklist. "Supplies" (a role) sits at the top of the right column directly above the systems, so it reads as a system. The title only mentions roles.
