Slide 1 · Set your walk-away price from conservative NOI before you see the seller's number

This lesson is about one number you set before you ever talk to the seller: your walk-away price.

By the end you'll build that number from three things. Conservative net operating income. Your loan terms. And the cash you actually have. Then you'll learn the contract terms that protect it: earnest money, contingencies, appraisal gap, seller concessions, closing date, and furniture.

And you'll learn a habit. Every negotiation decision gets recorded as proceed, renegotiate, or walk, with the reason in one line.

We stay with Cedar Ridge. The asking price is $399,000. Maya and Chris end up under contract at $385,000. You'll see exactly why $399,000 fails their tests and $385,000 passes. All Cedar Ridge figures are assumptions from our canonical example.

Slide 2 · Decide the most you'll pay first, then negotiate below that number

The asking price is the seller's opening position. It tells you what the seller hopes to get. It says nothing about what the house can carry as a rental.

A common way STR offers go wrong is working backward from the asking price. The buyer sees $399,000, then nudges the nightly rate up until the spreadsheet works. Now the offer depends on the most optimistic line in the model.

Reverse the order. Decide the most you'll pay first, from numbers you'd defend to a lender. Then negotiate below that number. If the seller won't come under it, you walk, and you already know why.

Sam's PA.4 lesson builds the underwriting package: revenue, expenses, cap rate, and cash-on-cash. I'm not redoing that here. What you bring back from PA.4 is two lines: base-case NOI and weak-case NOI. This lesson turns those two lines into a price.

PA.8 · Offer Strategy & Negotiation§ PA.8.23 / 19
Slide 3 · Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings, run on the costlier loan

Your walk-away price is the lowest of three ceilings.

Test one is base-case cash flow. At your expected revenue, the house must produce at least a minimum annual cash flow after the loan payment. You set the minimum. It should be more than zero, because base-case years still bring surprises.

Test two is weak-case survival. In a weak year, the house should break even. If it can't, you need a written, funded plan for the shortfall. A buyer who can't fund a weak year should hold the strict breakeven line.

Test three is cash. Down payment, closing costs, points, furnishing, lender reserves, a weak-year reserve, and your emergency fund. It all has to fit.

Use the more expensive financing quote for all three. That's the DSCR quote for Maya and Chris.

Why cash flow and not cap rate? Cap rate uses NOI but ignores your loan. Cash flow shows what the loan does to that NOI.

Slide 4 · Keeping $5,000 of base-case cash flow caps Cedar Ridge's price at $394,310

Canonical inputs: base-case NOI $29,209, weak-case NOI $18,162. Property tax and insurance are already inside NOI. Maya and Chris have $210,000 in liquid savings.

Test one. Because NOI already covers tax and insurance, cash flow is simply NOI minus principal and interest.

They want at least $5,000 a year in the base case. So the most principal and interest they can carry is $29,209 minus $5,000. That's $24,209 a year, or $2,017 a month.

At 7.25% over 30 years, $2,017 a month supports a loan of about $295,732.

With 25% down, the loan is 75% of the price. Divide by 0.75 and the price ceiling is $394,310.

I ran every step in Python. On your own deal, a lender's calculator or a spreadsheet PV function does the same job. We'll get to that in the worksheet.

Test two, strict version. Breakeven in the weak case means principal and interest can't be more than weak-case NOI.

So the most they can carry is $18,162 a year. That supports a loan of about $221,863. Divide by 0.75 and the price ceiling is $295,818.

Could more cash close the gap instead? Let's check. At $385,000, a breakeven loan of $221,863 means a down payment of $163,137. That's 42.4% of the price. Add closing costs and the $32,000 furnishing budget, and their $210,000 is gone.

Slide 6 · The lowest ceiling, the funded weak-case test, sets the walk-away price at $393,000

Maya and Chris have two W-2 incomes and savings. So they decide, in writing, that they can carry a weak-year shortfall of up to $6,000 a year. And they set aside two years of it.

PA.8 · Offer Strategy & Negotiation§ PA.8.49 / 19
Slide 9 · Seven contract terms decide how much risk you carry between signing and closing

Price is one term. These decide how much risk you carry between signing and closing. Purchase contracts are state-specific, often a standard form your agent uses. So treat all of this as questions for your agent and attorney.

Earnest money counts toward your cash to close. A bigger deposit can make an offer stronger. It also raises what you could lose if you miss a deadline.

The inspection period is where you test the business, not just the house: permits, HOA rules, insurance quotes, septic capacity. PA.9 builds that list.

Lenders generally size the loan on the lower of price or appraised value. Ask yours to confirm. If you offer gap coverage, only offer cash you already counted in Test three.

Seller credits reduce your cash to close, but lenders limit them. Get the limit in dollars first.

For furniture, ask if it fits your OP.6 design plan, and how your lender treats it. For existing bookings, ask whether the platform even allows a transfer.

Before you waive or shorten

Get written answers from your agent and lender before you shorten any contingency

Ask your agent

  • Can we still fit the inspection, septic or sewer check, and STR insurance quote?
  • What happens to my earnest money if I cancel after this expires?
  • What's normal here for this type of house?
  • Can the permit answer arrive before inspection ends?

Ask your lender

  • How many days do you really need to approve this loan?
  • What happens if the appraisal comes in $10,000 low?
  • Does approval depend on proof of STR permit eligibility?
  • What's your seller credit limit for this occupancy?
  • When does my rate lock expire, and what does extending cost?
Keep the contingency if

Any answer puts your earnest money at risk for a problem you can't test in time.

PA.8 · Offer Strategy & Negotiation§ PA.8.510 / 19
Slide 10 · Get written answers from your agent and lender before you shorten any contingency

Sellers sometimes ask for a shorter inspection period, no financing contingency, or appraisal gap coverage. Before you agree, get written answers to these questions.

From your agent: can you still fit the inspection, the septic or sewer check, and the STR insurance quote in a shorter window? What happens to your earnest money if you cancel after the contingency expires? What's normal in this market for this type of house? And can the permit or licensing answer arrive before inspection ends?

From your lender: how many days does it really need? What happens if the appraisal comes in $10,000 low? Does approval depend on proof of STR permit eligibility, and when? What's the seller credit limit? And when does the rate lock expire?

The rule is simple. If any answer puts your earnest money at risk for a problem you can't test in time, keep the contingency.

Slide 16 · Your walk-away price is the lowest of your three ceilings, rounded down

Here are the three ceilings, in formula form.

Test one: base NOI minus your minimum cash flow gives your maximum annual principal and interest. Turn that into a loan amount, then divide by your loan-to-price. At 25% down, that's 0.75.

Test two works the same way, starting from weak NOI plus your allowed shortfall. If you hold the strict rule, the allowed shortfall is zero.

Test three is the highest price where total committed cash stays at or under your liquid cash.

To turn a payment into a loan amount, use your lender's calculator or the PV function in a spreadsheet. PV of rate over 12, number of months, and the negative monthly payment.
