Slide 1 · Hand off everything the closing team learned

You own the house. This lesson makes it ready to become a rental.

You'll confirm the right insurance, move every utility and account into your control, and finish the safety work before anyone takes a photo. The main output is a dated operations handoff packet. It tells the launch team everything the closing team learned, including what's still open.

Slide 2 · The two teams rarely talk, so write it down

Up to now, your team was the closing team: agent, lender, inspector, title company, attorney. Now it's the launch team: insurer, cleaner, handyman, furnishing help, and maybe a co-host.

These two teams rarely talk. The inspector knows the dock railing is loose. The cleaner doesn't. The seller told you the septic company's name at the walkthrough. Your co-host will never hear it unless you write it down.

The handoff packet is how that knowledge crosses over. Every open item gets an owner and a date.

On your first property you're probably both teams. Write the packet anyway. The version of you answering a guest at 11 p.m. in February needs it.

  • Named insured matches how you took title
  • Short-term rental use covered, in writing
  • Liability limit covers the dock and water
  • Deductibles and exclusions
  • Loss of rental income terms
  • Insurer's conditions met, confirmed in writing
Slide 3 · Read your policy as the operator, not the lender

You bound a policy before closing because the lender required it. The lender cared that the house was insured. You care whether a guest injury, a burst pipe, or a month without bookings is covered.

Pull the declarations page and the policy form. The named insured has to match how you took title in PA.10. The effective date should be the closing date or earlier. A standard homeowners policy may exclude business use, so get the insurer's answer on rental use in writing.

Check the liability limit for the dock and the water. Note the deductibles, read the exclusions, and check loss-of-rental-income terms.

For Cedar Ridge, the insurer required the dock railing and lower-level alarms within 30 days. Send proof and get written confirmation.

Your own policy has to carry the full load

  • AirCover damage protection isn't insurance
  • Vrbo and direct stays aren't Airbnb stays
  • Most homeowners policies exclude flood
  • NFIP flood policies generally have a 30-day wait
Slide 4 · Your own policy has to carry the full load

Now the platform. Airbnb's AirCover for Hosts includes host damage protection and host liability insurance. Airbnb says the damage protection isn't an insurance policy, not all damage is included, and AirCover isn't a substitute for your own insurance.

It also covers Airbnb stays. A guest who books on Vrbo or your direct site isn't an Airbnb stay. So your own policy carries the whole load, and AirCover is a backup that may help on some Airbnb claims.

Then flood. FEMA says most homeowners insurance doesn't cover flood. The national flood program generally has a 30-day waiting period, with some exceptions. If you skipped flood at closing, the clock may start the day you buy.

Get your insurer's answers in writing, including how the policy handles non-Airbnb bookings.

Slide 5 · Take control of every account in week one

Transfer every account into your name or your entity's name in the first week. Use the meter readings from the PA.11 walkthrough so the bills split cleanly.

Electric and propane go on autopay, with a propane delivery schedule. The well has no bill, but note the shutoff and when the UV lamp is due. Schedule the septic program, confirm trash pickup, and set up mail forwarding and plowing.

Test internet speed in the bedrooms. The smart lock gives each stay and each vendor its own code. Remove the seller's codes.

Airbnb doesn't allow cameras that monitor indoor spaces, even if they're off. Outdoor cameras and noise monitors must be disclosed.

Put every login in a password manager. Don't email or text credentials, and turn on two-factor sign-in.

Slide 7 · File the permit and lodging tax on day 2

Diligence told you whether this address can run a rental. Now you file.

For Cedar Ridge, PA.9 found the seller's permit doesn't transfer. So Maya and Chris apply as new owners on day 2.

The same day, they register for lodging tax. That's separate from the permit, and it happens before the first stay. Lesson 3.3 in Part One shows how collection works on each booking channel.

Finish the safety work before any photos

Right after closing

  • Smoke and CO alarms working
  • Dock railing repaired
  • Clear exit from every bedroom
  • Clean well retest

During furnishing

  • At least one fire extinguisher
  • TVs and furniture anchored
  • Pool or spa drain covers
Slide 8 · Finish the safety work before any photos

Don't photograph a house you haven't finished fixing. Photos lock in what guests expect, and a listing can't promise a dock that isn't safe yet.

Start with the punch list from due diligence. Then walk the house against Airbnb's safety requirements. If smoke and carbon monoxide detectors are present, they must work and be tested. Blocked exits, exposed wiring, mold, and pests are on its hazard list.

For Cedar Ridge, the first group is the dock railing, the lower-level alarms, the well retest, and a clear exit from every bedroom.

The Consumer Product Safety Commission's rental tips add the second group: an extinguisher, anchored furniture, and drain covers. OP.6 builds those into furnishing.

Both groups finish before photos, on a one-page sign-off.

Here's the exercise. Build the operations handoff packet and date it. Update the date every time you change it. If you haven't closed yet, fill it in for Cedar Ridge first, then copy the blank version for your own property.

Slide 13 · Three rows must say done before you list

The packet comes with three rules.

First, the listing doesn't go up until three rows say done. Insurance, with rental use and the conditions confirmed in writing. The permit, meaning issued, or the licensing office confirmed in writing that you may list first. And the safety sign-off.

Second, every open issue has one owner and one due date. 'We' is not an owner. If a row says we, it belongs to nobody.

Third, update the packet version whenever a vendor, a code, or a policy changes. A packet with the old cleaner's number sends your next call to the wrong person.
