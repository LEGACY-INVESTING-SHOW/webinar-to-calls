Insurance

Read your policy as the operator, checking nine items the lender never asked about

  • Named insured matches your PA.10 decision
  • Effective date is the closing date or earlier
  • Covers short-term rental to paying guests, in writing
  • Liability limit per occurrence, including dock, water, and watercraft
  • Deductibles: all-perils, plus any wind, hail, or water deductible
  • Exclusions: slow leaks, mold, pools and hot tubs, 'business' use
  • Loss of rental income: when it pays, how it's calculated, for how long
  • Flood: your FEMA zone from PA.9 and whether you have a policy
  • Conditions: proof of railing and alarms sent, and confirmed in writing
PA.12 · Post-Close Checklist§ PA.12.23 / 19
Slide 3 · Read your policy as the operator, checking nine items the lender never asked about

You bound a policy before closing, because the lender required it. Now read it as the operator. The lender cared that the house was insured. You care whether a guest injury, a burst pipe, or a month without bookings is covered.

Pull the declarations page and the policy form. Check these nine items.

The named insured has to match your PA.10 decision. The effective date has to be the closing date or earlier, because any gap is uninsured time. A standard homeowners policy may exclude business use, so get the insurer's answer on rental use in writing.

Check the liability limit, especially for the dock and the water. Note the deductibles. Read the exclusions. Check loss-of-rental-income coverage: when it pays, how it calculates lost income, and for how long.

And the conditions. PA.9's insurer wanted the railing and alarms done within 30 days. Send proof and get written confirmation.

AirCover and flood

AirCover is a backup on some Airbnb claims, so your own policy carries the full load

What Airbnb says AirCover includes

Airbnb, AirCover for hosts
  • Host damage protection and host liability insurance
  • Screening and a safety line
  • Protection while you're hosting an Airbnb stay

What Airbnb says it isn't

Same page.
  • Host damage protection isn't an insurance policy
  • Not all damage is included
  • Not a substitute for your own insurance
  • A Vrbo or direct booking isn't an Airbnb stay
Flood has a waiting period

The NFIP generally has a 30-day wait, with exceptions such as buying when you make, increase, extend, or renew a mortgage. If you skipped flood at closing, the clock may start the day you buy.

PA.12 · Post-Close Checklist§ PA.12.24 / 19
Slide 4 · AirCover is a backup on some Airbnb claims, so your own policy carries the full load

Now the platform. Airbnb's AirCover for Hosts includes host damage protection and host liability insurance, along with screening and a safety line.

Airbnb also says host damage protection isn't an insurance policy, and not all damage is included. It says AirCover isn't a substitute for your own insurance, and suggests talking to your insurer about overlap.

Airbnb describes AirCover as protection while you're hosting an Airbnb stay. A guest who books on Vrbo, or on your direct site from OP.3, isn't an Airbnb stay. Your own policy has to carry the whole load. Treat AirCover as a backup that may help on some Airbnb claims.

One more on flood. FEMA says most homeowners insurance doesn't cover flood. The NFIP generally has a 30-day waiting period, with exceptions, including buying when you make or renew a mortgage. If you skipped flood at closing, the clock may start the day you buy.

Devices and credentials

Airbnb bans indoor cameras, even switched off, and requires disclosure of outdoor ones

Allowed, with rules

Airbnb, article 3061
  • Smart home devices, with disclosure encouraged
  • Outdoor cameras, disclosed with general location before booking
  • Noise decibel monitors in common areas only, disclosed

Not allowed

Same article.
  • Cameras that monitor indoor spaces, even if they're off
  • If the seller left an indoor camera, remove it
Credentials

Every login goes in a password manager with shared access. Don't email or text credentials. Turn on two-factor sign-in for utility, internet, lock, and platform accounts.

PA.12 · Post-Close Checklist§ PA.12.36 / 19
Slide 6 · Airbnb bans indoor cameras, even switched off, and requires disclosure of outdoor ones

Devices come with platform rules, so check them before you install anything.

Airbnb allows smart home devices and encourages you to disclose them. Outdoor cameras must be disclosed, with their general location, before booking. Noise decibel monitors are allowed only in common areas, and must be disclosed.

Airbnb doesn't allow cameras that monitor indoor spaces, even if they're turned off. If the seller left one, take it down.

Now credentials. Put every login in a password manager, with shared access for anyone who needs it. Don't email credentials or send them by text. The CFPB's closing-scam guidance warns that email isn't a secure way to send financial information. Treat account logins the same way.

Turn on two-factor sign-in for the utility, internet, lock, and platform accounts. It takes an afternoon and closes an easy door.

Safety sign-off

Finish the safety work before photos, because photos set what guests expect

  • Smoke and CO detectors: disclosed, working, tested regularly (Airbnb)
  • CO alarms on every level, outside sleeping areas (CPSC)
  • At least one fire extinguisher (CPSC)
  • TVs and furniture anchored to the wall (CPSC)
  • Pool and spa drain covers that meet federal standards (CPSC)
  • No blocked exits, exposed wiring, mold, pests, or contaminated water (Airbnb)
  • Instructions for features that could injure if misused: dock, lake, stairs
Cedar Ridge sign-off

One page: each item, who checked it, the date, and a photo. Dock railing, lower-level alarms, HVAC service, a clean well retest, and a clear exit from every bedroom.

PA.12 · Post-Close Checklist§ PA.12.69 / 19
Slide 9 · Finish the safety work before photos, because photos set what guests expect

Don't photograph a house you haven't finished fixing. Photos lock in what guests expect, and a listing can't promise a deck that isn't safe yet.

Start with the PA.9 punch list. Then walk the house against Airbnb's safety requirements. Airbnb says hosts must disclose whether smoke and CO detectors are present, and if they are, they must work and be tested. Its hazard list includes blocked exits, exposed wiring, poorly maintained hot tubs or furnaces, contaminated water, mold, and pests. Airbnb says it may require proof that issues are fixed before a listing can take reservations.

The CPSC's vacation rental tips add a few practical items: CO alarms on every level, an extinguisher, anchored furniture, and drain covers.

OP.6 builds these into the furnishing plan. Here, you just need the structural and system items done, on a one-page sign-off.

PA.12 · Post-Close Checklist§ PA.12.815 / 19
Slide 15 · Nothing goes live until the insurance, permit, and safety rows say done

The packet has three rules. I've turned them into gates you check before you publish anything.

The first rule is that nothing goes live until three rows say done. Insurance, with rental use and conditions confirmed in writing. The permit, if your local rules require it before listing. And the safety sign-off, signed and dated.

The second rule is that every open issue has one owner and one due date. 'We' is not an owner. If a row says 'we,' it belongs to nobody.

The third rule is about upkeep. Update the packet version whenever a vendor, a code, or a policy changes. A packet from three months ago with the old cleaner's number is worse than useless at 11 p.m.
