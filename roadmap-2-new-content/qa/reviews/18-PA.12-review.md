# Review: PA.12 Your Post-Close Checklist: Insurance, Utilities & Handoff to Operations

## First impression (3-5 sentences as the no-context learner)

The core idea lands fast: write down what the closing people knew so the people running the rental can use it, and don't list until insurance, the permit, and safety are done. After that I kept tripping over codes (PA.9, PA.10, PA.11, 0.3, OP.1 to OP.8, SR.1, PA.5, "Part One lesson 3.3") and acronyms (STR, PITIA, DSCR, NFIP, CPSC, CFPB) that the lesson never explains, and over people and a house (Maya, Chris, Sam, Cedar Ridge) it never introduces. The cash section (PA.12.7, slides 10-11) has correct arithmetic, but it pulls in an emergency fund, a "weak-year reserve", and a "$10,000 floor" from lessons I haven't seen, and the slide version leaves out the key fact that the $32,000 furnishing is already inside the $142,688. The day-by-day plan says every task has "exactly one owner", but half its rows list two owners, which breaks the lesson's own "'We' is not an owner" rule. The exercise is clear if I own a house today. If I don't, I have no idea how to start it.

## Confusing or unclear (quote + why)

- "Every open item gets an owner and a date." (PA.12.1) and the slide 12 title "A dated day 0 to 45 plan gives every task exactly one owner". The table's owners include "Maya, insurer", "Handyman, vendors", "Maya, Chris", "Maya, handyman", and "Both". Five of eleven rows have more than one owner, so the example breaks its own rule ("'We' is not an owner.").
- Slide 4 left column: "Host damage protection and host liability insurance". Right column: "Host damage protection isn't an insurance policy". At a glance this looks like a contradiction. The Markdown makes it clearer that the liability piece is insurance and the damage piece is not, but the slide doesn't show that split.
- Slide 4 title: "AirCover is a backup on some Airbnb claims, so your own policy carries the full load". The Markdown says AirCover "may help on some Airbnb claims". The title drops "may" and states it as fact.
- Slide 8 flow order: Apply → Wait → Add permit number → "Before first stay: Register for lodging tax". Read left to right, it says to register for tax after the listing exists, but the day plan (slide 12) registers on day 2, alongside the permit application.
- PA.12.6: "OP.6 builds these into the furnishing plan. Here, you just need the structural and system items done." Slide 9, though, turns the CPSC furnishing items (extinguisher, anchored TVs, drain covers) into a checklist for right now. The learner can't tell which items are due before photos.
- Slide 11 "What's left" box: "$231,000 - $142,688 - $2,955 - $4,885.50 = $80,471.50 left." The slide never says the $142,688 already includes the $32,000 furnishing. A viewer who just saw $32,000 in the table above will think it's missing from the subtraction, or counted twice.
- Slide 11 box: "Set-asides of $15,342 + $51,000 + $11,750". The three numbers are unlabeled on the slide, and the conclusion (below the $10,000 floor, rescued by later savings) isn't on the slide at all. The slide ends at "$2,379.50, before any saving since the offer", which leaves the viewer asking "so is that OK?"
- Slide 10 and slide 11 labels: "ALL FIGURES ON THIS SLIDE ARE ASSUMPTIONS". PITIA $2,557 and the $32,000 furnishing are called "canonical" elsewhere. Calling everything an assumption makes me doubt the numbers I'm supposed to trust.
- Slide 13 title: "Start the handoff packet with the property, coverage, and access facts". Access is on slide 14, not 13.
- Slide 13 caption: "The full packet in PA.12.8 also has a Source column for every field." A video viewer doesn't know what "PA.12.8" is or where to find it.
- PA.12.10 is one dense paragraph of four full lesson titles plus "bring X" instructions. It's hard to parse as prose. The slide 17 table does this better.
- PA.12.1 closing line: "The version of you who's answering a guest at 11 p.m. in February needs it." Good line, but it repeats on slide 15 ("worse than useless at 11 p.m."). Fine once, weaker twice.
