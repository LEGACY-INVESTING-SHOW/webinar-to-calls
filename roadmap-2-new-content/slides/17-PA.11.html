This is closing day. It's the day you move the most money you'll move in this whole program, so we're going to slow it down.

After this lesson you can check your final closing figures line by line against your accepted offer and your latest loan terms. You'll know which document does what. You'll know how to send your closing funds without handing them to a scammer. And you'll know what to collect when the keys change hands.

You'll leave with three tools: a closing-figures comparison, a closing-day checklist, and an unresolved items log that carries into PA.12.

Cedar Ridge is our example again. Every line-item fee I show you is an assumption built to add up to the canonical totals. None of this is legal, lending, or insurance advice.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.12 / 19
Slide 2 · Money, documents, and control change hands at closing, and they have to move in order

Closing looks like a pile of signatures. Underneath, three things move.

Money moves from you and your lender to the seller, the seller's lender, the title company, and a list of service providers.

Documents move ownership to you and give your lender a claim on the house.

Control moves too. Keys, codes, accounts, records, and the right to occupy.

Most closing problems come from one of those three getting ahead of the others. You wire money before you've checked the figures. You sign before the walkthrough. You get the deed but not the lock codes.

This lesson puts them in order. Check the figures first. Walk the house. Sign what matches. Verify the wire by voice. Then collect control before you leave.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.23 / 19
Slide 3 · A DSCR loan may not come with a Closing Disclosure, so ask which document you'll get

For most consumer mortgages, the lender must give you a Closing Disclosure. The CFPB says you must receive it at least three business days before closing. That window lets you compare final terms and costs with your Loan Estimate. The CFPB also says not to close until you've received and reviewed it.

Investment property adds a wrinkle. The CFPB's Regulation Z commentary treats credit to buy a non-owner-occupied rental as business-purpose credit. That falls outside many of the consumer disclosure rules. So a DSCR lender may give you a settlement statement from the title company instead.

The same commentary says a vacation home the owner occupies more than 14 days in the coming year doesn't get that treatment.

So ask your lender two questions early, in writing. Which document will I get, and when? Then ask for final figures three business days ahead anyway. On your loan it may not be a legal right. You're asking for time to check.

Final walkthrough

Walk the house against your PA.9 tracker before you sign anything

  • Seller repairs: see the septic work and the new well UV system, and get both invoices.
  • Included items: furniture, appliances, dock equipment, anything else in the contract.
  • New damage: water stains, broken windows, storm damage that wasn't there at inspection.
  • Systems: run every tap, flush toilets, test HVAC in both modes, check the water heater.
  • Photos and meters: photograph each room; write down utility meter readings with the time.
If you find a real problem

Don't sign and hope. Call your agent and attorney. Common fixes are a repair escrow, a credit, or a short delay.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.59 / 19
Slide 9 · Walk the house against your PA.9 tracker before you sign anything

The CFPB says to use the final walkthrough to confirm agreed repairs were made and that items the seller agreed to leave are still there. If something's wrong, contact the seller right away.

For an STR, the walkthrough is also your first look at the house as an operator. So bring the PA.9 tracker and walk it row by row.

Look at the seller's repairs, and get the invoices. Check the included items. Look for new damage. Run the systems. Then photograph every room and write down the meter readings with the time. You'll use those for the final bills and in the PA.12 handoff.

If you find a real problem, don't sign and hope. Call your agent and attorney. The CFPB notes you can walk away at closing, but you might lose your deposit and face other consequences under your contract. Let your attorney use that lever. Don't try it alone at the table.

Slide 10 · Know what each closing document does before you initial a single page

You'll sign a stack. Know what the main pieces do, and read the names on each one against your PA.10 worksheet.

The deed transfers ownership from the seller to the title owner you chose. The seller signs it and it's recorded with the county. Ask the title company when you'll get the recorded copy.

The promissory note is the promise to repay. Read the rate, payment, maturity, and any prepayment section.

The mortgage or deed of trust gives the lender a claim on the house. It holds the due-on-sale clause we covered in PA.10.

If there's a personal guaranty, read it with your attorney before closing day, not at the table.

The CFPB says to request your closing documents in advance. If what's at the table differs from what you reviewed, don't sign until you're comfortable.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.711 / 19
Slide 11 · Set up trusted contacts at contract, then verify every wire by voice at a saved number

Closing-day wire fraud targets exactly your situation. A known closing date, a large transfer, and several parties emailing each other. The CFPB warns that scammers send emails posing as your agent or settlement agent with last-minute changes to wiring instructions.

Here's how I'd turn the CFPB's guidance into a protocol.

Set it up early. At contract, identify two trusted people who can confirm payment instructions. Write down their numbers from a source you trust: a card handed to you, or a company website you typed in yourself.

Agree on a code phrase only they know.

Before you send anything, call the settlement agent at the saved number and read back the bank name, account name, account number, and amount. The CFPB says to avoid phone numbers or links in an email.

After the wire, call the saved number again and confirm they received the exact amount.

Wire fraud · red flags

Treat any change to wiring instructions as fraud until a saved number proves otherwise

  • A 'new account' email is the classic move. Stop and call the saved number.
  • Don't email financial information. The CFPB says email is never a secure way to send it.
  • Don't trust caller ID. Scammers can spoof numbers. Hang up and call the saved number.
  • If a wire goes wrong, act in minutes. Call your bank or wire service right away to try to stop or reverse it.
  • File a complaint with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3).
PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.712 / 19
Slide 12 · Treat any change to wiring instructions as fraud until a saved number proves otherwise

The last four steps of the protocol are about what to do when something feels off.

Treat any change as fraud until proven otherwise. A 'new account' email is the classic move. Don't reply to it. Stop and call the saved number.

Don't email financial information. The CFPB says email is never a secure way to send it.

Don't trust caller ID. The CFPB notes scammers can spoof phone numbers. If a call feels wrong, hang up and call the saved number yourself.

And if something does go wrong, minutes matter. The CFPB says to contact your bank or wire service immediately to try to stop or reverse the transfer, and to file a complaint with the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center.

For Cedar Ridge, the wire is $102,988. Ask your bank this week who to call, and what their cutoff times are, so you aren't looking it up in a panic.

Possession

Collect six kinds of control before you leave the closing or the house

  • Physical keys, garage remotes, mailbox keys, gate or boat-launch passes
  • Door and alarm codes, and admin logins for smart locks, cameras, thermostat, Wi-Fi. Seller removes their accounts; you reset every code.
  • Warranties and manuals: roof, HVAC, water heater, appliances, septic parts, well UV system
  • Vendor records: septic and well invoices, water tests, pest and HVAC service, plow and trash services
  • Permits: STR permit status for your new application, septic permit record, past building permits
  • Proof of payment: final figures, wire confirmation, title commitments, insurance declarations
Anything missing

Goes on the unresolved items log, with an owner and a date. That log is the bridge to PA.12.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.813 / 19
Slide 13 · Collect six kinds of control before you leave the closing or the house

Ownership transfers when the deed records. Control transfers when you have every way into the house and every record about it. Your contract says when possession passes.

Collect six kinds of things before you leave the closing or the house that day.

Physical keys and passes. Codes and admin logins for every smart device. Ask the seller to remove their accounts from each device, and then reset every code yourself.

Warranties and manuals, including the well UV system the seller installed. Vendor records, like the septic invoices, water tests, and the names of the seller's plow and trash services. Permit records. And proof of payment.

Anything you don't get goes on the unresolved items log. Every row needs an owner and a date.

PA.11 · Closing Day§ PA.11.1017 / 19
Slide 17 · Five parties confirm the figures, the wire, the documents, and the coverage

Here's who confirms what on closing day.

Your lender confirms which closing document you'll get and when. It explains any change to the rate, points, or fees, and it confirms the exact wire amount.

The title company or closing attorney confirms the settlement figures, the lien payoffs, recording, the title policies, and the wire instructions. Always by voice, at the number you saved.

Your attorney or agent confirms possession terms, what you can do if the walkthrough turns something up, and anything in the documents that differs from your contract.

Your insurer confirms the effective date, the named insured, and the mortgagee clause.

And your bank confirms how its wires work, its cutoff times, and who you call right away if a wire has to be stopped.
