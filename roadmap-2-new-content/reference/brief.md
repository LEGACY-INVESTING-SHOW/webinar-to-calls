## How the pieces fit

- **Roadmap 2.0** teaches whether a leased-property deal is legal, viable, and operationally manageable before someone signs it. Sam's 3.2 lesson is the live regulation-research demonstration; his 4.1–4.5 lessons handle market and property research. The Preston lessons below should teach the decisions and failure points around those demos.
- **STR Concierge** teaches ownership decisions from readiness through acquisition, closing, launch, and eventual refinance or exit. - **Shared resources** should be usable at an actual address or deal. Give each one a source/date field and a clear place to record what still needs confirmation.

## Part One: Airbnb Arbitrage Roadmap 2.0

### 1.3 — Is Arbitrage Still Viable in 2026? A Market Reality Check

**Description:** Give students a decision test, not a blanket yes or no. Use one plausible property to show how demand, supply, rent, startup cash, platform fees, local rules, and downside months determine whether a lease has room for profit.

**Topics to include:**

- The difference between national booking growth and a profitable *specific* unit; why a strong travel market does not rescue a weak lease. Airbnb's [Q2 2026 results](https://news.airbnb.com/airbnb-q2-2026-financial-results) and [AirDNA's 2026 outlook](https://www.airdna.co/outlook-report) are context, not deal-level proof.
- A simple contribution-margin example: booked nights × average nightly revenue, less platform fees, cleaning, utilities, supplies, insurance, management, and rent.
- Break-even occupancy, seasonality, nearby competing listings, and a stress case with lower revenue or higher rent.
- The three gates before signing: address-level permission, written landlord permission, and conservative deal math. Hand off the market-data demonstration to Sam's 4.1–4.5 lessons.

### 3.1 — How STR Regulation Actually Works: Licensing, Zoning & Primary-Residence Rules

**Description:** Explain the layers of permission that apply to an exact address. A city may allow short stays in general while a particular building, zoning district, license category, or lease still blocks the proposed operation.

**Topics to include:**

- City/county land use and zoning; state rules where applicable; building and fire rules; HOA/condo restrictions; lease restrictions.
- Hosted vs. unhosted stays, primary-residence requirements, permit caps, minimum-stay rules, and whether a permit transfers to a new operator.
- Examples of materially different rules: [New York City registration](https://www.nyc.gov/site/specialenforcement/registration-law/tips-for-hosts.page) and [Los Angeles home sharing](https://housing.lacity.gov/articles/home-sharing-ordinance).
- A permission flow: find the official ordinance, check the parcel/address and building, contact the licensing office if unclear, preserve written answers. Sam's 3.2 lesson can demonstrate the live search. [Airbnb's regulatory overview](https://www.airbnb.com/resources/hosting-homes/a/learn-about-hosting-regulations-7) is a starting point, not the final authority for an address.

### 3.3 — Occupancy Taxes & Platform-Verified Registration

**Description:** Show how an operator confirms *which* lodging taxes apply and *who* collects and remits them on each booking channel. Separate permit registration from tax registration, and keep both separate from federal income tax.

**Topics to include:**

- State, county, city, and tourism-district tax layers; taxable stay length, cleaning fees, exemptions, filing frequency, and zero-return requirements where applicable.
- What Airbnb collects in a jurisdiction and what the operator still owes for Airbnb, Vrbo, and direct reservations; reconcile booked revenue to tax reports. [Airbnb lists its collection areas](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2509) but says hosts may still have other obligations.
- How to enter and maintain an official registration or permit number on a listing; what happens when it expires or fails verification. [Airbnb permit instructions](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3953).
- A sample channel-by-channel tax worksheet for one address, with official tax-agency links and the date verified.

### 3.4 — Red Flags: Markets, Cities & Leases to Avoid in 2026

**Description:** Teach a fast rejection screen so students stop spending time on a property that cannot be operated legally or cannot survive an ordinary slow period. Make the examples conditional and sourced rather than publishing a permanent blacklist of cities.

**Topics to include:**

- Hard stops: prohibited unhosted use, primary-residence-only rules that the plan cannot meet, nontransferable or unavailable permits, HOA bans, or a lease without explicit permission.
- Commercial red flags: weak comparable revenue, concentrated demand around one event, new supply, high fixed rent, expensive furnishing, and thin cash reserves.
- Lease red flags: unilateral termination, unclear guest rights, conflicting insurance terms, or reliance on a verbal promise.
- A **reject / investigate / advance** scorecard. The [NYC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/specialenforcement/registration-law/tips-for-hosts.page) and [Los Angeles](https://housing.lacity.gov/articles/home-sharing-ordinance) rules show why a generic “Airbnb-friendly city” label is inadequate; Sam's research lessons provide the live market checks.

### 3.5 — Sublease Legality & Landlord Disclosure Best Practices

**Description:** Explain what written authority an arbitrage operator needs before accepting guests and how to document the actual short-term-rental use. The lesson should help students identify what counsel or an insurer must review, without presenting one contract clause as valid everywhere.

**Topics to include:**

- Distinguish an ordinary residential sublet from repeated short stays under local law and the signed lease.
- Written owner consent, permitted booking channels, guest access, occupancy limits, insurance, repairs, building rules, and the right to use the address in a permit application.
- What to disclose about the operating model, local team, guest screening, damage handling, and neighbors; save the final signed agreement and addendum.
- A worked review of ambiguous vs. explicit permission. [Airbnb tells hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1397) to check leases, landlords, and HOA rules before hosting. Tie the document walkthrough to the updated 5.3 sublease-addendum lesson.

### 5.7 — The 2026 Landlord Pitch: Handling Regulation & Liability Objections

**Description:** Build a candid landlord conversation around verifiable operating controls. The goal is a clear yes or no from the owner and an accurate written agreement, not a script that minimizes the nature of the business.

**Topics to include:**

- A short opening: who operates the property, who stays, how the unit is maintained, and why the arrangement can benefit the owner.
- Objections about legality, parties, noise, damage, neighbors, vacancy, and who responds at night; show the specific evidence or policy that addresses each.
- Distinguish the operator's STR and liability policy from platform protection. [Airbnb says Host damage protection is not insurance](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3733); its [liability insurance has terms and exclusions](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3145).
- Role-play a landlord who asks for the permit, insurance declaration, addendum, and local contact. End with written approval or a documented pass.

### 6.5 — When to Bring In a Local Property Manager

**Description:** Give remote operators a threshold for hiring local help and a way to choose between a cleaner, on-call vendor, co-host, and full manager. Compare the cost with the time, response obligations, and risk it removes.

**Topics to include:**

- Which tasks need a local person: turnovers, supply restocks, maintenance access, emergencies, inspections, and guest issues.
- What the owner/operator keeps: bank and platform ownership, compliance decisions, pricing approval, and performance review.
- Contract and vetting checklist: service area, coverage hours, response targets, fees, markup, authorization limits, backup coverage, insurance, reporting, termination, and whether local law requires a management license.
- Airbnb co-host account permissions and payout controls; [listing owners remain responsible for co-hosts](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1534). Work one example comparing expected management cost with expected net cash flow.

## Part Two: STR Concierge

### Module 0 — Ownership vs. arbitrage

#### 0.1 — Welcome to STR Concierge: What's Different About Owning

**Description:** Orient the student to buying and operating the underlying property. Show the major decisions and cash commitments from defining a buy box through the first 90 days of operations.

**Topics to include:**

- Owner vs. arbitrage operator: title, loan, down payment, maintenance/capital repairs, local approvals, and who makes final decisions.
- The course path: buy box → market/deal review → financing and offer → diligence → closing → furnishing and launch → 30/90-day review.
- The owner's working team: agent, lender, inspector, insurer, title/escrow, CPA, local operators, and the Concierge team; clarify each role.
- The decision gates that can end a deal. Use the current tracker as the course map; the older [Concierge outline](https://docs.google.com/document/d/13h0c_qqbJTJjEhQTYohPnzh0fcqGdmW9WjGye5KCXfo/edit) is background, not an extra syllabus.

#### 0.2 — Why Ownership Wins: Cash Flow + Tax Savings + Equity

**Description:** Explain the *possible* return streams from ownership and the costs and risks attached to each. Frame “wins” as a comparison a buyer must test, not as an outcome every property produces.

**Topics to include:**

- Separate operating cash flow, principal paydown, change in property value, and tax timing; do not add modeled appreciation to spendable monthly income.
- Compare owning with leasing using the same property assumptions: cash required, financing cost, reserves, repairs, downside exposure, and exit options.
- Show that depreciation deductions and losses depend on basis, use, placed-in-service date, passive-activity treatment, and household facts. [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527) and [Publication 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925) are the starting references.
- One conservative case where ownership works, one where the cash investment or debt makes it unattractive.

#### 0.3 — Is Ownership Right for You? A Capital & Readiness Checklist

**Description:** Give a prospective buyer a realistic go / wait / choose another path decision. The checklist should expose the full cash and time requirement before property shopping begins.

**Topics to include:**

- Sources and limits of funds: down payment, closing, furnishing, repairs, permit/insurance costs, launch losses, reserves, and personal emergency savings.
- Credit, lender eligibility, debt capacity, liquidity, and the ability to handle a delayed opening or weak first season. [Fannie Mae's reserve rules](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.1-01/minimum-reserve-requirements) show why the cash requirement extends beyond a down payment.
- Time and risk tolerance: owner decisions, oversight, local help, and personal use plans that may affect taxes.
- A one-page readiness scorecard with explicit next steps: proceed to PA.1, improve financing/liquidity, use the lease path, or wait.

### Phase 1 — Property acquisition

#### PA.1 — Discovery: Defining Your Buy Box

**Description:** Turn the buyer's goals and constraints into a search specification before anyone brings them listings. Produce a buy box that Sam can use for market choice and deal sourcing.

**Topics to include:**

- Goal hierarchy: cash flow, long-term ownership, personal use, tax planning, portfolio growth, and desired involvement.
- Property type, location, budget, minimum reserves, financing path, guest capacity, amenities, and acceptable operating complexity.
- Non-negotiables: legal STR use, acceptable insurance, lender fit, maximum cash to close, and minimum downside-case performance.
- An example of a tight buy box plus a clear “pass” rule; hand off to Sam's PA.2 market-selection and PA.3 deal-sourcing lessons.

#### PA.6 — DSCR Loans 101: How They Qualify the Property, Not You

**Description:** Explain how a rental-property lender uses property income relative to debt payments, and where borrower information still matters. The tracker title is shorthand: a DSCR lender may also examine credit, equity, reserves, property type, and STR legality.

**Topics to include:**

- DSCR numerator and denominator, using the lender's exact definition; sample calculation with a weak and a strong cash-flow case.
- How a lender verifies projected or historical rent, what happens when an STR has no operating history, and why lender assumptions may differ from an investor pro forma.
- Down payment/LTV, rate, points, prepayment terms, reserves, entity or guarantor requirements, and local permit eligibility.
- Questions to send to two lenders and how to compare written offers. [Kiavi's DSCR guide](https://www.kiavi.com/the-complete-guide-to-dscr-rental-property-loans) confirms borrower credit and down payment still matter; its terms are one lender's, not a market standard.

#### PA.7 — DSCR vs. Conventional Financing: Which Path Fits Your Deal

**Description:** Compare two real borrowing routes against the same example property and buyer. The comparison should make total cash, monthly payment, documentation, flexibility, and downside risk visible.

**Topics to include:**

- Conventional borrower qualification and rental-income documentation vs. the terms of a particular DSCR offer.
- A timely nuance: Fannie Mae's [September 2026 STR-income guide](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-3.8-03/rental-income-subject-property-short-term-rental) allows documented STR income for certain one-unit investment-property transactions; conventional financing should not be described as categorically unable to consider STR income.
- Compare Loan Estimates or written lender quotes: rate, APR where available, points, cash to close, payment, reserves, prepayment restrictions, and refinance assumptions. [CFPB comparison guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/compare/compare-loan-estimates/).
- A decision tree for a first purchase, a refinance, or a borrower whose income documentation is complex. The lender makes the actual eligibility call.

#### PA.8 — Offer Strategy & Negotiation for Investment Property

**Description:** Teach students to set a walk-away price before negotiating. Use the underwriting, financing, permit risk, inspection risk, and desired reserves to shape an offer that can still work after closing.

**Topics to include:**

- Offer ceiling from conservative net operating income and cash required, not just asking price or an optimistic nightly rate.
- Earnest money, inspection and financing contingencies, appraisal gap, seller concessions, closing date, and included furniture/equipment.
- What to ask an agent and lender before waiving or shortening a contingency; what changes after a low appraisal or inspection finding.
- A mock offer and counteroffer with a documented *proceed / renegotiate / walk* decision. Sam's PA.4 handles the deeper underwriting package.

#### PA.9 — Due Diligence: STR-Specific Inspections, Appraisal & Title Review

**Description:** Build a diligence sequence that tests both the house and the proposed STR business before a contingency expires. The student should leave knowing which professional verifies each risk and what evidence to save.

**Topics to include:**

- Property condition: roof, HVAC, water, sewer/septic, pests, electrical, access, parking, pool/hot tub, fire safety, and budgeted repairs.
- Business suitability: address-level STR use, permits, HOA/condo rules, insurance quote, guest capacity, and whether existing bookings or a permit can transfer.
- Distinguish an [inspection from an appraisal](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/schedule-a-home-inspection/); review the title commitment, easements, liens, survey, and the difference between [lender's and owner's title coverage](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-owners-title-insurance-en-164/).
- A diligence tracker with owner, deadline, finding, cost, and decision for each item; use Sam's PA.4 assumptions as the baseline to revise.

#### PA.10 — Entity & Tax Structure Setup: LLC, S-Corp or Holding Company

**Description:** Explain the separate choices of who owns title, who operates the STR, and how the activity is taxed. Give buyers a decision worksheet for their attorney, CPA, insurer, and lender rather than a universal entity recommendation.

**Topics to include:**

- Liability separation, ownership partners, financing and lender restrictions, state registration/fees, bank accounts, insurance named insureds, and title transfers.
- Distinguish an LLC's legal form from an S-corporation tax election; [the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p3402) describes how an LLC can elect S-corporation treatment, and the [SBA](https://legacy.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure) treats tax status and legal structure separately.
- Where the operating entity, property-holding entity, management agreement, and beneficial owners would appear in the paperwork.
- Questions for the CPA on depreciation, participation, personal use, and exit consequences; Sam's PA.5 covers the cost-segregation walkthrough.

#### PA.11 — Closing Day: Documents, Wire Fraud Prevention & Possession

**Description:** Walk through the buyer's final verification and money movement, then the handoff of physical and digital control. Use a sample Closing Disclosure and a simple closing-day checklist.

**Topics to include:**

- Compare the Closing Disclosure with the accepted offer and latest loan terms; the [CFPB says it is normally provided at least three business days before closing](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/close/review-documents-before-closing/).
- Review title/escrow instructions, final walkthrough, insurance effective date, deed, note, settlement figures, and possession terms.
- Verify wire instructions by calling a previously saved, independently verified number; do not use a number supplied in a last-minute email. [CFPB wire-fraud guidance](https://files.consumerfinance.gov/f/documents/cfpb_mortgages-scams_one-pager.pdf).
- Collect keys, codes, warranties, vendor records, permits, and proof of payments; record what remains unresolved after closing.

#### PA.12 — Your Post-Close Checklist: Insurance, Utilities & Handoff to Operations

**Description:** Bridge the gap between owning a property and having a safe, insured, ready-to-list rental. Make the handoff explicit so nothing falls between the closing team and the launch team.

**Topics to include:**

- Confirm the STR-specific insurance policy, effective date, deductibles, exclusions, and business interruption terms; do not assume platform protection replaces it. [Airbnb's AirCover explanation](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3733).
- Utilities, internet, smart locks, mail, waste pickup, local contacts, permits, and vendor access; transfer accounts securely.
- Repairs and safety sign-off before photography and publication; [Airbnb's host safety rules](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2904) include alarms, secure access, and known hazards.
- A dated operations handoff packet: property facts, floor plan, contacts, credentials, inventory, maintenance schedule, emergency plan, and open issues.

### Phase 2 — Operations launch

#### OP.6 — Furnishing & Interior Optimization for an Owned Property

**Description:** Design the property for its intended guest and its maintenance budget, not for photographs alone. Show a room-by-room purchase plan with durability, safety, replacement costs, and listing accuracy.

**Topics to include:**

- Guest profile, occupancy and sleeping layout, storage, work space, outdoor use, and accessibility claims that can actually be met.
- Furnishing budget, delivery/assembly schedule, spare inventory, linen cycle, owner-use storage, and a reserve for breakage.
- Safety and insurability: exits, smoke/CO alarms, pool/hot-tub controls, and permitted security devices. [CPSC rental safety tips](https://www.cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/VacationRentalSafetytips002_0.pdf?VersionId=MKcrFCYmuih6xmpoX59wcdmexPwzAXrO) and [Airbnb device rules](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061).
- Photo-ready checklist and a room inventory that Sam's OP.1 listing setup can use. [Airbnb notes](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/39) that accurate amenities and strong media affect guest decisions and visibility.

#### OP.7 — Building Your Ops Team: Cleaner, Co-Host, or Property Manager

**Description:** Show which jobs must be covered for every stay and who is accountable when the owner is unavailable. Compare hiring a set of vendors with hiring a co-host or full manager.

**Topics to include:**

- Turnover cleaner, backup cleaner, handyman, emergency contact, guest messaging, inspections, bookkeeping, and supply replenishment.
- Who has platform access, spending authority, guest data, keys, and after-hours responsibility; define a backup for each role.
- Vetting and written agreements: scope, fees, quality standard, photo proof, response time, insurance, data handling, and termination.
- Use [Airbnb's co-host permission levels](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1534) to demonstrate least-needed access. Avoid paying twice for the same task through a co-host and manager.

#### OP.8 — SOPs: Cleaning, Guest Communication & Emergency Response

**Description:** Create a small operating playbook that another person can follow without guessing. Demonstrate a normal turnover, a guest problem, and a genuine emergency from first alert to resolution.

**Topics to include:**

- Cleaning and inspection checklist with photos, linen and consumables inventory, missed-clean escalation, and owner approval for replacements.
- Message sequence from booking to checkout, response ownership, human review of automated messages, and exception handling.
- Emergency matrix for fire, CO alarm, leak, lockout, injury, outage, and severe weather; local numbers, vendor contacts, stop-work authority, and incident records.
- Guest privacy and safety-device rules: [Airbnb's host safety requirements](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2904) and [indoor-camera prohibition](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3061).

#### OP.9 — Guest Experience & Review Strategy in the AI Ranking Era

**Description:** Teach the operator-controlled parts of guest experience and listing quality. Keep claims about the “algorithm” tied to what Airbnb currently publishes; do not promise a rank boost from undocumented AI or structured-data tricks.

**Topics to include:**

- Accurate photos, amenities and sleeping arrangements, competitive total price, open dates, fast responses, check-in clarity, cleanliness, and issue resolution. [Airbnb's search explanation](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/39) names quality, popularity, price, location, availability, and host behavior.
- A guest journey with checkpoints before arrival, during the stay, and after checkout; collect feedback and fix recurring friction.
- Review requests that are neutral and compliant, plus a process for responding to criticism without disputing every negative review.
- Track response time, complaint reasons, cleanliness misses, cancellations, rating distribution, and repeat-stay signals. [Airbnb's host ground rules](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2895) emphasize accuracy, reservations/refunds, communication, and cleanliness. Sam's OP.1–OP.5 handle channel setup and pricing tools.

#### OP.11 — Scaling Your Portfolio: Refinancing, HELOCs & Property #2

**Description:** Make the second-property decision depend on proven first-property performance and remaining liquidity. Explain how refinancing or home-equity borrowing changes debt service, flexibility, and risk.

**Topics to include:**

- Read actual trailing revenue, expenses, maintenance, occupancy, debt service, and reserves against original underwriting before expanding.
- Refinance math: new payment, closing costs, loan term, equity removed, debt coverage, and what happens in a lower-revenue season.
- HELOC mechanics, variable-rate exposure, draw/repayment periods, and the risk to collateral. [CFPB's HELOC guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-home-equity-line-of-credit-heloc-en-107/).
- Same market vs. new market, team capacity, cross-property systems, and a pass rule for property two. Sam's OP.10 supplies the 30/90-day performance-review baseline.

#### OP.12 — Exit Strategy: Hold, Refinance, Sell, or 1031 Exchange

**Description:** Give an owner a decision framework for changing or ending the investment. Compare after-tax proceeds, time, future debt, operational burden, and opportunity cost across the options.

**Topics to include:**

- Hold vs. refinance vs. sell using an updated valuation, remaining loan balance, capital repairs, cash flow, and sale costs.
- Depreciation and possible gain-recognition consequences; consult the CPA before relying on a modeled net-sale figure. [IRS Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527).
- The basic 1031 path for qualifying business or investment real property, including the usual 45-day replacement-property identification and 180-day completion deadlines; [IRS guidance](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/like-kind-exchanges-real-estate-tax-tips) and [Form 8824 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i8824).
- A decision worksheet that records current value, debt, net proceeds, tax estimate, next-property assumptions, and who must approve an exchange. Avoid implying a personally used vacation home automatically qualifies.

## Part Three: shared resources

### SR.1 — Regulatory compliance checklist, by state and city

**Description:** Build an address-level research and evidence template used with Roadmap Module 3 and Sam's PA.2 ownership-market lesson. It should lead to a documented *permitted / prohibited / unresolved* decision, not a static claim that a whole state is “legal.”

**Fields and sections to include:**

- Full address, parcel and jurisdiction; property type; planned hosted/unhosted use and stay length.
- State, county, city, zoning, HOA/condo, landlord/lease, and insurance checks, each with **official source URL, date checked, result, and reviewer**.
- Permit/registration category, prerequisites, caps, primary-residence test, renewal, transferability, required local contact, safety rules, and advertising/permit-number requirements.
- Occupancy-tax registrations and channel remittance; written questions sent to the city; unresolved items and the professional who will clear them.
- Decision and recheck date. Use examples from [NYC](https://www.nyc.gov/site/specialenforcement/registration-law/tips-for-hosts.page) and [Los Angeles](https://housing.lacity.gov/articles/home-sharing-ordinance) to test the template.

### SR.3 — Cost segregation & tax-impact estimator

**Description:** Build a clearly labeled scenario tool to support Sam's PA.5 lesson and a CPA conversation. It should estimate depreciation timing and a possible tax effect while keeping eligibility, classification, participation, and actual return treatment as explicit inputs to confirm.

**Fields and sections to include:**

- Acquisition and placed-in-service dates, purchase price, land allocation, improvements, furnishing costs, study fee, and asset categories/assigned lives from a cost-segregation study.
- Eligible bonus-depreciation assumption by tax year and acquisition date; the [IRS's 2026 guidance](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-issue-guidance-on-the-additional-first-year-depreciation-deduction-amended-as-part-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill) describes 100% first-year bonus depreciation for *eligible property* acquired after January 19, 2025. Do not apply it to land or the entire building.
- Rental vs. personal-use days; average guest stay and material-participation inputs; loss limits and household tax-rate assumptions from the CPA. [IRS Publication 925](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p925) and [Publication 527](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p527).
- Output separately: ordinary depreciation, accelerated depreciation, estimated current-year deduction, **illustrative** tax effect, future basis/recapture considerations, and the assumptions still unverified.
- A worked low/base/high example and a source tab. The [IRS cost-segregation guide](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p5653.pdf) can inform the evidence requested from a study provider.

### SR.5 — Tool comparison guide: PMS, dynamic pricing, AI guest messaging

**Description:** Create a neutral selection worksheet that helps an operator choose a small connected stack for their property count and channels. Sam's 9.12/9.13 and OP.4/OP.5 lessons are the product walkthroughs; this guide should compare decisions and handoffs, not duplicate the demos.

**Fields and sections to include:**

- Property count, channels, direct-booking need, team size, budget, and who will own guest payments and tax remittance.
- PMS/channel-manager criteria: native integrations, calendar and rate sync, single source of truth, permissions, owner statements, reporting, payment handling, data export, and migration effort. [Airbnb supports PMS connections](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2683); [Vrbo explains](https://help.vrbo.com/articles/About-Vrbo-integration) that integration can change where listings are edited and who handles payments/taxes.
- Dynamic-pricing criteria: floor/ceiling controls, minimum stays, event overrides, booking pace, comp-set quality, and how an operator reviews automated changes.
- AI messaging criteria: approved templates, information sources, confidence/fallback rules, human escalation, emergency handling, guest-data access, and audit trail.
- A weighted comparison table, current pricing/date checked, a two-week trial checklist, integration test bookings, and a clear recommendation field. Recheck vendor terms before publishing; avoid declaring one tool best for every operator.

## Production checks before scripting

1. **Pick real examples.** One arbitrage unit and one owned-property deal should recur through the lessons so the decisions build on each other. Label assumptions and use permissioned, anonymized client materials if available.
2. **Get local and professional review where the lesson relies on it.** Have an STR attorney review lease/regulation examples for their jurisdiction; have the lender review the loan comparison; have the CPA review entity, participation, depreciation, and 1031 examples.
3. **Confirm the current product.** Update platform screens, permit workflows, lender terms, and software comparisons immediately before recording. Official [Airbnb search factors](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/39) are a better basis than an unsupported claim about a new “AI ranking” rule.
4. **Keep each handoff clean.** Link to Sam's relevant demo at the end of the Preston lesson, and have Sam's demo return the student to the next decision or worksheet. That preserves the 27 new Preston deliverables without re-recording his assignments.
